The Network Interface Card (NIC)

A 10-gigabit Ethernet network card for a trading system. Market data enters from the wire on the left, is unwrapped layer by layer — Ethernet, then IPv4, then UDP — and clean messages reach the application on the right; replies flow back along the mirror path. Boxes inside boxes are real containment; numbered circles follow one received packet. Click anything to see what it does — and scroll to the bottom for what one packet is made of, byte by byte.

Generated, not drawn: every box and arrow is parsed from the design's own 119-edge connection table and compile-checked interface records, re-asserted at build time — this page cannot drift from the design.

anatomy of one packet — every byte on the wire

The smallest legal market-data packet occupies 84 bytes of wire time (a byte is 8 bits; where network standards say “octet” they just mean a byte). Read left to right: this is the exact order the bytes arrive, and every layer of wrapping is stripped by a module in the diagram above. Only the 18 payload bytes — about a fifth of the wire time — survive to the application. The brackets show the wrapping: each layer's envelope contains everything beneath it. The rhythm of arrival: at 10 Gb/s — ten billion bits, i.e. 1.25 billion bytes, every second — the wire delivers one byte every 0.8 ns, so back-to-back minimum-size packets land every 67.2 ns. The strip below replays that rhythm slowed twenty-million-fold — each passing packet ≈ 1.3 seconds here, 67.2 nanoseconds on the wire:

Phase 2 makes the payload real. A recorded NASDAQ trading day is replayed through this exact wrapping — MoldUDP64 sequences carrying ITCH 5.0 add / execute / cancel messages — and the receive path above unwraps every one of them at line rate, feeding the parser and order book that attach at APP.

block diagram

20 modules · 64 bits per beat · 156.25 MHz

The receive path enters at the WIRE anchor and flows left to right through the MAC, the Ethernet layer, IPv4 and UDP to the application. Transmit is its mirror, right to left along the bottom. The ARP resolver sits in the middle of Ip_complete_64, answering queries the transmit path raises.

Toggle the config/status planes: the 12 config fields and the chip's other control plumbing (CRC state, the ARP cache) travel 31 control-class wires; the 21 status strobes each ride one wire into Nic_top and leave as a single bundled record — 22 status wires in the table.
