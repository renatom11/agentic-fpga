PROCESS-MEMOIR — the companion volume

What this file is. It is the history half of docs/PROCESS.md. That file states the law this organization works under; this one holds everything the law used to say and no longer does — every corrected claim preserved beside what refuted it, every edition's item-by-item ledger of what it changed and what it refused, the debts each edition owed and to whom, and the map from that document's function names onto this program's own seats, paths and tokens.

What this file is not. It states no rule. Nothing here binds any seat. Every claim preserved in Part I is preserved because it was found false, and every one of them is marked as such at the point a reader meets it.

And the self-description this volume carried for one edition, corrected here because it was false of its own contents. [B.12·17] The line above used to continue "and nothing here is current." That is true of Part I, which is the superseded record, and it is not true of Annex B, which is a live ledger: dated conditions still open, an owed-confirmation matrix, an amendment queue with owners, and — the case that made the falsity load-bearing — the two OPEN — CONDITION rows a review council set as gates on any certification of the export unit at the level of the whole. Those are present-tense governance state. A reader of the core alone therefore learned that one of those instruments was owed and never that a verdict blocks the unit's central claim on it. The conditions are now stated in the core, at §6.0, where the claim they gate is made; they remain here with their history, and this volume's honest self-description is the one that distinguishes its two halves:

Part I is history and binds nothing. Annex B is this program's own live ledger of debts, conditions and owed acts — it binds no adopter, and it is current for this program. Where a row of Annex B and the core disagree

about what is owed, the core governs and the row is stale.

The general form, and it is the split's own rule applied to the split's own product: a volume separated by tense must actually be separated by tense. Annex B moved here by noun-locality — it is made of program nouns, so §1–§6 could not carry it — and noun-locality is a different axis from tense. The sixth edition's split rule says in terms that it divides by tense and not by audience, and its second clause did not. Naming the exception is what keeps the rule usable; leaving it unnamed is what made the first page false.

The core governs. Where this volume and docs/PROCESS.md disagree about what any rule is, docs/PROCESS.md is right and this file is stale. There is no case in which a sentence here overrides a sentence there. If you find a disagreement, it is a defect of this volume, and the repair is always to this volume.

Why the two files exist. For five editions the law and its history were one document, interleaved at paragraph granularity: a reader wanting what is the rule now had to separate the running line from preserved false claims, edition genealogy and annex archaeology in nearly every section. Three consecutive councils named it; the document's own graders scored readability B− and conciseness C+; the separation was recorded in the document itself as NOT ATTEMPTED, for the second consecutive edition. The sixth edition performed it. The governing decision record is docs/adr/ADR-0022, which carries the alternatives considered, the failure modes the split creates, and what it explicitly does not decide.

Who this volume is for. Three readers, and it is worth naming them because the core is not written for any of them. An auditor checking whether a correction was actually made rather than merely announced. A reviewer of the split itself, verifying that nothing was lost — Part 0 is written for exactly that act. And a later edition's author, who needs to know which repairs have already been tried and why the refused ones were refused, so that a rejected option does not return as a fresh proposal and get rejected again at full cost.

  2. Every anchor resolves: each Part I heading names a section of docs/PROCESS.md, and that section exists there.

  4. git diff d96a5b1 -- docs/PROCESS.md, and every removed passage is found in one of the two volumes or is a named repair in B.11 or B.12 — with the fifth edition itself as the standard for the search, never this volume's manifest. Three auditors ran the printed self-checks and all three passed; the loss surfaced the moment somebody diffed against the committed fifth edition instead. It is a reviewer's act and no script performs it — the governance check that would is commissioned, with its owner, at the core's §6.0.

The rule that generalizes all of this, adopted by the seventh edition as the core's own drafting law and repeated here because this volume is where its counterexample lives: [B.12·6]

No printed self-check may use, as its standard, a list the same edition wrote.

The test to apply before printing any self-check: name the artifact this check would have to disagree with, and ask who wrote that artifact. If the answer is this edition, the check cannot fail and is decoration. The document had already convicted itself of this shape three times — a count stated over its own list, a column defined by the practice that published it, a census whose population its own author fixed — and then rebuilt it into the one structural act of an edition. A defect class you can name and still commit is a class you have described rather than understood.
