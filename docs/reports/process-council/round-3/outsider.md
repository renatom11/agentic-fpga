THE OUTSIDER — opinion on docs/PROCESS.md (third edition). Basis: the document read whole, lines 1-4142, no other file opened. Where I cite Bob or Bill on the repository, I say so; everything else is checkable from the text alone.

1. THE DOCUMENT NEVER DECIDES WHO IT IS FOR, AND EVERY STRUCTURAL DEFECT FLOWS FROM THAT. (C3.1, C3.6, C4.5; endorsing Charlie F3/F4/F7 and Bill F5/F6, with additions.) "Read this first" and "How to read this" never name a reader. What the opening 240 lines actually serve is the document's councils — people who hold the repository and the prior editions. Three symptoms only an outsider feels: (a) The ordering inversion. The reader is taught the stamp grammar for a hundred lines, learns to lean on [MC] and C-nn, and only at line 134-148 is told the boundary: "The stamps do not cover this document. They cover the document it used to be." That sentence is the single most important sentence for a cold reader and it arrives after the apparatus it disclaims has been installed in the reader's head. Honest ordering is boundary first, grammar second. (b) The dialect is reactive by construction. It contains exactly the nine terms that already burned a cold reader ("Five came from the first cold reading and four from the second") — which guarantees that the terms that never burned an insider stay undefined forever. That is why "seat", "agent", "role", "spawn" and "posture" — the load-bearing vocabulary, used thousands of times, genuinely ambiguous at §1.2's shared-worker-journal reconciliation — have no entry while "limb" and "cure" do. The document even states the mechanism ("the seat that can see the new opacity is the one that was not in the room") and then ships with its own cold-reader row (B.1, last row) marked "edition 3: owed". A document whose headline claim is stranger-sufficiency shipped without running its stranger check; Charlie's F3-F7 are, verbatim, what that owed row would have returned. (c) §1.0 is an excellent one-paragraph version of the whole organization, and it is buried at line 279 behind the apparatus preface. The preface justifies itself by §5.8 ("prohibitions belong at the top") — but §5.8 licenses one imperative sentence, not 220 lines of stamp semantics. The document applies its own rule wider than the rule — the exact "guard wider than the rule" defect §3.5 teaches readers to catch. Cheapest highest-value repair in the whole review: put §1.0 (or one sentence pointing at it) at line 19.

2. THE CORRECTION APPARATUS DOES NOT CONVERGE, AND THIS EDITION IS ITS OWN PROOF — WHICH ELEVATES THE STRUCTURAL FIX ABOVE ANY LIST OF LINE REPAIRS. (C9, C3.5; Sal Rules 2, 5, 7; endorsing Bill F6 and Bob F4/F5, with cold-visible evidence.) §2.7 says it in terms: "per-claim editing of a document this size does not converge — two consecutive editions purged the class and both shipped fresh instances of it." The third edition then demonstrates it in text a stranger can check without the repo: §1.5 puts "Four mechanisms in it are domain-free and load-bearing:" over a list of five bullets — the precise header-count defect the document narrates twice as a paid-for lesson (§1.4's margin: "the number in a header is worth checking against the list under it"); §2.3 contradicts itself within ten lines, granting the packet directory to "three leads and every worker role" and then counting the shared classes as "shared by two to four seats", inside the very passage that commands "Compute it"; §3's packet table gives the Finding row an author-dependent relay class ("Verbatim when the auditor's") and the next paragraph rules "Relay class is a property of the packet type, not of the round"; "How to read this" still says "Five ... are defined in The dialect" over a nine-term dialect. None of these needs the repository — a cold reader trips on all four. The conclusion is not "fix the four". It is that the document's architecture — a single file where each edition adds a stamp/margin/annex layer it may not remove — is structurally divergent: mass roughly tripled, normative payload roughly constant, and the fork contract (§6.1) concedes the point by instructing an adopter to strip stamps, margins, [CORRECTED] narratives and Annex B, i.e. most of the mass. A document whose own fork operation deletes most of itself has told you its mass is in the wrong place, and it assigns the 135-act stripping job to the reader with the least context. Recommendation shape (defect and acceptance test, not wording): separate the normative core (rules, failure classes, facsimiles, adoption order) from the program memoir (stamps, margins, Annex B, correction archaeology) as two renderings of one source, per Sal Rule 4's partition and Rule 5's rule-first pattern. Acceptance tests: the fork contract becomes executable as a file deletion rather than 135 distributed editorial acts; and B.1's cold-reader row runs green on the core before an edition ships, not "owed" after. Do not mistake the volume of Bob's MINORs for the finding — per Rule 7 the finding is the single structural one they jointly prove.

3. THE INVENTED-LAW HAZARD IS THE ONE A COLD READER CANNOT DEFEND AGAINST — BOB'S F1 IS THE ACCURACY FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST FROM MY SEAT. (C8.1, C2; Bob F1.) I read §3's minting blockquote ("A new packet type may be minted only by the amendment procedure... No seat mints a type by using one"), presented as "The rule:" and "the invariant worth exporting", and I took it as this organization's standing law — I had no way to do otherwise. Bob reports it exists in no constitution, no ADR, no charter, carries no stamp and no "(no posture row)" marker, and that practice is its inverse; the text itself half-confesses two paragraphs later ("None of these was minted by the route above"), and "Six types are defined" cold-reads as a count of constitutional law. The document's own §2.7 states the norm this violates: "A document that discovers a rule its own constitution should carry proposes it; it does not enact it by writing it down." This is the exact failure the posture apparatus was built to prevent, committed in the one place the apparatus was not applied — and it is worst for precisely the blank-AI reader the headline claim invokes, who will replicate the invented rule as inherited law. I rank it beside Charlie's F1 as a blocker: a stranger's inability to distinguish the document's proposals from the organization's law defeats the document's purpose even where every other sentence is true.
