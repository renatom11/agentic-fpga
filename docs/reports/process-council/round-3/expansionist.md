THE EXPANSIONIST — what the council is pricing wrong

My colleagues have audited a document. What sits in this repository is a stack of at least five separable products, and the reports name only one of them. Every finding Charlie, Bob and Bill filed is real; almost every one of them, read from my seat, is a cheap unlock on an asset the council is valuing at a fraction of its worth. Sal's own Rule 2 says C5 is the master test. I say the master test is aimed at the smallest of the five things this program has built.

1. The posture apparatus is a second product, and it is bigger than the process it describes. The stamp grammar ([MC]/[RE]/[P1]/[PLANNED]/[CORRECTED]/[UNANCHORED], "Read this first"), the posture list as a committed measurement with a path (docs/reports/audit/PROCESS-claims-posture.md), the edition anchor, the boundary-decay rule ("a boundary claim about a document's own coverage decays faster than any claim inside it"), and the fork contract's per-mark table (§6.1) together constitute a general technology for keeping claims-bearing prose honest against machinery. That is documentation rot — the largest unsolved problem in every engineering organization on earth — with a worked, executed solution: 128 claims re-run, 15 false found, all cured with provenance. Charlie's F5 prices the apparatus as reader-cost with expired assurance; Bob's stamp-honesty check is the rebuttal the council should headline — ~30 stamps traced, essentially all honest, the [PLANNED]s candid, the [P1]s locating. The apparatus WORKS; what it lacks is the one instrument that generalizes it: the doc–shell drift check (§6.0, B.2 item 9), which is not a bug-fix but the prototype of prose-claims-vs-machinery CI. Fund that instrument and Charlie's "no visible seam" complaint dissolves mechanically — and you hold a standalone kit artifact (a 14th row for §6.0's table: the posture apparatus itself, domain-free) applicable to security policies, compliance narratives, runbooks, model cards. Acceptance test already in the record's idiom: a stranger applies the apparatus to one foreign document and the audit returns findings.

2. §3.9 is the most valuable ~330 lines in the repository, and it is not about FPGAs. Sealed predictions with message-text discrimination, negative controls, the sealed/seeded two-column discipline with named grounds, equivalence only on a proof quantified over the specification's input space, rehabilitation with expiry conditions, "the gate asks present-tense" — this is a complete, incident-hardened theory of how to verify that a grader grades. The AI industry's eval crisis is exactly this document's opening sentence of §3.9: a green suite is consistent with a suite that checks nothing. Pre-registered seals = preregistration for evals; equivalent defects = unfalsifiable eval items; the survivor-form expiry = eval drift. Charlie's F1 (seal author/freeze/scorer indeterminate) is the round's best finding precisely because it is the only blocker standing between §3.9 and extraction as a domain-general standard for adversarial verification of test suites and eval harnesses — his own acceptance test says the fix is one passage plus one table row. Close F1 and publish §3.9 separately. That is not scope creep; it is the LH2-g discipline (§3.10) applied to the document's own crown jewel.

3. The federation machinery is a network product hiding in a correction. §6.0's four-step transit (outbox packet → inbox pull request → shell federation law → no privileged lane for the origin), HT-01, and docs/federation/outbox describe a working inter-organization pipeline for exchanging crystallized failure-knowledge. Nobody's report treats this as more than an accuracy repair (B.8 item 5). Run it forward: N adopters, each running §3.10 harvests, each delivering LH2-g lessons through the same inbox — the failure museum (§5) stops being one program's tuition and becomes a compounding, shared immune system. Every adopter makes every other adopter's process description truer. Bill's F1 (unpinned shell, thirty-one commits ahead) is the defect that, inverted, is the enabling discipline: the fix is not merely a SHA in a line, it is shell RELEASES — tagged, changelogged, with PROCESS.md editions pinning facsimiles to a release. That converts Bill's CRITICAL-adjacent finding into the ordinary release hygiene of an upstream with an ecosystem, which is what the shell should become.

4. Grade the derivative, not the snapshot. Sal's verdict shape (§4) issues a static grade. The record supports a stronger, falsifiable claim: edition 1 stalled three reviewers at the same commit-handoff gap; edition 2's cold run returned eighteen halts, three stops; edition 3 carries a repair per halt, with the halt cited at each site (§6.2). The cold-adoption run costs one seat, one day — the document proves this — and B.1's cold-reader row is already standing per-edition. Make the cold RUN standing too, and "replication-grade" stops being a council opinion and becomes a measured trajectory: halt count per edition, trending to zero across independent runs. No document class I know of has ever had an empirical convergence metric for its own executability. This one is one Routine away from having it. The council should write its conditions as that cadence, not as a one-time bar.

5. The record itself is a benchmark corpus. A hundred-plus packets, 21 ADRs, journal volume chains to .v11, one sign-off with per-class anchor cells, 86 never-merged mutation refs, a G0 retro-audit that blocked on a critical finding — this is the only complete, auditable ground-truth corpus of a multi-agent engineering organization that actually shipped verified work. Sal's C5 walkthrough legs (rehydrate from BOARD.md; run one work cycle; score a campaign; survive a death) are, verbatim, eval scenarios for agentic-organization competence. Packaged, repo-plus-document is an evaluation environment nobody else can offer, and every future model generation re-runs the C5 test for free — each run also feeding point 4's metric.

6. Undervalued single sentences the council should protect from any tightening pass: "the reviewing tier is at least the producing tier" (§1.2) — the first crisp statement of review economics for mixed-capability agent orgs, and it will be quoted for years; "a residue routed to a seat is only as live as that seat's spare capacity" (§1.1) — a general law of compensating controls; §4.7's minimal human surface plus canary power — an executed instance of exactly the minimal-human-governance interface that oversight frameworks for autonomous systems are currently trying to invent from nothing. These are LH2-g assets; Bob's F8 (incidents exported without their incident, against LH1) is the right complaint in the wrong direction — the fix is to finish attaching the scars, not to trim the claims.

7. The cheap unlocks, priced. Nearly every MAJOR filed has its repair already specified inside the document: the mechanical subsection index (B.8 refusal 4 names "derived mechanically at read time" — a script beside five existing enforcement scripts; closes Charlie F4/Bill F5 at near-zero cost); the two-line completion of §1.3's nine-field charter list (Bill F4); the four never-stated literals (Charlie F2 — an afternoon); the glossary mapping function-names to reference-implementation names (Bill F7 — one page, and it doubles as the federation identifier-map the transit step already performs). The council should notice the pattern: this document's defect ledger is unusually monetizable because the org's own conventions make every defect carry its acceptance test.

Verdict posture from my seat: grade the export unit, not the half — Charlie's F9 poses the choice and the expansionist answer is to enlarge the denominator, not shrink the claim. Then treat the blocking list as an investment schedule: F1-Charlie and shell-pinning first (they gate the two extraction products), drift check second (it generalizes the apparatus), standing cold runs third (they convert the headline claim into a measured trajectory). If this works even slightly better than expected, the output is not a replicable FPGA org. It is a self-verifying documentation standard, a portable eval-integrity discipline, a federated lesson-exchange network with a maintained upstream, a benchmark corpus, and a governance interface — five products, one repository, and the council is about to file it under "process documentation."
