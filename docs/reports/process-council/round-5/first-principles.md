OPINION OF THE FIRST PRINCIPLES THINKER

**The document has already written the correct verdict on itself, in one sentence, and then declined to act on it.** §6.0, in the export-mechanics passage: "The document believed it was the primary artifact with an executable appendix. The record shows it is the rationale half of a shell-primary unit, and it fails exactly where it impersonates the machine half." That sentence is the whole review. Everything else — Sal's ten criteria, Charlie's 22 findings, Bob's re-executions, Bill's stall map — is corollary. The council's real question is not whether the sixth edition is a good process reference; it is why a program that has correctly diagnosed itself as the rationale half keeps spending its rounds — six editions in ten days — perfecting the rationale half. The owner wants a framework that survives re-adoption after failed exports. The failure data says the binding constraint is no longer description. Another edition of this document does not move it.

**Strip the assumptions and the problem decomposes into exactly three cargoes, and the document is the right vehicle for only one of them.** What must transfer to a new program is: (1) the machine — scripts, formats, thresholds, rule numbers; (2) the record — proof the machine ran a real program; (3) the judgment — why each rule exists, which separations are load-bearing, what breaks first under schedule pressure. The two adoption runs measured this decomposition directly: run one stalled on missing interfaces (fixed by facsimiles), run two still invented ~850 lines of enforcement *logic* (fixable only by the shell, never by prose — §6.0's own bet, "half-vindicated and half-untested"), and the residual stalls in run two's log are judgment gaps: HALT-09's undetermined seat set (Bill F2, unrepaired), Step 2's formless review artifact (Bill F7). Meanwhile the one part "every reviewer of every edition has certified as transferring whole" (the document's own words, front matter) is §5, the failure museum — the judgment cargo, and a small fraction of 4,920 lines. First principles: the machine transfers as a machine, the record transfers as a repository, and prose is the right vehicle only for judgment. This document is ~4,900 lines of vehicle carrying mostly the wrong two cargoes — facsimiles of what the shell should own, and a self-measurement apparatus that measures the prose.

**The origin program ran the decisive experiment on prose-vs-machine law and the document refuses to learn from its result.** Verified myself: `agents/PROTOCOL.md` contains zero occurrences of R10 or R11, while `scripts/agent_commit.sh` refuses by those names — the constitution never absorbed ADR-0017's written diffs (Bob F3). ADR-0020's restatement debts stand unpaid in four charters and the live gate checklist's "verbatim" quotes (Bob F2). ADR-0022, the record governing this very edition's warranty and split, is stamped PROPOSED / NOT IN FORCE with all three signatures owed while both its decisions operate (Bob F5; verified). And the split's own printed falsifier 4 returns false when actually run — ~14 fifth-edition margins in neither volume (Bob F1). Read these as one datum, not four findings: in this organization, *every* text whose truth is maintained by re-reading decayed within days, and *everything* enforced by a runnable check stayed true. The document itself states the law it is breaking (§6.1: false claims "cluster where it describes artifacts, not where it describes scripts... because scripts refuse and artifacts merely testify") — and it is 4,920 lines of testimony describing refusals. Its stamped evidentiary spine froze at 1,445 lines while the file grew to 4,920; its truth-maintenance instrument is, literally, this council — a five-seat manual audit per edition. An artifact whose maintenance cost is a council cannot be the primary unit of a re-adoptable framework. A repository whose maintenance is CI can. C10 is not a criterion this document scores poorly on; it is the physics that rules out this document class as the primary artifact.

**The verification effort is aimed at the half adopters will not execute — a full inversion of the owner's goal.** Bill's live probe confirmed the shell is real, public, pinned — and §6.0 concedes the with-shell path is untested: "the only path this unit has evidence for is the one no adopter with network access will take." So: stamps, falsifiers, posture lists, two halt logs, four councils, three auditor reports — all of it audits the fallback path and the rationale volume. BOOTSTRAP.md, the document adopters are told takes precedence at minute one, has never been audited by anyone in this record; the doc–shell drift check "does not exist" (disclosed on page one, line 19); the shell's constitution template exports the un-reconciled R1–R9 text (Bob F3's kit consequence). The terminal metric for the owner — blank AI + empty repo + reachable shell → working organization — has zero measurements, and none of the five of us was tasked to produce one. Sal's probe 5.2 is called "the decisive probe" in his own framework and no seat ran it. My strongest single recommendation to the council: the condition on any verdict should be running that probe and auditing BOOTSTRAP.md against §6.2's invariants — not a seventh edition. Bill F1's proposed repair (convert §6.2 into order-independent invariant checkpoints: founding range declared; machinery before adversarial review; CI green before protection; live-fire bounce; gate closes before WO-0001) is the only prose change that serves the real path.

**Where the reports converge, promote; where they polish, decline.** Per Sal's Part IV rule 9, the strongest convergence in the round is Charlie F3 + Bill F6, reached independently from opposite seats: day-zero work (Step 0's trace) routes through Annex C in the volume that declares "nothing here is current," and the fork contract deletes that map as the first act of the step that needs it — plus ~110 in-text pointers into a ledger a fork deletes. Conversely, I would decline to condition anything on the fact-once violations, the A.6b ordering, the preamble toll, or the hybrid stamp forms (Charlie F9–F17): they are blemishes on an artifact whose category is wrong, and polishing them ratifies the category.

**Answer to the question I was asked: is a process reference the right artifact? No — not as the primary one, and the document knows it.** The right export unit for the owner's goal is: (1) the shell as the primary, self-verifying artifact — BOOTSTRAP.md tested by an uncommissioned with-shell run, the doc–shell drift check built as CI *in the shell* so "machine-checked" is literal rather than stamped, the constitution template reconciled with the scripts it describes, per-seat charter templates carrying the load-bearing mechanisms §1.5 enumerates for the auditor alone (Bill F4) and the minimum-legal-seat-set derivation §1.2 owes (Bill F2); (2) a short judgment volume — the failure museum, the five separations and legal fusions, the invariant checkpoints, §6.3's thesis — which is the only cargo prose uniquely carries and, tellingly, the only part of this document everyone certifies; (3) the repository itself as the record, which it already is. The stamp-and-falsifier method is the genuinely original invention here and should survive — retargeted at the shell, where its claims can refuse instead of testify. The sixth edition performed the record/law split; the seventh act should be the machine/judgment split, and it is not an edition of this file. What I am voting against is not this document's honesty — it is the best-calibrated artifact of its class I have seen, exactly as Bob says — but its center of gravity: a program whose export keeps failing does not need a truer description of itself; it needs its description demoted to commentary on a bootstrap that has been watched to work. The document wrote that sentence itself at §6.0. The council's verdict should simply make it binding.
