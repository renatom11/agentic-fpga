CHARLIE COLD-READER AUDIT — /home/user/agentic-fpga/docs/PROCESS.md
Reviewed as claimed: "a complete, project-agnostic description of how an agentic engineering organization works," read with zero project context. Findings ranked by severity: what would most mislead or lose a cold reader first, and which claimed rules cannot be implemented from the text alone.

=== CRITICAL — mechanisms that cannot be implemented from the text, or internal contradictions that mislead ===

C1. The commit mechanics — the document's central machinery — are never actually described, and the text contradicts itself about who commits.
§1.1 is absolute: the orchestrator is "the **sole committer** — no other agent runs a commit or a push." Yet §2.3 says "Each agent may stage changes only inside its declared scope," and the §2.6 path-isolation rule reads "Every staged non-journal path must be in the committing agent's scope" — plural committing agents. §2.1 says "Every commit carries exactly one agent's work plus that agent's own explanation of it." Nowhere does the document explain how a worker's staged changes plus its journal append become a commit executed by the orchestrator, nor how the commit script determines WHICH agent staged what (trailer identity? handoff protocol? one agent per working tree at a time?). Git has one index per worktree; "each agent stages only in its scope" is not a native concept, and the enforcement script the whole of §2 rests on cannot be built without resolving this. This is the single largest gap: the load-bearing mechanism of the entire constitution is asserted, not described.

C2. The substrate model is missing, and §2.3's disjoint-scopes claim contradicts §3.2's shared packets.
§2.3 states "because scopes are disjoint, one-agent-per-commit is automatic for the scoped seats." But §3.2 says a work order "carries a return log that the participants append to" — at least two seats writing one file — and §1.5 confirms shared writability by exception: "Because the auditor cannot write into shared packets…" (implying other seats can). Either the scope table overlaps on packet paths, in which case scopes are not disjoint and the "automatic" one-agent-per-commit claim is false, or all packet traffic is orchestrator-transcribed, which the document states only for the auditor. A cold implementer cannot write the scope table. Relatedly, §4.1–4.2 presuppose an execution environment (shared working tree? per-agent clones? "An undeclared dirty path is an abort"; "if the current commit moves while an agent is working") that is never once described. I cannot tell whether concurrent agents share a checkout, and both disciplines change meaning depending on the answer.

C3. The seeder seat does not exist in the organization.
§1.2 presents a fixed roster (sponsor, orchestrator, three leads, workers, auditor) and §1.2's failure class explicitly warns against roles that are "understood" rather than written. Then §3.9 introduces "an independent seat authors **defect manifests**" and rules for it ("The seeder never operates the repository"; "The seeder reads from an allowlist"). Which seat is this? It cannot be the verification lead (its suite is the subject under test), cannot be the auditor as chartered (§1.5: "It can write only its own reports… not even the shared task packets" — manifests are surely not audit reports), cannot be the orchestrator (it is the operator, and "the operator never authors the evidence"). The document's most elaborate mechanism is run by a seat the document never seats. Unimplementable as written.

C4. Sealed predictions: the withholding mechanism is absent, the file is self-referential, and nobody is named to unseal or adjudicate.
§3.3's rule is "a seal is a file in the commit that claims it, or it is not a seal," protecting against "a claim that a result already exists and is being withheld from the reader." But a committed file in a repository with unrestricted read access (§2.3: "Read access is unrestricted except where a charter says otherwise") is withheld from no one. Is the seal committed in plaintext and protected only by prompt/packet blinding (the §1.4(c) posture), or committed as a hash with content revealed later? The document never says, and the two designs are materially different. Second: the sealed file "carries… the commit at which it was frozen, and its own hash at that commit" — a file cannot contain its own hash; the reader must invent a body-only hash or an external ledger, i.e., invent the mechanism. Third: "the single line that is flipped at unsealing" and "a wrong prediction is not amended, it is adjudicated" name no seat with unsealing or adjudication authority, and §3.9's scoring bullets likewise say "the reviewer reads the ground and may refuse it" without ever naming the reviewer.

C5. Load-bearing vocabulary is used plainly but never defined, violating the document's own stated convention ("Terms this system uses in a particular way are introduced in **bold** at first use," How-to-read section).
- "Round" — used constantly as the apparent unit of work (§2.4 "the round where someone is in a hurry"; §4.5 "the round's journal entry… the round is a respawn"; §4.8 "A round was commissioned") and never defined. Is a round a spawn? A work-order lifecycle? A commit? Every discipline in §4 quantifies over it.
- "Cure" — §3.6 "a cure inside an already-ruled rule," §4.8 "A commissioned cure may be **stopped**," §5.7 "The cure." Inferable as "remedy," but "a cure inside an already-ruled rule" is opaque legalese a cold reader cannot apply.
- "Dispatch" — §4.1/§4.2 ("what the dispatch did not declare"; "a dispatch names every other agent") — is a dispatch the work order of §3.2, the spawn prompt, or a third thing?
- Packet-family drift: "task packet" (§1.0, §1.1), "packet" (§3 passim), "work order" (§3.2), "sign-off packets" (§1.2), "defect packets" (§4.3). §3 is titled "The artifact grammar" but the grammar never states what a packet generically is or enumerates the packet types; the reader must reverse-engineer the taxonomy.

=== MAJOR — structure and content that lose the reader or leave rules unenforceable ===

M1. §1.4's header says "Separation of duties — three lines, each drawn against a specific temptation" and then lists four items, (a) through (d). Either the count is wrong or "lines" means the three org lines, in which case the header does not describe the list at all. Worse, §1.4(a)'s exception is incomprehensible at that point in the document: "the review is split: the competent seat checks the substance and a second seat checks that the guard is exactly the rule and not wider (§3.5)" — "guard" and "rule" are countersignature concepts defined two chapters later, and as written the sentence appears to permit exactly the self-review that (a) forbids ("the competent seat checks the substance" of its own artifact).

M2. Branch topology is incoherent as stated. §2.6's serialized-history rule: "One working branch, no per-agent branches… merges to the trunk must be trivial and are verified as such." So there are (at least) two branches — a working branch and a trunk — but their relationship, who merges, when, and what "trivial" means (fast-forward? empty diff?) are all unstated; "verified as such" names a check with no predicate. Then §3.9 permits campaign artifacts "on refs that are never merged" and marked pushed refs — additional branches the one-branch rule appears to forbid, with no stated exemption. A cold implementer cannot configure the repository.

M3. §3.9's scoring block is the point where the project-agnostic claim fails hardest. Thirteen dense bullets read as minutes of arguments the reader did not attend: "Row sets rarely discriminate between defect classes; messages do" (rows of what? first and only use); "A check that no possible defect can reach counts one observation twice if counted" (impenetrable — twice relative to what count?); "The gate asks whether the suite as it stands now kills what was seeded" (which of §3.7's four gate types is never linked); a rendering "which is **unscoreable** and whose run is a scope report" ("scope report" undefined). The section itself says this "is where most of the program's hardest reasoning went" — and that reasoning did not make it onto the page in liftable form. I could not re-implement this scoring discipline from the text.

M4. The worker journal model contradicts the journal ownership rule. §1.2: workers "share a journal template per role, with each spawn identified by a token minted into its prompt." If successive spawns are distinct agents (§1.0: "never reused") appending to one per-role journal, that violates §2.2's "No agent writes another agent's journal" — unless the role, not the spawn, is the "agent," which the document never says. Also unresolved: workers cannot commit (C1), and §2.6 allows staging a foreign journal "only as a newly created, entry-free file (onboarding)" — by whom, for a worker spawned mid-program?

M5. Ownership of the constitution is muddled across three passages. §1.2: the specification lead owns "all documentation." §2: the protocol is "amended only by the procedure in §2.7, and only the orchestrator commits it." §3.6: constitutional text from a decision record is "applied to the constitution by the seat that owns that file, in its own commit" — which seat is that, given the previous two statements? Similarly, §3.6's acceptance act is "a separate journal entry by the seat with authority," but no authority table exists anywhere: for a given record class, the reader cannot determine the accepting seat. And §1.2's cross-reference for the orchestrator's clerical edits points to §3.6, which is about decision records; the transcription mechanism it seems to mean lives in §1.5 and §3.7.

M6. Referenced-but-never-specified artifacts. The text leans on artifacts a cold reader has no way to construct: the "enforcement self-test" (§2.7 amendment precondition, §6.2 adoption step 4 — never described: what it tests, where it lives, who owns it); "the org chart" (§1.6 recovery sequence — never introduced as an artifact; charters were the role documents); the "journal index… refreshed at boundaries" (§1.6 — which boundaries? refreshed by whom, under whose write scope?); the sponsor's "single file [that] tells them how to spot-check the whole program by hand in four commands" (§4.7 — a tantalizingly specific claim with zero content; the four commands are exactly what an adopter would need); the "domain pack" a domain-grade lesson "must name" (§3.10 — packs are never defined); and §3.10's "collating seat" and the operator of "the classifier" are never named.

M7. §3.4's test for self-serving findings cannot be applied: "check whether the finding's two halves move in the same direction… a filing that widens its filer's exposure in one limb while narrowing it in another…" A finding's "two halves" and "limbs" are never defined (the only "two halves" defined in the document are a rule and its failure class, in How-to-read — which cannot be what is meant here). The document's stated safeguard for the integrity of its whole findings economy rests on an unstated anatomy.

=== MINOR — friction, forward references, and local defects ===

m1. The bold-at-first-use convention is repeatedly broken by forward references without pointers: "sign-off" bolded in §1.2 but defined in §3.8; "campaign" used in §3.3's sealed-prediction contents six sections before §3.9 defines campaigns; "a sealed sweep, an undisclosed mapping" (§3.3) never defined anywhere; "seeded-defect dispositions" in §3.8's sign-off contents before §3.9; "work-order identifier" (§2.1) and "gate checklist" (§2.5) before their sections.

m2. Machine-checked rules with unspecified predicates: §2.1 "An entry without work is allowed only when explicitly marked as such" — the mark's syntax and location (entry header? trailer?) are unstated, yet the commit script must parse it; §2.6 Trailers "protected keys cannot be shadowed or duplicated" — the protected keys are never enumerated and "shadowed" is undefined; §3.9 "No agent in the graded line is running while a manifest is applied" — "the graded line" is ambiguous (the suite is the subject under test, suggesting verification, but the safety-net clauses cited are "in the builder charters").

m3. "Phase" and "milestone" are both used as program structure (§1.6 program-state file: "current milestone"; §3.7 gates "per phase"; §4.6 E1 "Phase-gate approval") with no definition of either or of their relationship.

m4. §3.6 is in tension with itself: the headline rule "Every non-obvious choice gets one" is immediately narrowed by the ruling that a record "is owed when a change moves a **reading rule**… and is not owed for a figure, a scope, or a cure inside an already-ruled rule" — a non-obvious choice of figure appears to fall under both the yes and the no. The narrowing sentence is also among the least parseable in the document.

m5. Local garbles a cold reader trips on: §3.7 "each entry must state in the signer's own log that it signs that item" (an entry states in a log — the entry is in the log; presumably "each signature must reference an entry that states…"); §2.4(b) "A warning is right there — not as a weaker version of a refusal…" (reads as "nearby" before resolving to "correct, in that case"); §5.2 "It then happened a second time, at a third item, in the same round."

m6. Canary tension in §1.5: "The mechanism is documented; the instances never are" — yet "a missed canary is a critical finding against the auditor," and a critical finding is an E4 escalation and an artifact, which necessarily documents the instance. Presumably "never documented in advance" is meant; as written the two sentences conflict.

m7. §1.2 states worker review only for the implementation lead ("Reviews worker output in its line"); whether the verification and specification leads carry the same duty for their lines is left to inference, in a document that elsewhere insists roles must not be inferred.

=== SUMMARY JUDGMENT ===
The document keeps its promise on the "why" layer: failure classes are consistently stated and genuinely transferable, and §5 and §6 are the strongest, most self-contained sections. It does not keep its promise on the "how" layer. The five critical findings share one shape: the machinery most emphasized as load-bearing — the commit gate, the scope table, the seeding separation, the seal — is described by its properties and its failure classes but not by its mechanism, and in three places (C1, C2, C3) the stated properties contradict each other. A cold adopter following §6.2 step 2 ("Turn on the mechanical layer before the first real work") would stall immediately: the mechanical layer cannot be built from this document alone.
