FIRST PRINCIPLES OPINION — the document answers the wrong question, and the org already knows the right answer

Strip the framing. The owner's actual problem is not "describe the process"; it is "make this framework survive transplantation, given that previous exports died." Rebuild that problem from zero and it has three coupled layers that must arrive together: the REASONS (why each rule exists), the RULES (what compliance is), and the CHECKS (the machinery that makes the rules real). §6.3 diagnoses the previous failure correctly — rules shipped without reasons read as ceremony and get dropped. Then the document over-corrects into the mirror-image failure: it ships reasons plus rule-statements and withholds every check. By its own axiom (§2.4: "a rule without its check is a suggestion"), PROCESS.md is a 1,445-line suggestion. Bill F1 is the empirical proof: §6.2 step 2 compresses ~1,500 lines of incident-hardened bash — the SIGPIPE landmine, chain-enumeration-from-state-not-filesystem, trailer anti-shadowing — into one bullet. Charlie, the controlled experiment, confirms the prediction: a cold adopter stalls at step 2 (C1, C2). The theory "reasons regenerate mechanisms" is falsified by this repo's own history: twenty ADRs, a 42-case self-test, and multiple green-locally-red-in-CI incidents were needed to get the mechanisms right, and none of that regenerates from reasons.

Four consequences of taking the problem apart honestly:

1. The purity rule is a category error, and it is the root cause of most findings in all three reports. "This document contains no project" conflates two different kinds of noun. Domain nouns (modules, FPGA, encodings) rightly go. Substrate and infrastructure nouns — the runtime where agents cannot spawn (§1.1, named once, never conditioned on — Bill F3), the 256 KiB read-tool limit silently anchoring the journal thresholds, GitHub's branch-protection semantics (Bill F4), the enforcement scripts themselves — are not "project." They are the ground the mechanisms stand on. Stripping them does two harms at once: it makes the mechanisms unimplementable (Charlie C1–C3: the commit machinery, the scope table, the seeder seat cannot be reconstructed), and it exports substrate constants disguised as process constants, which the next adopter will import unmeasured. The fix is structural, not editorial: a substrate-parameters annex where every constant carries its anchor and the instruction "measure this from your environment" — the discipline §2.2 already applies to one parameter and the document nowhere generalizes.

2. The document violates its own root class, §5.7, and this is why Bob's findings exist. Apply the document's own general test to the document: who is made worse off when a claim in PROCESS.md is false, and does that party control whether the falsehood is flagged? Same party — the org describing itself, with no auditor pass over the text, no countersignature from the seats its claims constrain, no check binding its mechanical claims to the scripts. The measured result is exactly what §5.7 predicts: the document is false precisely where it praises its own honesty — "write scopes forbid it" is false against policy.sh and four commits (Bob F1); "every rule is re-verified by CI" is false for the size cap, the very asymmetry §2.4 calls the defect (F3); the recovery drill is asserted as performed and never happened (F4); the seal is described with a self-hash field that cannot exist and that the record's actual seals explicitly disclaim (F7); a rule one day old is narrated as standing law (F6). Under the org's own grammar (§5.4), PROCESS.md is testimony formatted as structure. A process document in this organization is a normative-adjacent artifact and must be treated as one: claims-vs-enforcement audit before landing, posture declared per claim (§5.5's own rule — each clause says whether it is machine-enforced, review-enforced, or aspirational), FAILs preserved (§3.8: the dormant auditor and the never-run drill belong in the text as open items, not as achievements).

3. "Complete" is an unfalsifiable claim, and this org owns the machinery for exactly this class of claim and did not use it. §3.9 exists because "the tests pass" is unfalsifiable until seeded defects are put in front of the suite. "This document is complete" is the same class: a green self-report. Its falsifier is a cold adopter attempting §6.2 — and Charlie ran that campaign for free and scored a survivor-rich result: the mechanical layer cannot be built (C1), the seeder is a seat the roster never seats (C3), the unit of work ("round") is undefined (C5). Bill F13 names the missing instrument: adoption has no definition of done. First principles demand one: cold agent plus the export kit yields a green enforcement self-test, a live-fire bounce on the platform dependency, and a bootstrap retro-audit pass — the G0 pattern (Bill F2) which is itself structurally necessary for ANY self-referential rule system (a constitution cannot ratify its own ratifier; the founding act is always outside the rules and laundered retroactively) and which the document omits entirely while §6.2 hand-waves adoption in five bullets. Adoption is a program. The document that describes programs better than anything else in this repo declines to describe the one program its reader will run.

4. The right artifact already half-exists, and the document's silence about it is the single most telling defect. The board and ADR-0018 A1 show a two-track export already running: an executable shell repo (harvests route to "the shell's universal set and named domain packs"; PR #3 open; shell-transcription boxes sit on the live P1-module-ready gate). PROCESS.md never mentions it. So the org is maintaining two export vehicles that do not reference each other — which is §2.4's own worse-than-either failure lifted one level: two artifacts that disagree about what the process is, with the disagreement invisible from both sides. Bob F6 shows the drift already present at birth (PROTOCOL §10 still states the abandoned transient-apply model while PROCESS narrates pushed refs). There is no files-list-equality check binding PROCESS.md's mechanism descriptions to policy.sh, so they WILL diverge; prose that duplicates mechanism outside the constitution is the five-documents-one-figure failure of §2.7, self-inflicted.

Verdict on the council's question: a process reference is the right COMPONENT and the wrong UNIT OF COMPLETENESS. Do not discard this document — §5, the failure classes, and the posture taxonomy are the genuinely portable core all three auditors independently confirm, and they honor the stated thesis "the failure classes are the export." But re-shape the deliverable: (a) the spine of the export is the executable shell — scripts, CI, constitution template, charter templates, launcher prompts, packet schemas — with PROCESS.md demoted to its rationale layer, each rule stated once beside its check and its failure class so the triple cannot drift; (b) a substrate annex with every anchored constant and its measurement rule; (c) a bootstrap chapter written from G0's real record (self-signed founding, adversarial charter review, retro-audit as the laundering mechanism); (d) the word "complete" earned by a gated cold-adopter live fire, not asserted. The document's deepest irony is that it contains every principle needed to convict itself: a rule without its check is a suggestion; the author grading its own homework is the root class; a control claimed mechanical and not is worse than one known advisory; an untested recovery procedure is a hypothesis. An untested adoption procedure is one too. The failure classes are the export — but only a kit that ships reasons, rules, and checks together, bound by a check of their agreement and proven by one cold re-instantiation, solves the problem the owner actually has.
