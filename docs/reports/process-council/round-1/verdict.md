## Where the Council Clashes

**Condemn or capitalize.** The Contrarian and the Expansionist read identical facts and reach opposite postures. The Contrarian: the document is an unreviewed self-assessment (landed same-day, one seat, no work order, no countersignature, no audit) containing false mechanical claims — by the org's own law it is a DRAFT that must be bounced, and accepting it is the org waving its own homework through. The Expansionist: the gaps ARE the roadmap, the artifacts to close every critical finding already exist in-repo, and the correct response is extraction and packaging, not indictment. They disagree because they weight different audiences — the Contrarian defends the org's internal integrity (stateless agents will be mistrained by false "you cannot" statements at every spawn), the Expansionist defends the external opportunity. Both are right about their own audience. The verdict below takes the Contrarian's process and the Expansionist's destination.

**More prose or less.** The Outsider prescribes discipline edits to the text itself: rebuild the deleted referents in §3.9, define the absorbed dialect (round, cure, dispatch, limb), stamp every sentence. The Executor and First Principles Thinker say the fastest path is a starter repo, with PROCESS.md demoted to the rationale layer — "the fix is not more prose." Reasonable advisors disagree because they are fixing different failure modes: the Outsider is fixing confident misdirection (a careful stranger reasons diligently to false conclusions), which only text repair cures; the Executor is fixing the day-one stall, which only tooling cures. These are not alternatives — but they compete for the same revision budget, and the council did not resolve the split. The Recommendation does.

**Take §3.9 on faith or rewrite it.** Bill (F12) argues the scoring calculus should transfer verbatim — no adopter will re-pay "the program's hardest reasoning" before their first campaign mis-scores. The Outsider argues it cannot transfer at all as written: it is "a verdict archive mislabeled as doctrine," thirteen compressed rulings whose case files never made the page, and its one plain-language instruction ("fixed cadence") is wrong against the record (the real control is per-module gating between review-accept and sign-off — Bill F6 and Bob F6 agree). The disagreement is genuine: verbatim transfer of opaque rules preserves calibration but violates the document's own thesis that rules without understood failure classes get dropped. The resolution is neither: rewrite the referents using the anonymized-pattern method (which the authors demonstrably command), and mark the section "import as rules, do not re-derive."

**Whether n=1 disqualifies the claim.** The Contrarian: one program, ten days, one module PASS delivered the same day the document shipped — every "order that works" claim is untested generalization, and the ceremony-to-product ratio is the unstated number that decides adoption. The Expansionist: it is the only battle-tested governance kernel in existence and the record's density is the moat. Both true; the honest fix is scoping the claims, not deleting them.

**The theory of transfer is falsified by the org's own history.** The document bets that reasons regenerate mechanisms. Reasons did not regenerate mechanisms even for the people who owned them.

**Two export vehicles that do not reference each other.** Bill stumbled on the Shell extraction in the task record; the First Principles Thinker elevated it: the org is maintaining an executable shell repo and a prose reference that never mentions it, with no check binding them — §2.4's "worse than either alone" failure, self-inflicted one level up, and Bob F6 shows the drift already present at birth (PROTOCOL §10 still describes the abandoned transient-apply model).

**Two undersold strengths.** The Expansionist caught what the critics missed: Bob's audit is itself the demo — a cold outsider mechanically falsified the org's claims from its own record in one session, which is the traceability property working; and the record contains a real live-fire proof (a force-push refused on a transient ref) that §2.5 demands and the document never cites.
