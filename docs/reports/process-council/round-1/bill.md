# ADOPTION AUDIT — docs/PROCESS.md as a standing-up document for a new organization

Auditor: BILL. Subject: /home/user/agentic-fpga/docs/PROCESS.md (1,446 lines). Enforcement surfaces inspected for comparison: /home/user/agentic-fpga/agents/PROTOCOL.md, /home/user/agentic-fpga/scripts/ (agent_commit.sh, policy.sh, check_journals.sh, test_protocol.sh, verify_journal_chain.sh), /home/user/agentic-fpga/.github/workflows/journal-check.yml, /home/user/agentic-fpga/docs/gates/G0-checklist.md, /home/user/agentic-fpga/docs/SPONSOR.md.

Framing: I read the document as the person responsible for standing up a NEW org in a different domain, with nothing from this repo in hand except the document itself — which is the document's own claim about itself ("stated so that it can be lifted into a program that has nothing in common with the one that paid for it"). Findings ranked by adoption risk.

## F1 — CRITICAL: The mechanical layer is commanded but never supplied, and the prose cannot reconstruct it

§6.2 step 2: "Turn on the mechanical layer before the first real work. Coupling, append-only, path isolation, files-list equality, and continuous-integration re-verification." One bullet. What that bullet stands for in the origin repo is ~1,500 lines of battle-hardened bash across five scripts plus a CI workflow, and the document contains no specification of any check — not the commit-script behavior, not the CI workflow shape, not what the self-test must cover.

Worse: the prose cannot carry what the scripts know. The scripts encode paid-for fixes that are invisible in PROCESS.md and that an adopter re-implementing from the document will re-pay one incident at a time:

- Trailer anti-shadowing via `git interpret-trailers --parse` plus uniqueness of protected keys (check_journals.sh: "a crafted body line or duplicate trailer cannot shadow the real one (R6)"). PROCESS §2.6 says only "protected keys cannot be shadowed or duplicated" — the *how* is the hard part.
- Chain enumeration "from the state being checked, never the filesystem" (policy.sh: "a worktree glob in check_journals.sh would validate old commits against today's worktree, crediting history with volumes it never contained"). A naive reimplementation does exactly the forbidden thing and passes every test until history rotates.
- The SIGPIPE/pipefail landmine in `volume_header_field` (policy.sh comment: "an early exit SIGPIPEs a `git show` producer once the volume outgrows the pipe buffer... green locally, red in CI"). Nothing in the document even gestures at this class.
- Merge triviality defined operationally: "its tree must equal one parent's tree" (check_journals.sh). PROCESS §2.6 says merges "must be trivial and are verified as such" — not implementable as stated (see F10).
- `--no-renames` on every diff, rename/copy refusal for journals, the R8 seed-tightening ("only volume 01 of a chainless agent may be seeded").

The document's purity rule ("This document contains no project. No modules, no domain... no identifiers") strips exactly the pointers an adopter needs: it never says a reference implementation exists, where it lives, or that one was extracted into a reusable shell (this program's own task record shows "Shell: chunk 1 — enforcement core + PROTOCOL v2 into new repo" — an adoption vehicle PROCESS.md never mentions). Day one under the document alone is a constitution-shaped essay and zero tooling, with step 2 of the adoption order being a multi-week engineering effort presented as one line.

The document's own principle convicts it: §2.4, "a rule without its check is a suggestion." PROCESS.md exports the rules and withholds the checks.

## F2 — CRITICAL: The bootstrap is undescribed, and it necessarily violates the document's own rules

Every normative instrument requires separation: "Every normative change is signed by a seat that did not write it" (§3.5); "A record proposes; a different seat accepts" (§3.6); charters change "only by the amendment procedure." On day one there are no seats. The document never resolves this. The origin repo did: G0-checklist.md shows the orchestrator wrote and self-signed items 1–7 (protocol, all nine charters, journal seeds, self-test, CI), with three compensating controls — an adversarial charter review, sponsor ratification (item 8), and an auditor *retro-audit of the entire bootstrap commit range* (item 10, "AUD-0001... PASS WITH FINDINGS", plus item 11's re-verification gate release). That retro-audit is the mechanism that launders the self-signed bootstrap, and it appears nowhere in PROCESS.md.

§6.2 step 1 says "Ratify the roles first, adversarially. Have the charter set attacked from at least three lenses" — attacked by whom? The reviewers must be spawned by someone under some rules before any rules are ratified. §1.3's anonymized pattern ("three independent reviewers were set on the charter set") elides the same question. The adopter is left to invent the bootstrap sequencing: who writes the constitution, under what identity pre-G0 commits land, how the sole-committer commits before the commit script exists, how journals are seeded (the R8 mechanism exists precisely for onboarding and is described in one table row), and that the first auditor act should be a retro-audit of everything that preceded it.

## F3 — CRITICAL: The document's spine rests on substrate facts it names once and never conditions on

§1.1: "The first monopoly starts as a fact about the substrate: in the environment this was built for, a spawned agent cannot itself spawn. Rather than fight that, the design makes it load-bearing." An adopter on a substrate where subagents CAN spawn loses the mechanical basis of the spawning monopoly — it reverts from "cannot" to "must not," which is exactly the degradation §2.3 warns about ("converts 'you must not' into 'you cannot,' and the second is the only one that survives a hurried round"). The document gives no guidance for that case: no compensating check, no statement that the monopoly must then be declared review-enforced per its own §2.4 posture rule.

Other silent substrate anchors an adopter must find alone:

- Journal size thresholds. §2.2: "anchored to a stated reason (one volume should be one readable unit)." The actual anchor is in policy.sh: "S (soft, warn) is anchored to the 256 KiB Read-tool limit" — a Claude Code tool constant. The document never says the number must be *measured from your reader's actual limit*; an adopter imports 256 KiB as if it were a process constant.
- Read-blinding. §1.4(c) honestly declares it unenforceable ("nothing in a general agent runtime can deny read access to a path"). But an adopter whose runtime CAN sandbox reads is never told to upgrade the control from prompt-and-audit to mechanical — the honesty note fossilizes as a permanent exemption.
- The spawn-prompt mechanism. §1.3: "An agent's first mandatory action on every spawn is to read its own charter and the shared rules." Made mandatory *how*? In the origin: launcher definitions in .claude/agents/ whose prompts encode it, plus the minted spawn short-id ("work-order id + spawn UTC timestamp... which the worker copies verbatim into Trigger", PROTOCOL §4.1). PROCESS §1.2 reduces this to "a token minted into its prompt" with no format, no uniqueness property, no statement that launchers are an artifact class the adopter must build.
- Model-class assignment (leads vs workers on different model tiers) — an operational decision with cost and quality consequences, absent entirely.

## F4 — HIGH: The one declared out-of-repository dependency is stated as a subset of what is actually required

§2.5 declares "force pushes are prevented by branch protection configured in the hosting platform... a one-time sponsor duty, on a gate checklist." Good instinct; incomplete content. The real G0 item 9 and its click-path require substantially more than no-force-push: restrict deletions; empty bypass list ("an empty bypass list makes a ruleset admin-proof by default" — the admin threat is the sponsor's own pushing account); protection on BOTH main and the working branch; a *required* status check on main; an ordering constraint ("Do this only after item 5 is green — the journal-check status check must have run at least once before GitHub will list it"); and the two-ruleset split with its rationale ("GitHub rejects direct pushes to any branch with required status checks... so the check requirement must bind main only"). An adopter following PROCESS.md configures no-force-push, believes the guarantee, and lacks: deletion protection, admin-bypass closure, and — most important — the platform-side *requirement* of CI. §2.5's "continuous integration is the adjudicator" is only true if the platform refuses merge on red CI; a CI that runs but does not gate is a self-report one level up, which is §2.5's own failure class. The document never states that CI must be made blocking by platform configuration. Non-GitHub adopters (GitLab, Gerrit, bare git) must re-derive all of it from one sentence.

## F5 — HIGH: No artifact inventory, no templates, and the grammar–enforcement coupling is unstated

Section 3 describes roughly ten artifact families (journal entries, work orders, sign-offs, review verdicts, findings, countersignatures, decision records, gates, sealed predictions, defect manifests, harvest notes) by content-list, but ships zero schemas, no naming or numbering conventions, no directory layout, and no single enumerated list of "the files your org must have before work starts" (charters, constitution, org chart, program-state file, journal index, launcher prompts, gate checklists, sponsor guide, ADR template, packet templates, harvest block). The origin repo has all of these; the adopter must reverse-engineer the inventory from scattered mentions.

The deeper hazard: conventions and enforcement are coupled. The checks *encode* the grammar — the entry-header regex `^## \[J-<agent>-NNNN\]`, the four trailer keys, the literal section name `### Files-in-this-commit`, the volume-header field names, the journal path pattern, the packet prefixes. Every grammar choice the adopter makes is also an enforcement-surface choice, and the document never says so. Its own §2.4 names the resulting failure exactly: "a rule and its check that disagree about what compliance is are worse than either alone, because the disagreement is invisible from both sides." Re-deriving grammar and checks independently — which is what the document's silence invites — is the shortest path to that failure.

Also load-bearing and absent: §3.1's parser-safety rule ("An entry body may never contain a line that looks like its own journal's entry header... because the parsers are deliberately simple") IS in the document — one of the few places implementation leaks through — but it is the exception that proves how much else did not.

## F6 — HIGH: Following the document's seeded-campaign trigger produces a weaker control than the original

§3.9: "on a fixed cadence, an independent seat authors defect manifests." That is not what the origin runs. PROTOCOL §10 sequences the campaign as a *merge precondition*, per module: "for each module, the campaign runs after rtl_lead's RV- ACCEPT and before dv_lead may issue SO- PASS, so every PASS reports the disposition of every seeded mutation." A campaign that gates every sign-off and a campaign that runs "on a fixed cadence" (read: quarterly sweep) are materially different controls — the first makes every green sign-off falsifiable; the second leaves windows in which "the tests pass" is exactly the unfalsifiable claim §3.9 exists to kill. The document's only hint at the true sequencing is "No agent in the graded line is running while a manifest is applied," which does not communicate the precondition relationship. An adopter implementing the document as written imports the ceremony and misses the placement, which is where the value lives.

## F7 — HIGH: The machinery assumes a greenfield, agents-only repository, and the document never says so

Every check runs "over the entire pushed history" (§2.5), and every commit in that history must carry trailers, a journal append, and a scope-conformant file set. Consequences the document leaves unstated: (a) adoption into a repo with pre-existing history fails the full-history check on every pre-adoption commit — there is no epoch or grandfathering mechanism described anywhere; (b) a mixed human/agent team breaks R1–R9 the first time a human commits without the script — the document's org has exactly one human, who never commits, and nothing marks this as an assumption rather than an incidental. §6.2's "Retrofitting these onto an existing history is far more expensive" hints at (a) without stating the blunt truth: as specified, the layer only works on a repository whose commit #1 already complies. An adopter with an existing codebase or human colleagues is stranded on day one with no stated options (fresh repo + import commit under the protocol; epoch marker; per-range checking — all workable, none mentioned).

## F8 — MEDIUM-HIGH: The lessons harvest routes into infrastructure the document never defines

§3.10 requires domain-grade rules to "name the domain pack it belongs to" and war stories to be "kept, binding nowhere, re-offerable later." Neither "domain pack" nor the destination of general-grade rules is defined anywhere in the document. The origin routes harvests into a separate federation: PROTOCOL §7 — "the orchestrator collates... into the generic shell's LESSONS file with permalinked provenance, the shell unfreezing for exactly one commit per harvest, sponsor-visible at the gate," with routing rules per grade — and the real export packet (docs/federation/outbox/SO-xgmii_rx_64.md, 353 candidate rows, hide-tests, fences, provisional ids) shows how heavy that pipeline actually is. An adopter can run the classifier §3.10 describes and then has nowhere to put the output; the harvest cadence the document tells them to start on day one (§6.2 step 5) dead-ends at an undefined noun. Either the document should define a minimal local destination (a LESSONS file in-repo) or state that federation is optional infrastructure with its own document.

## F9 — MEDIUM-HIGH: The sponsor's instruments are named but not given — and the sponsor is the least recoverable seat

Two instruments the whole design leans on exist only as titles:

- §4.7: "A single file tells them how to spot-check the whole program by hand in four commands." The four commands are not in the document. (The origin's docs/SPONSOR.md exists; its content — where to look, what the six classes look like in plain language, the bounce rule — is precisely what a new sponsor needs and none of it transferred.)
- §1.5 canaries: "the only instrument that distinguishes" a silent auditor from a clean program. No canary catalog, no cadence guidance, no difficulty calibration, no examples beyond "hand-edit a record, ask for an altered relay." The adopter's sponsor — explicitly anticipated as a non-specialist (§6.2 lens 3) — must hand-craft convincing process violations with zero guidance. The one control that cannot be self-checked by the org is the least specified in the document.

## F10 — MEDIUM: §6.1's "transfers directly" list overclaims, and collides with the document's own re-verification rule

"Transfers directly: the seat topology and the two monopolies; the write-scope table; the commit rules..." Two problems. First, the write-scope table is literally this repo's paths (policy.sh: `libs/*|top/*|bin/*...` per agent) — an artifact the document, by its own purity rule, does not contain; what transfers is the *shape* (disjointness, auditor singleton, handoffs-shared-except-auditor, orchestrator-owns-enforcement-surfaces). "Transfers directly" applied to an artifact the adopter has never seen is a claim about nothing. Second, ten lines below, the document (correctly) orders: "The one thing that must not be imported as a claim: any statement in this document that a control is mechanically enforced. Check it in your own machinery before you repeat it." Every item on the transfers-directly list IS such a statement once ported. The two passages pull in opposite directions with no reconciliation; the honest formulation — rule *statements* transfer, every enforcement *claim* re-verifies — is derivable but unstated.

## F11 — MEDIUM: The commit-rule table omits rules the real layer enforces

§2.6's nine-row table is presented as "the numbered set." The real layer also enforces: (a) R10 volume-chain integrity re-verified *per commit* in CI — gapless volumes 01..N, back-link path/sha256/Continues-from verification, entry-id contiguity across the concatenated chain (check_journals.sh's largest block). §2.2 describes chaining as a property but not that it is checked at every commit over all history, which is what makes it a guarantee; (b) the blob-size gate (R11, ADR-0002 — large files refused, "large data ships as fetch script + checksum manifest") — absent from the document entirely, arguably project policy but part of the enforced set an adopter would port; (c) the operational definition of a trivial merge (tree equals one parent's tree; octopus refused) — the table's "verified as such" is not implementable without it; (d) the CI workflow runs the *enforcement self-test itself* on every push (journal-check.yml step "Protocol enforcement self-test") — a check-the-checker discipline the document nowhere mentions; §2.7 only requires self-test updates at amendments.

## F12 — MEDIUM: Import/re-verify guidance is inverted in both directions at specific points

§6.1 draws the line well in general ("Has to be re-earned: the judgment about which rules... need to be machine-enforced"). But:

- Should be imported verbatim, document undersells: the §3.9 scoring taxonomy (sealed vs seeded columns, unscoreable classes, negative controls, the equivalence-proof standard quantifying over the specification's input space, the survived-defect evidence form, the unreachable-check rule). The document itself says this is "where most of the program's hardest reasoning went" — yet §6.1's re-earn clause invites an adopter to re-derive calibration, and nothing marks scoring as the part to take on faith *as rules* precisely because no adopter will re-pay that reasoning before their first campaign mis-scores.
- Should be re-derived, document presents as fixed: the three-class seeding floor ("at least three, spanning distinct defect classes" — calibrated to hardware mutation classes); the E3 escalation class ("Toolchain and licensing" — this program's sponsor surface, not a universal); the E6 threshold; the size thresholds (F3); the three charter-review lenses. Each is stated as part of the machinery with no marker that it is a parameter another domain must re-anchor.

## F13 — LOW-MEDIUM: No acceptance test for the adoption itself

§6.2 step 3 demands live fire for exactly one control: "verify it by live fire — an attempted violation that actually bounces. An unverified protective setting is a belief." Correct — and applied to nothing else. The origin's G0 required a 26-scenario self-test green, CI green on real pushes, and an auditor retro-audit before any work order issued. The document's step 4 says "Run the enforcement self-test" without ever describing what scenarios one must contain (per §2.4's pair principle, at minimum an accept and a reject case per rule — left to inference), and defines no equivalent of G0's "every bootstrap commit itself satisfies the commit protocol." The adopter has no stated bar for "the machinery is on."

## What actually transfers as-is (verified against the real surfaces)

For completeness of the mandate: the parts of the document an adopter can take without re-derivation, because they are substrate- and domain-free and match the implemented system: the two-monopolies argument and its costs (§1.1); the auditor's four structural properties (§1.5); statelessness and the recovery read-order (§1.6/§9); the coupling/append-only/files-list *rule statements* (§2.1–2.2, §2.6 — statements, not checks; see F1/F10); the machine-vs-review-enforced posture taxonomy (§2.4, §5.5); the work-order state machine and out-of-scope discipline (§3.2); R-SEAL-1's claim/promise/retrospective distinctions (§3.3); the finding direction-test (§3.4); narrow countersignatures and delta-signatures (§3.5); instrument-does-not-edit-what-it-governs (§3.6); the operating disciplines of §4 (abort-first, declared siblings, verbatim relay, push-at-landing, incident recovery, refusal); and the failure museum wholesale — §5 is the document's genuinely portable core and needs nothing from this repo.

## Day one, as the document leaves it

The adopter opens §6.2 and finds: step 1 requires charters that do not exist, written from §1.3's content list (usable) by a process with a staffing paradox (F2); step 2 is a one-line stand-in for the largest engineering effort in the adoption, with no reference implementation named and the hard-won details unexported (F1); step 3 is actionable but a subset of what the origin actually required (F4); step 4 references a self-test the adopter has not been told how to build (F13); step 5 starts a harvest whose output has no defined destination (F8). Missing from the order entirely: write the constitution (the document describes what §2's protocol contains but the order never says to produce one); build the launcher prompts; produce the sponsor guide; run the bootstrap retro-audit. The document is an excellent explanation of *why* every mechanism exists — its stated thesis, "the failure classes... are the export" (§6.3), is honored — but it systematically withholds *what to build and in what form*, on the theory that reasons regenerate mechanisms. The origin's own history says otherwise: the mechanisms took twenty ADRs, twenty-six self-test scenarios, and multiple green-locally-red-in-CI incidents to get right, and none of that is recoverable from the reasons alone.
