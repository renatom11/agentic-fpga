1. THE DOCUMENT'S IMPLIED READER IS THE READER OF ITS OWN FIRST EDITION — AND NO ADOPTER WILL EVER BE THAT PERSON. This is the structural form of the curse of knowledge here, and it is the finding I most want the council to weigh above the itemized defects it produces. A very large fraction of the normative text states the current rule only as a diff against an absent prior text: §1.4 opens its separations-of-duties list with a parenthetical about what "the first edition's header said" before the list itself has been read; §3.9's survivor exhibit consists almost entirely of a retraction of a passage I do not have ("this passage previously opened on a defect... an event this record does not contain"), forcing me to reconstruct the actual discipline from the negative space of a correction; §1.4(b) and §1.5 cite "its own §6.1 test" — a section number of the *first* edition, resolvable only through a gloss thirteen hundred lines away. The authors know what the first edition said, so contrast reads to them as information; to a stranger it is a two-edition diff I must maintain in working memory while the plain rule is nowhere stated plainly. Charlie's Finding 3 and Bill's F8 name this; I confirm every instance from my own cold read and add the consequence Sal's guidance #3 makes decisive: since Bob's verification shows the correction apparatus is TRUE, every one of these repairs is pure architecture — state the rule first, put the archaeology behind it — and not one requires weakening a claim (C3, C4.7, C9.4).
