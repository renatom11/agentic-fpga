# CHARLIE — Cold-Reader Audit of /home/user/agentic-fpga/docs/PROCESS.md

**Method and scope.** I read the full document (5,446 lines) and nothing else — no journals, no scripts, no charters, no shell. Everything below is derivable from the text alone. Of Sal's framework I could apply only the text-facing criteria: C3 (human readability), C4 (AI-actionability from the text), C5 (replicability judged as textual sufficiency, not against the repo), C6 (agnosticism as visible in the text), C9 (internal consistency). C1-census, C2-accuracy, C7-matrix-vs-scripts, C8-provenance and C10-ownership require the repo as ground truth and are outside a cold reader's reach; where a finding touches them I say so and judge only what the text does to a reader.

**A scope fact the council should have before the findings.** The mandate says the document "claims to be a complete, project-agnostic description... sufficient for a stranger." The fifth edition does not make that claim. Its opening paragraph re-scopes it explicitly: "Read on its own, this document is a description... from which you can understand every control and rebuild none of them cheaply. The replication claim belongs to the unit, not to this half" — and it reports its own two adoption runs, one returning "roughly one hundred percent of the executable layer invented" and the second still inventing "some eight hundred and fifty lines of enforcement script and push-hook logic" (§6.0, §6.2). So the largest possible cold-reader finding — "I could not rebuild the enforcement layer from this text" — is true, pre-confessed, measured twice, and disclosed on page one. My findings below are therefore the gaps and hazards the document does NOT already confess, plus the places its confession apparatus itself misleads or loses a reader.

## Findings, ranked by what most misleads or loses a cold reader

### F1 · MAJOR · C3.2/C3.6 (structure) — The current law is not extractable without processing five editions of archaeology
The document interleaves mechanism with its own revision memoir at paragraph granularity. §1.1 — nominally "why one seat holds both monopolies" — spends more lines on a dormancy table, three margin notes, and a demoted-count dispute than on the mechanism. §3.3's actual rule set must be assembled around a withdrawn rule, a margin about the withdrawal, and four constraints replacing it. The reader who wants "what is the rule now" must, in nearly every section, separate the running line from `SUPERSEDED` blocks, `[CORRECTED]` stamps, annex pointers, and edition genealogy. The document knows: the "core/memoir separation" is recorded as "NOT ATTEMPTED, for the second consecutive edition, and the endorsement is now overdue" (B.10 refusal 1), and its own external grading gave readability B− and conciseness C+. As the cold reader that grading anticipated, I confirm the diagnosis: the honesty apparatus — genuinely effective against false confidence — has metastasized into the primary obstacle to comprehension. This is the single largest cost to the reader, it is acknowledged, and it is twice-deferred.

### F2 · MAJOR · C9.5, cross-filed C7-shaped (from the text alone) — The spawning monopoly is given three incompatible enforcement postures
- §1.0: "Sole-spawner and sole-committer: `[RE · C-01, C-06]` — no repo instrument binds who runs the version-control tool" (a gloss that addresses only the *committing* half, attached to a stamp covering both).
- §1.1: "**Both monopolies are rules the seats keep, not properties the machinery holds.**" Then, two paragraphs later, of the same first monopoly: "The first monopoly starts as a fact about the substrate: ... a spawned agent cannot itself spawn."
- Annex A.1: "the sole-spawner monopoly (§1.1) is free — **nothing needs to enforce it**."

"A rule the seats keep" and "free — nothing needs to enforce it" cannot both be the operative posture of one control. A charitable reader can reconstruct the reconciliation (repo machinery vs runtime substrate; the [RE] residue covering what the substrate does not bind), but the document never performs it — and this is the exact defect it names against itself in §1.1: "A stamp on a compound sentence is a stamp on its strongest limb, and it will be read as a stamp on its weakest." A replicator whose substrate matches A.1 cannot tell from the text whether they still owe a compensating control. On the document's signature axis — enforcement honesty — this is its own convicted class, live in its own first section. Repair: split the two monopolies' postures the way §1.1 already splits "history is serialized."

### F3 · MAJOR · C5.5/C1.5 (depth floor) — "Phase" and "milestone" are undefined primitives, so the branch topology and gate cadence cannot be operated
§2.6: the trunk "receives the working branch only at milestone boundaries." §3.7: "per phase a specification-freeze gate, an artifact-ready gate, and a phase-acceptance gate." §1.6's facsimile has "Current milestone / Milestone roadmap." §4.6's E6 presupposes "a phase tracking past twice its estimate." Nowhere does the document say what a phase is, what a milestone is, whether they coincide, who declares either, or what event obliges a trunk merge relative to a phase-acceptance gate. A replicator can build every commit rule and still not know when history moves to the trunk or how many gates stand between founding and first merge. This is a parameterization gap the text does not flag (unlike E6's threshold, which Annex A.9 flags properly).

### F4 · MAJOR · C4.2/C5.3 — The countersignature has no artifact form anywhere in the document
§3.5 specifies properties (narrow scope, asked of the constrained party, refusable) and a delta-signature mechanism whose "traffic is recorded as *owed*... the ledger has to be somewhere a later reader is obliged to look (§5.5)" — with no statement of where a signature physically lives (journal entry? decision-record block? packet return log?), what it looks like, or where the owed ledger is. Every other load-bearing artifact got a FACSIMILE in this edition; this one did not. The omission is admitted only in a buried refusal row (B.8 refusal 7: "the countersignature's physical form" is among items "owed a round") — which a reader of §3.5 will never find, since §3.5 does not point there. I cannot generate a conforming countersignature or delta-signature from this text, for a mechanism §1.0 makes constitutive ("Nothing normative changes without a numbered decision record and a signature from a seat that did not write it").

### F5 · MAJOR · C9.4 (example violates its own stated grammar, in the same block) — §3.7's signature rule contradicts §3.7's own facsimile

### F6 · MAJOR · C5.3/C9.5 — Two authoritative, disagreeing shapes for the day-zero roster file, left standing knowingly

### F7 · MAJOR · C1.5/C5 (own judgment; framework's C1 is repo-facing but the textual half applies) — Review routing above the worker tier is unstated, and "normative" is never delimited
§1.4(a): "Every output is accepted by a seat that did not produce it `[RE · C-20]`." The document routes worker output (to its lead, §1.2), sign-offs (merge precondition), and normative change (decision record + countersignature). It never says who accepts a lead's ordinary output: the verification lead's suite code, the specification lead's spec diffs, the orchestrator's program-state edits. Compounding it, the boundary of "normative" is undefined — §2.7 enumerates constitution/charters/enforcement scripts, §1.0 says "nothing normative changes without...", and a cold reader cannot tell whether a specification change is "normative" (needing record + countersignature) or ordinary work (needing... an unnamed acceptor). The universal (a) claim has no routing table behind it for exactly the seats whose self-review the museum (§5.7) says is the root class.

## Bottom line for the council
Read cold, the document does not fail where a naive reviewer would expect (hidden mechanisms, secret jargon, unexplained flows) — its confession apparatus is real and mostly works. It fails in three characteristic ways instead: (1) the memoir has overgrown the mechanism, and the deferred structural separation is now the dominant readability defect (F1); (2) the confession apparatus itself has begun committing the document's own convicted classes — compound-stamp posture on the spawning monopoly, header-counts over non-matching lists, stale restatements of its own size — which is uniquely corrosive because the apparatus is what a reader is told to trust (F2, F8); and (3) a small set of load-bearing mechanisms still have no implementable form and no disclosure of that fact where the reader meets them: countersignatures, phase/milestone semantics, above-worker review routing, the roster's two shapes (F3–F7). None of these requires restructuring except F1; F2–F8 are line-level repairs the document's own repair machinery is built to cut work orders from.
