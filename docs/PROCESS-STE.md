# PROCESS-STE — the process handbook in Simplified Technical English

**What this volume is.** This is a derived rendition of `docs/PROCESS.md`, eighth
edition. The first revision was derived from that file at commit `f84907b`, the parent
of the commit that added this file. **This revision is derived from the source as the
commit carrying this revision leaves it**, because that commit first repairs four
currency residues in the source and then re-derives the rendition sites that carry
them. This volume restates the handbook's rules in controlled English, so that an
agent or a stranger can parse each rule without a human to resolve an ambiguity.

**This revision answers a cold reading.** A commissioned read-only probe was given
this volume and the source and asked to grade the rendition claim by claim. It
returned three major and ten minor fidelity defects here, and behind four of them it
surfaced residues in the source itself. All are repaired in the commit that carries
this revision, and `docs/PROCESS-MEMOIR.md` §B.13 carries the finding-by-finding
record. **Read that as one cold reading, and not as a standing check**: nothing binds
this volume to the source, and the instrument that would is still owed (§6.0).

**This volume is not law.** `docs/PROCESS.md` governs. Where the two files
disagree, `docs/PROCESS.md` wins and this volume is the defect.

**This volume is re-derived per edition, or it is marked stale.** The source
carries an edition anchor in its head. If the source's edition moves and this file
does not, treat this file as the older edition.

**This volume restates no posture stamp.** The source marks each claim with a
bracketed stamp — `[MC]`, `[RE]`, `[P1]`, `[PLANNED]`, `[CORRECTED]`,
`[UNANCHORED]`, `[SF]`. Those stamps stay in the source. This volume states each
calibration in words on a **Status** line and cites the source section. A reader
who needs the stamp and its posture-list row opens the section that the **More**
line names.

**The calibration is the content.** The source separates six kinds of claim: a
rule a script refuses, a rule only a seat or a form carries, an instrument that
does not exist, an act performed once on one date, an instrument refused on
purpose, and a control that has never run. This volume carries each claim at the
same strength. If a sentence here is stronger than its source sentence, this
volume is the defect.

**The mode split.** **Strict mode** applies to everything normative: the rules,
the procedures, the commit rules, the adoption order, the substrate parameters and
the definitions. **STE-flavored mode** applies to the compressed rationale, which
is the **Reason** and **Failure** lines. There the structural rules apply in full
and the lexical rules are advisory.

**The Simplified Technical English caveat.** This volume applies the **rule
categories** of ASD-STE100. It does not apply the official ASD approved-word
dictionary, which this program does not hold and may not redistribute. Read every
word here as plain English chosen for one meaning, and not as a certified approved
word.

## Departures, flagged

Simplified Technical English permits simple tenses only. This volume keeps a
compound form at five sites, because the simple form loses the current relevance
that the source's claim depends on. The keeps are listed here and nowhere else.

1. **"has never existed"** — the escape ledger (§1.5, §3.8). The simple past says
   the file was absent at some past time. The source's claim is that the file is
   absent now and was absent at every commit.
2. **"has never run"** — the recovery drill (§1.6). The source's claim is that the
   act is still outstanding.
3. **"has never fired"** — escalation class E6 (§4.6). The source's claim is that
   the class remains untested now.
4. **"no marked reference has ever been merged"** (§3.9). The source states a
   measured property of the whole history up to now.
5. **"has never happened"** — the relay-fidelity spot-check (§4.3), and an adoption
   run by an uncommissioned party (§6.0). At §4.3 the source's claim is that the act
   is still outstanding. At §6.0 it gates a live warranty now.

This volume also keeps sentences past the length caps where a shorter form drops a
scope qualifier the source makes load-bearing. **Four** sites are marked **Kept
long**: the placement of a negative control, the definition of a normative change,
the evidence form for a defect that survived its own campaign, and the disposition
form for a campaign kill. *(This paragraph said "five" and listed the two-column rule
for a tally as the fifth. That rule is rendered inside the caps and carries no marker,
so the count is re-derived here against the markers themselves.)*

**The length residue, measured rather than claimed — and the method printed, so a

## Glossary

The source calls these terms **the dialect**. A competent stranger to this program
reads each of them wrongly without a definition.

- **seat** — one role in the organization. A charter defines a seat. Each seat
  holds a write scope and a reasoning log. Whatever agent is spawned against the
  seat fills it. A seat is not an agent: an agent is one spawn, and a seat outlives
  every spawn of it. A seat is not a person and not a team.
- **round** — one spawn of one seat against one commissioned unit of work. A round
  is not a commit: one round can produce several commits, or none. A round is not a
  work order: one work order can take several rounds.
- **dispatch** — the instruction text that a seat receives at spawn. It can carry a
  work order, or be the whole commission by itself. A packet is versioned and
  diffable. A dispatch is not.
- **kit** — the transferable contents of the export unit: the artifacts an adopter
  obtains instead of writing them. The kit is not the shell. The shell is the
  repository that holds most of the kit.
- **launcher** — the spawn-time text bound to one seat. It is the prompt an agent
  receives before it reads anything. There is one launcher file per roster row. It
  names the seat, binds it to a capability tier, and makes two acts that seat's
  first acts: read the charter, and run the precheck of §4.1.
- **cure** — the concrete repair of a filed finding, as distinct from the ruling
  that sustains the finding. A cure inside an already-ruled rule applies a rule
  some earlier act decided, and owes no new decision record.
- **limb** — one separable clause of a rule, a finding or a signature. A reviewer
  can sustain, refuse or sign one limb independently of its siblings. A finding
  with two limbs can be half correct.
- **rule** — the norm the organization intends. **guard** — the text or the check
  that enforces a rule. A guard and a rule are two different objects, and §3.5 exists
  to ask one question about the gap.
- **residue** — the part of a control that its instrument does not reach, named in
  the same breath as the control. A residue statement names the seat that holds it
  and the cadence at which that seat discharges it. A residue with no named owner
  is a residue nobody holds. A residue with an owner and no cadence is routed to
  nobody. The word names an accounting duty, and not a disappointment.
- **council** — a convened review round, and not a standing body. Several
  independent reviewers read one artifact at the same time, each from a declared
  seat, and each returns its own committed report. One chair adjudicates them into
  one verdict with conditions.
- **census** — an exhaustive re-derivation of one property over every instance of
  it in the record. One act runs it. The report is a distribution and not an
  example. A census reports what it measured, at what band, over what population.
- **surface** — one of the two places a rule can be checked. The **commit surface**
  is the local gate that refuses before a commit object exists. The
  **pushed-history surface** is the re-check that runs in continuous integration
  over all history. A rule present on one surface and absent from the other is the
  asymmetry defect of §2.4.
- **margin** — a passage carrying the superseded text of a correction and what
  refuted it. The running line carries the current truth, and the margin carries
  the history. A correction that lives only in a margin is not made.
  `docs/PROCESS.md` contains no margins, and this volume contains none.
- **the superseded-text sentinel** — the fixed token that opens every preserved
  superseded claim in `docs/PROCESS-MEMOIR.md`, which is the only file containing any
  instance of it. **annex pointer** — a bracketed reference in backticks, such as
  `[B.9·2]`, pointing into Annex B or Annex C of that same file. This volume carries
  none of either.

These terms are not in the source's dialect list. This volume defines them because it
uses each in one sense only. **posture** — the declaration of how one clause is
enforced (§5.5). **packet** — a versioned file carrying work or a verdict between
seats. **journal** — one append-only reasoning log per seat identity. **charter** —
the versioned file defining one seat, in nine numbered sections (§1.3). **gate** — a
committed checklist a program must fully sign to move to its next stage (§3.7).
**sign-off** — the verification line's verdict on one artifact (§3.8). **seal** — a
committed file holding a frozen prediction (§3.3). **campaign** — one run of
deliberately seeded defects against a test suite (§3.9). **harvest** — the act of
mining a reasoning log for candidate rules (§3.10). **founding range** — the commit
range from a repository's first commit to the commit that completes the machinery
(§1.7). **the shell** — the executable half of the export unit, in a separate
repository (§6.0).

## 1. The shape of the organization

### 1.0 The one-paragraph version

1. A human **sponsor** owns a small fixed set of decisions, and owns nothing else.
2. One **orchestrator** runs as a single long-lived session. It is the only seat
   that starts other agents, and the only seat that writes to version control.
3. Under it sit a few **leads**. Each owns one discipline — specifying, building or
   verifying — and holds a permanent reasoning log.
4. Under the leads sit **workers**. The orchestrator spawns a worker for exactly
   one task packet, and no seat reuses a worker.
5. An **auditor** sits beside all of them. It reports to nobody it can grade, it
   writes only its own findings, and it cannot repair what it finds.
6. Work moves as versioned files, and not as conversation.
7. Every commit carries exactly one agent's work, and that agent's own explanation
   of the work.
8. Nothing normative changes without a numbered decision record and a signature
   from a seat that did not write the change.

**Status.** These claims hold at six strengths.

- Sole spawner: a **substrate fact**. The runtime enforces it (§1.1, Annex A.1).
- Sole committer: **review-enforced**. No instrument in this repository binds who
  runs the version-control tool, and the audit that measured this claim graded its
  unqualified form **false** (§1.1).
- The auditor's write restriction: a script refuses a violation.
- Work-to-explanation coupling: a script refuses a violation, and continuous
  integration re-checks it.
- One agent per commit: **review-enforced** (§2.1).
- The countersignature on a normative change: **review-enforced**. The source's own
  first edition is the counterexample, because it landed with one seat, no work
  order and no signature.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.0.

### 1.1 Why one seat holds both monopolies

1. The orchestrator is the **sole spawner**. No other agent starts an agent.
2. The orchestrator is the **sole committer**. No other agent runs a commit or a
   push.
3. The orchestrator allocates each packet number at the commit that first carries
   the packet.
4. A merge into the trunk must introduce no content of its own. Its tree must equal
   the tree of one of its parents.
5. A merge with more than two parents is refused.
6. Work lands on one working branch. No seat rebases pushed history. No seat
   force-pushes.

**Status, limb by limb.**

- **The spawning monopoly is a substrate fact.** In this runtime a spawned agent
  cannot spawn. Nothing in the repository enforces it, because there is no act to
  refuse (Annex A.1).
- **The committing monopoly is a rule the seats keep.** No instrument binds who
  runs the version-control tool. What the machinery holds is narrower and real: no
  seat can commit outside its own write scope, whoever runs the tool.
- **Rule 3 is review-enforced.** No instrument allocates a packet number or refuses
  a duplicate one. What makes the numbering, the ordering and the attribution of the
  whole record enforceable is that one seat commits, so allocation sits under one
  authority.
- **Rules 4 and 5 are machine-checked**, on the pushed-history surface.
- **Rule 6 is held by no script anywhere.** The hosting platform's branch
  protection holds it, and nothing else does (§2.6, Annex A.5).
- **The never-merged property of the marked campaign references is a measured
  fact**, re-checked reference by reference by an independent seat. It is not a
  mechanical guarantee (§3.9).
- **The orchestrator's own correct behaviour is the one thing the machinery cannot
  check.** Every other seat is bounded by a write scope, and its scope is
  everything.

**Failure.** Uncontrolled fan-out leaves no record of who commissioned what, and a
control that cannot name the party it binds cannot be applied. Interleaved
histories let two seats commit versions of one file in an order neither chose.

**The residue of the review-enforced tier, summed.** Each local claim in the source
is true. The sum is a different fact from any of its parts. The figures are the
source's, measured from ratification on 2026-08-01.

| Compensating control | How often it has operated since ratification |

| Audit of recorded reading (*Inputs* sampling) | **Once**, at ratification. Not once over the build phase. |
| Narrative sampling of entries for vacuity | **Once**, at ratification. |
| Attribution splitting for the unscoped seat | **Once**, at ratification. |
| Relay-fidelity spot-check on protected classes | **Never.** Two receiving-seat checks fired instead, which is a different control. |
| The escape ledger | **Never instantiated.** The file has never existed. |

**Status of the cadence.** Every interval in that table lives in the auditor's
charter, and the orchestrator spawns the auditor. **A cadence written into a
charter is owned by whoever spawns that seat.** The source does not enact a cadence
duty. It routes the duty as an amendment candidate, and until that amendment lands
the honest posture of the whole tier is the table above.

### 1.2 The seats, by function

1. **The sponsor** is a human, owns the short fixed list of §4.7, and reads one
   status file to learn whether anything needs them.
2. **The orchestrator** plans, routes, spawns, commits and escalates. It writes the
   program-state file, and applies clerical edits to files whose authority lives
   elsewhere. It never authors the evidence it is asked to judge.
3. **The specification lead** owns the specifications, the numbered requirements,
   the decision records and all documentation. It adjudicates interface disputes,
   and its ruling takes the form of a specification diff plus a decision record.
4. **The implementation lead** owns everything that ships as the product artifact,
   reviews worker output in its own line, and never writes the tests that grade
   that line.
5. **The verification lead** owns the tests, the reference models, the replays and
   the measurements, and issues the sign-off packets of §3.8. It derives every test
   from the specification, and never from the implementation.
6. **Workers** are spawned per packet, receive exactly the context that packet
   carries, and return exactly what it asks for.
7. **The auditor** is independent. It audits every seat, the orchestrator included.
   It writes only its own reports, cannot repair what it finds, and its verdicts
   reach the sponsor unedited.

**Status.** A script refuses a commit in which the implementation line stages a
test. A script refuses a commit in which the auditor stages anything but its own
reports. **No script reads** the clause barring the orchestrator from authoring the
evidence it judges, or the clause barring the verification lead from deriving a
test from the implementation. See §1.4(c) for what carries the second clause.

**Workers share one journal per role.** The spawning seat mints a **spawn short-id**
into each worker prompt, and the worker copies it into its entry. That short-id is
what preserves attribution inside a shared log. The journal's identity is the role,
and not the spawn.

**The minimum legal seat set.** The separations of §1.4 decide which functions may
share a seat. The answer is **four internal seats and one external human**.

| Fusion | Legal? | Which separation decides it |
|---|---|---|
| building with verifying | **Never.** | (b), and (c) makes it unrecoverable. |
| auditing with anything at all | **Never.** | (a) with (d). This seat's isolation is total. |
| proposing with accepting one normative change | **Never**, per change. | (a). This constrains the act, and not the roster. |
| specifying with verifying | **No.** | (a). A fused seat has no acceptor for the specification. |
| specifying with routing and committing | **Yes, at a stated price.** | Permitted by (a) to (e). Write the price into that seat's charter. |
| a lead with its own workers | **Yes.** | No separation mentions worker roles. |
| any seat with the sponsor | **Never.** | §1.7. The ratifying party sits outside the roster by construction. |

**Status.** This derivation is review-enforced, and no instrument reads a roster.
Above the floor, the count of building and verifying seats follows the shape of the
work, and the count of worker roles follows the budget.

**The capability-tier invariant.** **The reviewing tier is at least the producing
tier.** A seat may be graded by an equal or a stronger reasoner, and never by a
weaker one. The launcher definitions bind the tier per seat, and four other
artifacts restate it (Annex A.8). **No script reads a tier.**

**Failure.** A weaker reviewer cannot see the defect the review was convened for,
and it fails silently, because a weaker reviewer returns *accept* fluently.

**Two further rules, both review-enforced.** **Contingent seats are chartered
before they are activated**: write the seat into the roster now, give it a charter
and a scope row, and leave it dormant with its activation trigger named as a stated
event. **Onboarding a seat is one act, and not four**: the charter, the scope-table
row, the launcher text and the seeded empty log land together in one commit, and
every intermediate state of a split is a defect.

**Failure.** A seat under schedule pressure absorbs an adjacent duty because doing
it is faster than routing it, and the absorbed duty is almost always a check on the
absorbing seat.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.2.

### 1.3 Charters — a role is a document, and not an understanding

1. Each seat has a **charter**: a versioned file with nine numbered sections. Every
   charter in this program has the same nine.
2. An agent's first mandatory action on every spawn is to read its own charter and
   the shared rules.
3. A charter changes only by the amendment procedure of §2.7. An instruction does
   not change a charter.

**The nine sections.** **Identity** (name, capability tier, who it reports to, who
spawns it, journal path, write scope). **Mission**, in one paragraph.
**Responsibilities**, each with the artifact it lands. **Interfaces**, one row per
counterpart. **Inputs, outputs, definition of done. Evaluation criteria**,
falsifiable as percentages or counts. **Escalation rules**, naming which classes go
up. **Journaling and commit obligations**, including the seat's harvest duty.
**Context and references**, with the read restrictions stated as unenforceable
where they are.

**Status.** Every launcher prompt carries the charter-reading obligation. **No
instrument verifies that the read happened.** The *Inputs* section of the resulting
entry is the only record of it, and the sampling of that section is owed (§1.4(c)).
The charter directory sits outside every seat's write scope except the
orchestrator's, so no seat can amend its own charter, and that narrower property is
machine-checked. **No script checks that a decision record accompanies a charter
amendment.**

**Failure.** An agent that does not know its boundaries invents them at the moment
of maximum inconvenience, and a role that exists only in somebody's head cannot be
audited, because no artifact states what compliance would have looked like.

**Status of the founding charter review.** Three independent reviewers attacked the
charter set before ratification, on **2026-08-01**, from three lenses: role
coherence, enforceability, and readability by a non-specialist sponsor. They
returned **twenty-six findings, one of them critical**, and the program accepted
all of them. **This act happened once. Nothing obliges the next one.** Two caveats
ride the exhibit. The review happened before the repository existed, so **its
evidence does not survive in version control**. The severity split as first
published was **wrong**, and its own author corrected it in place. If you run this
review, run it into committed artifacts.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.3.

### 1.4 Separation of duties — five separations

**(a) No seat reviews its own work.** A seat that did not produce an output accepts
that output. Where the producing seat is the only competent reviewer, split the
review by question: the competent seat answers *is the substance correct?*, and a
second seat answers *is the guard exactly the rule, or is it wider?* The second
question is answerable by the party the guard will bind, and its honest answer can
be *no*.

| Output | Accepted by | In what artifact |
|---|---|---|
| A worker's deliverable | its commissioning lead | a verdict in the packet's return log |
| A lead's ordinary output | the seat the output constrains or measures, on the split-by-question rule | a countersignature (§3.5) in the constrained seat's own log |
| A normative change | a seat that did not write it, and never the proposing seat | a numbered decision record with a separate acceptance act |
| The verification line's verdict | nobody inside the line | a sign-off packet (§3.8) |
| The auditor's findings | nobody — the owner dispositions them, and the auditor re-verifies | the auditor's own report, and the owner's cure |
| The orchestrator's own work | the auditor | an audit report |

**Status.** Separation (a) and its routing table are review-enforced throughout. No
script reads an acceptance.

**What "normative" means.** A change is normative when it moves what a rule
requires, what a mark means, or who is bound. It is one of exactly four classes.

1. The constitution, any charter, or an enforcement script.
2. A **reading rule** — what a figure, a signature, a column or a piece of evidence
   means.
3. The **posture** of any clause, on either surface.
4. The source document's own central claim — what the artifact warrants, and to whom.

**Kept long.** These four are **not** normative and owe no decision record and no
countersignature: a figure, a scope, a schedule, and a cure inside an already-ruled
rule. A specification diff is normative only where it moves the reading-rule class,
and it is ordinary work where it moves a value the requirement already ranges over.

**Failure of (a).** An author reads their own artifact for what they meant, and a
reviewer reads it for what it says. That gap is where nearly every defect in this
record lived.

**(b) Builders never write the tests that gate their own artifacts.** The
implementation line cannot write in the test tree. The verification line cannot
write in the implementation tree. **Status:** a script refuses this at commit time,
continuous integration refuses it again, and an audit probed it in both directions
on both surfaces.

**Failure of (b).** Under time pressure a builder repairs the failing check instead
of the failing artifact. The boundary removes the option, and not the temptation.

**(c) The test author is blinded from the implementation.** Give the test author
the specification, and not the code. The task packet omits the implementation
source on purpose, and records that omission as a property of the packet.

**Status.** **File permissions cannot enforce this.** Nothing in this agent runtime
can deny read access to a path (Annex A.4). Three things carry it: the prompt
content, the packet content, and an audit of the *Inputs* section of each reasoning
log. The first two are real artifacts. **The audit of *Inputs* is owed**, and
§1.1's table records that it has operated once.

**Failure of (c).** A suite that encodes what the code does is perfectly green and
completely worthless: it fails if the implementation changes, and passes if the
implementation is wrong.

**(d) The party a measurement grades is not that measurement's check.** Where a
rule creates a check on a seat, route the check to some other seat. If the natural
reviewer of an artifact is the party the artifact grades, that routing is a defect.

**Status.** **The source states no posture for this separation**, and this volume
states none for it. What the source does stamp is the **exhibit**, and it stamps it
**unanchored**: the audit could not locate that episode in the committed record, so
the source keeps the exhibit, marks it, and records that it owes an anchor or a
removal. **The rule is independently anchored**, because the constitution codifies
the same shape and it recurs as the first disguise in §5.7. **An unanchored exhibit
is a different defect from an unanchored rule**, and collapsing the two is how good
rules get deleted with their bad exhibits.

**(e) The thing that grades is itself graded, against something the organization
did not write.** A reference model may not judge an artifact until somebody has
shown that the model agrees with an **external anchor**: an independent
implementation of the same specification, obtained from outside the organization
that produced both the artifact and the model. Establish the agreement per stimulus
class. State what the anchor does not cover in the same place as what it does
cover. Commit the agreement **before** the model issues its first verdict.

**Status.** Review-enforced. The rule is constitutional, it is a named precondition
in the verification lead's charter, and it is a numbered criterion of the one
sign-off in the record. **No script reads any of the three carriers.**

**Failure of (e).** A model and an artifact built inside one organization, from one
specification, by parties who read it the same way, agree exactly where both are
wrong, and the apparatus reports that agreement as a pass. This is the one
separation a schedule can defeat, because an external anchor is something you must
obtain.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.4, for the anonymized patterns and the sign-off
exhibit.

### 1.5 The auditor is a different kind of seat

The auditor is not a stricter reviewer. Five properties make it structurally
distinct, and each is a control.

1. **It writes only its own reports** — not the specifications it audits, not the
   tests, not the artifacts, not the shared task packets.
2. **It never repairs what it finds.** It routes a finding to the owner.
3. **It audits the orchestrator too**, including the seat that spawns it.
4. **It re-executes evidence.** A claim in an evidence section must reproduce at
   that commit, and the auditor re-runs samples.
5. **It owns the ledger of what verification missed.** Every divergence discovered
   **after** a sign-off passes is recorded in a standing ledger in the auditor's own
   report directory, and not by the line whose sign-off missed it. Each entry names
   the sign-off that let the divergence through, the event that surfaced it, and the
   owning line's own account of the root cause.

**Status, item by item.**

- Items 1 and 2: one script holds both. The auditor cannot stage the artifact it
  would repair.
- Item 3: this happened **once, on 2026-08-01**. Nothing obliges the next one.
- Item 4: **stated as a duty, and not as a running practice.** Sampling has been
  performed **twice** in the program's life, and the whole build phase lies between
  the two instances.
- Item 5: **review-enforced.** The auditor's charter names the ledger as a mandatory
  artifact and a checked item of its definition of done. No script reads it.
  **Sample size: zero. The ledger has never existed.** A search of the whole history
  for the path, and for any file of that name, returns nothing.

**Read item 5 exactly.** The source describes a **designed** control with a named
owner, a named artifact and named fields, and **no instance**. A reader who cannot
tell *no escapes yet* from *ledger running* has been told nothing. **Two readings
are available and this program cannot presently distinguish them.** Either
verification has missed nothing since ratification, or nothing has been looking.

**The auditor authors the defect manifests of §3.9.** A manifest **is** one of the
auditor's own reports, authored into its own report directory under its own
identity, inside the write scope item 1 enforces.

**Transcription of the auditor's verdicts.** The auditor records its verdict on a
packet in its own committed report, and the orchestrator transcribes it into the
packet under its own identity. The authority lives in the auditor's artifact.
**Status:** the prohibition half is machine-checked, and **the transcription duty is
a convention**. A transcription nobody makes leaves the verdict live in a report
nobody routed.

**What the auditor's charter carries.** Five mechanisms in it are domain-free and
load-bearing. **Cadenced duties, and not standing intentions** — a duty with no
interval is the residue §1.1 describes. **The escape ledger as a named owned
artifact**, with the fields each entry must carry. **The canary interface, stated as
an information rule**: *I am told nothing in advance, and there is no other direct
contact with the sponsor.* **No direct interface with workers** — a finding about a
worker's output goes to that worker's lead. **Evaluation criteria written as
falsifiable percentages**, because a criterion nobody can fail grades nobody.

**The sponsor's canary power.** The sponsor may deliberately plant a process
violation, and tells nobody in advance. A missed canary is a critical finding
against the auditor. Once the auditor catches an instance, that instance becomes a
finding like any other. **Status:** review-enforced. **No instance is announced in
advance, and none is discoverable by construction before somebody catches it.**

**Failure.** After the fact, no reader can distinguish *this was always compliant*
from *the auditor made this compliant*. And a silent auditor and a clean program
produce identical output, so the canary is the only instrument that distinguishes
them.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.5.

### 1.6 Agents are stateless, and the repository is the memory

Every agent starts each spawn with no memory of the last spawn. Continuity lives entirely in committed files.

1. **Charters** say who you are.
2. **Reasoning logs** say what was done, and why.
3. **Packets** say what is owed, and to whom.
4. **The program-state file** holds the live picture.
5. **The roster file** holds one row per seat.
6. **A journal index** holds one row per log.

**Status of the journal index.** The design calls it a boundary-refreshed
navigation aid. **In this record, one act wrote it at the founding, and no act has
refreshed it since.**

**The roster file has six columns**, and §1.6 of the source is the single authority
on them: Seat, Tier, Reports to, Charter, Journal, Status. **The roster carries no
scope column.** A seat's write scope is written into that seat's charter section 1,
and **the policy module's scope table is the charters' scope fields made
executable**. The chain runs roster, then charters, then policy module.

**The recovery procedure.** A fresh orchestrator reads four things in order: the
program-state file, the shared rules, the roster file, and the log tails of any
seat with open work.

**Status of the recovery drill.** The constitution schedules a drill in the future
tense: kill the orchestrator mid-program, and rehydrate on purpose. **The drill has
never run.** No log, gate or board row in the record contains one. **The recovery
procedure is therefore currently a hypothesis, and the claim that the organization
survives the loss of its orchestrator may not presently be made.**

**Failure.** A program whose real state lives in one session's context window dies
with that session, and it dies silently, because the replacement starts confidently
from a partial picture.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.6, for the two facsimiles.

### 1.7 Genesis — the bootstrap paradox

Every rule in the source is enforced by a seat some earlier act created. Run that
backwards and it terminates in an act no rule authorized: the first seat writes the
constitution that binds it, grants itself its own scope, and commits under a
protocol that has nobody to check it.

**The paradox cannot be dissolved. Pay it down in four acts, in this order.**

1. **Found the organization, and mark the founding as self-signed.** Admit that the
   founding commits are one seat's work. **Do not defer the machinery.** Install the
   commit rules, the append-only journals, the scope table and the
   continuous-integration re-check **before the first real work order**.
2. **Launder the roles through adversarial review, before anyone works under
   them.** Three independent reviewers, three lenses: role coherence,
   enforceability, and readability by the non-specialist who will sponsor it.
3. **Have the sponsor ratify the organization.** The organization cannot vote
   itself legitimate. This is the act no seat inside can perform for itself.
4. **Retro-audit the whole founding range, as the auditor's first act.** The auditor
   declares its own weakness in the report: *I witnessed none of this.*

**The founding range, defined once.** It is the commit range from a repository's
first commit to the commit that completes the machinery — in §6.2's order, acts 1a
and 1b together. Four properties bind it. Name it in a committed founding declaration carrying its
commit count as data. Keep it minimal. Declare it self-signed in the founding gate's own
row. Examine it by name in act 4's retro-audit, which states how many commits it found
outside the gate.

**Status.** Act 1 was performed **once, on 2026-08-01**. Act 4's retro-audit
returned **seventeen findings, one of them critical**, and the gate blocked on that
finding until a decision record dispositioned it and the same seat re-verified the
disposition in a second report.

**The gate is the artifact.** One checklist carries every item, and a journal-entry
reference signs each one, including the two items the sponsor owns and the item
that closes only on the auditor's re-verification. The first real work order issues
only when every row is signed.

**Failure.** An organization's earliest and most consequential commits are the only
commits no control ever touched. The defence is that no control existed yet, and
that defence is true and is exactly the problem.

**What this program's own genesis still owes.** The adversarial review's evidence
does not survive in version control. The retro-audit's severity tally was published
wrong and corrected in place by its author. **The recovery drill has never run.**

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.7.

### 1.8 Phase, milestone, and when history moves to the trunk

1. A **milestone** is the planning unit: one row of the program-state file's
   roadmap, a named, scoped, sequentially numbered body of work with a status.
2. A **phase** is a milestone that carries a full gate triple. Not every milestone
   is a phase. A milestone becomes a phase at the point where it produces artifacts
   that get specified, built, verified and accepted, and it then takes the three
   gates of §3.7.
3. **The trunk-merge trigger** is a milestone boundary, and a milestone boundary is
   the landing of that milestone's last gate. For a phase that is the
   phase-acceptance gate. For a non-phase milestone it is whatever gate row the
   roadmap named as closing it.
4. The merge is trivial by the refusal in §2.6, so **the merge is a publication act,
   and never an integration act**.

**Status.** These definitions are review-enforced and are a planning convention. The
only mechanical thing near them is the merge-triviality refusal of §2.6, which does
not know what a milestone is.

**What transfers.** The requirement to fix a parameterization transfers. This
program's parameterization does not. Pick your two words, write down which of your
milestones are phases, and **state the merge trigger as an event in the record — a
gate row landing — and not as a cadence or a judgement.**

**Residue.** Nothing mechanical reads the roadmap, so a program that merges without
the gate row has broken a convention and no check fires. The instrument that
notices is the gate's own open-items table.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §1.8.

## 2. The constitution and its enforcement

One shared rules document — the **protocol** — binds every agent, and every agent
reads it before acting. It defines how work moves, how reasoning is preserved, and
how the rules are mechanically enforced. Only the amendment procedure of §2.7
changes it, and only the orchestrator commits it.

**Status.** The reading obligation is review-enforced. The amendment procedure is a
procedure no script tests. The committing restriction is scope-enforced, and it
holds.

**Two properties are non-negotiable.** **Traceability**: for any two commits, the
diff between them shows the change and, adjacent in the same diff, the responsible
agent's own explanation of it, and a script refuses a commit that breaks the
pairing. **Independence**: the party that built a thing never grades the
verification of that thing, and nobody the auditor audits grades the auditor.

### 2.1 One agent per commit, coupled to a journal entry

1. Every commit carries exactly one agent's work.
2. Every commit must also carry a pure end-of-file append to that agent's own
   reasoning log, containing a new entry.
3. A commit that carries work without an entry is refused.
4. A commit that carries an entry without work is legal only when a named trailer
   marks it, and the entry's file list must be the empty marker.
5. The commit message ends in fixed metadata: the agent's name, the work-order
   identifier or `none`, and the entry identifier.

**Status.** Rules 3, 4 and 5 are machine-checked. That metadata makes an agent's
whole thread of work recoverable with one log query.

**The trailer keys are protected.** Three keys are always present, and the fourth
appears only on a journal-only commit. No author may supply them as extra
trailers, duplicate them, or shadow them with a later line. The commit gate
refuses, and the pushed-history re-check refuses again. The empty file-list marker
is the literal line `- (none)`. **Reason:** an author who can mint a second
`Agent:` line can choose which one a reader believes.

**How a commit actually happens.** There is one working tree and one index, so
"each agent stages only inside its scope" is not a version-control feature.

1. The acting seat edits files and appends its journal entry in the working tree.
   It runs no version-control write command of its own.
2. The acting seat returns and names the exact set of paths it touched. Its journal
   entry declares the same set.
3. The orchestrator stages that set and invokes the commit script **with the acting
   seat's identity as a parameter**. The identity is an argument, and not an
   inference from the diff.
4. The script evaluates every rule against that identity: every staged non-journal
   path sits inside that seat's scope, the staged journal is that seat's own and is
   a pure append, the entry identifier is exactly one past the chain's last, and the
   declared file list set-equals the staged paths. Any failure aborts before a
   commit object exists.
5. On success the script writes the commit with the fixed trailers, including the
   identity it was given.

**Two consequences.** The committer **asserts** the identity and the scope table
checks it, and nobody proves it, so a mis-asserted identity is refused the moment it
touches a path outside the asserted seat's scope. The residue is a committer that
asserts an identity whose scope covers the paths anyway, and that residue belongs to
the orchestrator. Serialization is required and not incidental, because one tree and
one index means two seats cannot both be mid-edit in that tree.

**The scopes are not disjoint.** In the audited scope table, four grants overlap. The packet directory goes to
three leads and all four worker roles. The implementation tree goes to a lead
and its worker. The test tree goes to a lead and two worker roles. The tools
tree goes to a lead and one worker.

**What is actually true.**

1. **One journal append per commit is the mechanical invariant.** The machinery
   holds this in every case, for every seat.
2. **One agent per commit is emergent only where two seats' scopes are disjoint.**
   That is a property of your scope table, and not of the rule. Compute it:
   intersect the scopes of every pair of seats, and every non-empty intersection is
   a pair for which attribution is review-enforced.
3. **For the seat whose scope is everything, splitting is audit-enforced.** That
   audit is presently owed. It has been performed once, at ratification.

**Routed, and not applied.** The same false premise sits in the constitution's own
honesty note on this rule. Repairing it there is an amendment: it is another seat's
file, and it needs a numbered decision record. The source records it as an
amendment candidate and does not touch it. **Discovering that a document inherited
its error from a source does not authorize the reader of the source to edit the
source.**

**Failure.** Reasoning lost between the decision and the diff leaves a later
reviewer two options — re-derive the decision, or assume it was deliberate — and
both are wrong roughly as often as they are right. A second failure is attribution
collapse: when one commit contains two agents' work, no review verdict can be aimed.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.1, for the trailer facsimile.

### 2.2 Journals are append-only, and a journal is a chain

1. Each agent has one reasoning log. It begins with a frozen header and grows only
   by whole entries appended at the end.
2. No act edits anything above the last byte.
3. No agent writes another agent's journal. The one exception is the creation of an
   empty, entry-free volume for a seat that has none.
4. A seed of that kind is not a step on its own. It is one of the four things §1.2
   requires to land together when a seat is created.
5. The commit gate verifies the append byte-wise: the version at the previous commit
   must be a prefix of the version being committed.
6. Journal deletion and journal renaming are refused outright.
7. Corrections append. A later entry that names an earlier mistake corrects it, and
   no act edits the earlier entry.

**Status.** Rules 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are machine-checked. The same discipline extends
to any frozen record: a seat marks a superseded row, and does not rewrite it.

**Failure.** A record that can be tidied is not evidence, because if any past
prediction can be improved after the result is known, then no prediction in the
file can be trusted, including the honest ones.

**Volumes and the chain.** Journals grow without bound, and a file that exceeds
what a reader can load in one piece stops being read at all. The remedy is rotation
into a new volume file, and rotation is exactly where a per-file append-only
guarantee breaks, because a per-file check cannot see a volume somebody dropped.

1. A journal is therefore a **chain**.
2. Each new volume carries, in its frozen header, the previous volume's path, its
   SHA-256 digest and its byte count.
3. The active volume is the highest-numbered volume.
4. Every earlier volume is frozen, and any change to an earlier volume breaks the
   successor's recorded digest.

**Status.** The chain rule is machine-checked. Within the header block the path and
the digest are checked, and the volume number and the continuation identifier are
checked. **The byte count is checked by nothing.** It is an author's assertion
inside a list of verified fields. The source keeps it because it is cheap to read,
and marks it because an unmarked assertion inside a verified block borrows that
block's authority.

**Where the chain is verified, by limb.** On the **commit surface** the gate
verifies the chain headers on a rotation commit — predecessor present and
untouched, declared volume number, back-link path, digest and continuation
identifier correct — and verifies entry-identifier monotonicity across the chain on
every commit. It does not walk every volume on every commit. On the
**pushed-history surface** the re-check walks the whole chain: gapless numbering,
back-link, digest, and contiguous entry identifiers across every seam. A
**standalone chain verifier** performs the same walk from a bare checkout with no
history, which makes the property checkable by a stranger holding only a clone.

**Failure.** A guarantee checked per file rather than per identity. Without the
chain, "this file is append-only" stays true of every file while a whole volume of
history disappears.

**Two size thresholds.** A soft threshold warns and a hard threshold refuses. Both
are anchored to a measured substrate fact: the largest file the agents' own reading
tool returns in one call. The numbers and their anchor are in Annex A.3.

**Status of the two surfaces.** Both evaluate the same predicate, with different
postures, by design. The commit gate **refuses** an append past the hard threshold.
The pushed-history re-check carries the same size limb as a **warning**: it names
the volume and its byte count, and exits clean. **A bypassed over-threshold append
therefore still lands.** From the landing of that limb onward the push names it
every time, so **the gap closes to *detected and named*, and not to zero.** That
limb landed on **2026-08-12**, and §2.5 states the ground for the asymmetry.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.2, for the rotated-header facsimile.

### 2.3 Path isolation — every seat has a write scope

1. Each agent may stage changes only inside its declared scope.
2. The scope table is part of the constitution.
3. The commit gate checks it, and continuous integration re-checks it.
4. Read access is unrestricted, except where a charter says otherwise.
5. Where a charter restricts a read, the document says plainly that a prompt and an
   audit enforce the restriction, and that the filesystem does not.

**Status.** Rules 1 to 3 are machine-checked. Rule 5 is review-enforced.

**The table's format.** One row per seat. The cell holds a list of allowed path
prefixes, and may carve narrower prefixes out of an allowed prefix. Everything not
listed is denied, because **the check is an allow-list, and an allow-list fails
closed**. Two rows are special: the seat whose cell is "everything", and every
seat's own journal path, which is carved out of the scope test entirely because the
coupling rule of §2.1 forces every commit to touch it.

**The consequences are the separations of §1.4 made physical.** The implementation
line cannot stage tests. The verification line cannot stage the implementation. The
auditor cannot stage anything but its own reports. Each worker is narrowed further
by its packet.

**Status of the last consequence.** The **class** scope is machine-checked. **The
per-packet narrowing is not.** A worker granted its line's tree may stage anywhere
in that tree, and the narrowing to the two files its packet names lives only in the
packet and in the review of the packet.

**Read the arithmetic off your own table before you trust it.** In this program's
audited table, **four path classes are shared by two to four seats**, and for every
such pair one-agent-per-commit is review-enforced, and not emergent.

**Failure.** This control converts "you must not" into "you cannot", and the second
is the only form that survives a hurried round.

**The residue, named.** Read restrictions are not mechanizable in this agent
runtime (Annex A.4). The program compensates with three things it can check: what a
packet contained, what an agent recorded reading, and an auditor that samples both.
**Two of those three are real artifacts. The third is dormant.** State it that way
in your own document, or you will describe a tripod standing on two legs.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.3, for the scope-table facsimile.

### 2.4 Mechanical refusal over advisory warning, and the exceptions

**The default is refusal.** A rule with an opt-out is exercised at exactly the
moment it matters, which is the round where somebody is in a hurry, which is the
round the rule was written for. Apply this test to every proposed control: **a
warning is not a verdict, and its absence is not a clearance.**

Two classes of rule are deliberately not blocking.

**(a) Rules whose predicate is semantic, and not lexical.** Distinguishing a claim
from a quotation of a claim is not a string match, and neither is distinguishing a
genuine relay from a summary. For these rules the program mints no machine rule at
all. The posture is declared **review-enforced**, and the clause says so in its own
text. A script may emit an advisory note, and the clause states in the same breath
that the note's absence proves nothing.

**Status of class (a).** One advisory note of this class went through a decision.
This program **designed that note and then refused it**, on **2026-08-12**, after
tracing its two reachable cases. In the accidental case the files-list rule already
refuses the commit and prints the offending path, so the note is loud where a
refusal already fires. In the legitimate case the entry's body is about the file, so
the predicate is false and the note is silent exactly where the residue lives. **A
note that is loud where a refusal already fires, and silent where the residue
actually is, has no case left to serve.**

**What replaced it.** Not a weaker instrument but a different kind: an **evidence
form** obliging the committing seat to record a byte-check in its own entry, which
converts an unenforceable habit into a claim one pass over history can measure.

**Read the posture exactly.** This clause is no longer **owed**. It is
**deliberately absent by a decided refusal**, which is a stronger statement than an
absence. **The other advisory note this clause's family contemplates — the one the
constitution's withheld-results rule names — is still unbuilt, no script emits it,
and that one remains owed.**

**Failure of class (a).** A mechanical check whose predicate is a proxy for the real
rule produces false convictions, and something worse: a **clearance**. The check
passed, so the reviewer stops looking, and the real rule goes unexamined forever.

**(b) Rules whose subject is testimony, and not structure.** Where the thing being
checked is an agent's own attestation and the record of it is already frozen, a
blocking check makes a legitimate correction impossible. A warning is the correct
instrument there. It is not a weaker version of a refusal. It is the right shape,
because the failure it catches must remain fixable.

**Status of class (b).** The designed class now has two implemented members. Since
**2026-08-12** a drift band compares each entry's header stamp against the time of
the commit that carries it and emits an advisory line. It runs on **both surfaces**,
and it never refuses. The local gate reports one entry, and the history re-check
aggregates per chain, so the roster bounds the line count rather than history.
**The band's two sides are not equally anchored.** The fast side rests on a
measurement, meaning no false positives against known-good behaviour. The slow side
is a symmetric default, and the record itself flags it as unanchored.

**The general principle underneath both classes.** A rule without its check is a
suggestion. **A rule and its check that disagree about what compliance is are worse
than either alone**, because the disagreement is invisible from both sides. Design
every control as a pair, and where the pair cannot exist, write the absence down.

**The corollary.** If a check exists on one surface and not on the other, the
asymmetry is itself the defect. Both surfaces, or neither.

**Status of the corollary.** It was adopted **once, on 2026-08-03**, in the act that
minted it. That act mirrored a large-file gate into the pushed-history re-check and
gave it its own numbered rule and test case. **That same act did not mirror the
journal size threshold, which sits in the same rule family.** That unrepaired
instance stood for nine days and four editions, and closed on **2026-08-12**.

**The instruction that comes with the corollary.** **A corollary adopted without a
sweep of its own kind is a corollary adopted for one case.** If you adopt this rule, the
adopting act should enumerate every check on either surface and dispose of each one, in
that act.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.4.

### 2.5 Continuous integration is the adjudicator

1. Continuous integration re-verifies every rule the commit script enforces.
2. It re-verifies them over the entire pushed history, and not merely over the newly
   pushed range.
3. Every commit tool has a bypass flag, and continuous integration catches a locally
   bypassed check before merge.

**Status.** Rule 3 is machine-checked. Rule 1 as written replaces a claim an audit
found false, and rule 2 states the corrected scope.

**The single exception closed on 2026-08-12.** "Closed" does not mean "identical on
both surfaces".

- **Both surfaces now evaluate the same predicates.** The one rule that ran on the
  commit surface alone — the journal size threshold — now runs on the pushed-history
  surface too, over the same subject: the active volume.
- **The postures differ per limb, deliberately.** The blob gate re-verifies on the
  history surface as a **refusal**, with the journal paths carved out. The journal
  size limb re-verifies there as a **warning**, on the recorded ground in §2.2.
- **The closure bought naming, and not prevention.** A commit made with the local
  hook bypassed still lands its oversized append. It can no longer land it
  unnoticed.

**The general form for an adopter.** Both surfaces must evaluate the same rule.
They need not deliver the same verdict. Where the harm a rule guards is permanent,
refuse on both. Where it is bounded and self-curing, refuse where the act can still
be prevented and name it where it can only be observed. **Write down which of the
two you chose, and why.**

**The quantifier in "the entire pushed history", delimited.** The re-check
adjudicates **the lineage**: the working branch and the trunk, over all of their
history. **It does not adjudicate the marked campaign references of §3.9**, which
are pushed and never merged and sit outside its frame entirely. Two frames
therefore exist. The commit gate binds what passes through it, and it is a wrapper
somebody invokes. The re-check binds what lands in the lineage. **An act that is
neither is bounded by neither.**

**Failure.** A check that runs only where the checked party runs it is a
self-report. And an incremental range check cannot detect a rewrite of history
before the range, which is exactly the attack the append-only property exists to
prevent.

**The one out-of-repository dependency, declared and discharged.** None of this
survives a force push, and the hosting platform's branch protection prevents force
pushes. The machinery cannot make that setting for itself. The program names this
as a one-time sponsor duty on a gate checklist, and states plainly that until
somebody does it the no-rewrite guarantee is convention rather than enforcement.

**Status.** The record closed this twice. The setting was configured at
ratification, on **2026-08-01**, with an empty bypass list, so that the
administrator account the program pushes with cannot override it. Live fire verified
it on both protected branches the same day: an attempted violation actually
bounced, and a journal entry cites it on the gate row. Months of rounds later, the
platform's rules refused a force-push-with-lease on a transient reference in the
middle of an unrelated incident.

**Failure.** An undeclared external dependency inside a control. A guarantee that
silently depends on a setting nobody has verified gets cited for the life of the
program, and has never been true.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.5.

### 2.6 The numbered commit rules

The rule set is small and numbered, and every later argument cites a rule by its
number. The identifiers below are this program's own. Yours may differ. **What may
not differ is that yours exist, are stable, and are what every later argument
cites.**

**Read the table with one note.** Thirteen rows carry eleven numbers. Two
identifiers appear twice, because one rule has two limbs enforced on different
surfaces. The table is ordered for reading, and not by identifier.

| Id | Rule | Function | Enforced where |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R1` | One agent per commit | Split mixed-agent changes into sequential commits. The mechanical invariant is one journal append per commit. | Both surfaces, **as the invariant**. The splitting itself is review-enforced for overlapping scopes and for the unscoped seat (§2.1). |
| `R2` | Coupling | Work must stage a pure append to the responsible agent's journal. A journal-only commit is legal, and a trailer must mark it. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R3` | Append-only | The previous version of the journal must be a byte prefix of the staged version. Journal deletion and renaming are always refused. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R4` | Files-list equality | The entry's declared file list must set-equal the commit's changed paths, excluding the author's own journal. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R5` | Monotonic entry ids | The new entry's number is exactly the previous number plus one, across volumes and not merely within a file. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R6` | Trailers | The fixed metadata block must be present and well formed. No author may shadow or duplicate a protected key. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R7` | Path isolation | Every staged non-journal path must sit in the committing agent's scope. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R8` | Foreign journal seeding only | Another agent's journal may be staged only as a newly created, entry-free file. Modifying an existing one is always refused. | Both surfaces. Machine-checked. |
| `R10` | Journal chain and rotation | Volumes are gapless. Each carries its predecessor's path and digest. Only the active volume may change. | Both surfaces, by limb (§2.2). Machine-checked. |
| `R10` | Journal size thresholds | A soft threshold warns. A hard threshold refuses. Both are anchored to the reader's single-file limit. | **Both surfaces, by limb, since 2026-08-12.** The commit surface **refuses** above the hard threshold. The pushed-history surface evaluates the same predicate over the same subject and **warns**. |
| `R11` | Large-file gate | A staged file over **1,000,000 bytes** is refused, with the journal carved out. The refusal names the sanctioned route. | Both surfaces — the one place the both-surfaces corollary was applied. |
| `R9` | Merge triviality | A merge commit's tree must equal the tree of one of its parents. Merges beyond two parents are rejected outright. | **Pushed-history re-check only.** The commit script does not enforce it. |
| `R9` | One branch, no rebases, no force pushes | Work lands sequentially on one working branch. No act rewrites pushed history. | **No script anywhere.** The hosting platform's branch protection holds it, and nothing else does. |

**The divergence a reader would otherwise inherit as clean.** This program's
constitution enumerates **nine of these eleven numbers**. `R10` and `R11` appear in
no clause of it. The scripts refuse by those two names, an accepted decision record
carries the written constitutional diffs that would add them, and **nobody has
applied the diffs**. So the refusal messages an operator reads cite two rule numbers
the constitution does not contain.

**Status.** The table above is measured against **the scripts**, and every
enforcement cell in it re-executes true. **The constitution is measured against
nothing.** This divergence statement is review-enforced, and it is a statement about
another seat's file.

**Three consequences.** The kit's constitution template is exported with this gap in
it. An adopter running act 1d of §6.2 is being told to do the thing the reference
program itself did not finish. And the general form transfers: **a written-but-
unapplied amendment is indistinguishable, from inside the document that cites it,
from an applied one.** The citation resolves, the decision record exists and is
accepted, and only opening the amended file shows that nothing moved.

**Three things the identifier column makes visible.** Thirteen rows carry eleven
numbers, and a reader meets that at the head of the table. **A row is not a case**:
a self-test grows by cases per amendment and not per row, so any equation between
row count and case count is uncomputable. **One rule has no script to name it at
all** — the branch limb of `R9` — so the self-test's own form cannot be written for
it, and what stands in its place is the live-fire bounce against the platform, cited
by identifier.

**The large-file threshold and its remedy.** The gate refuses any staged file over
**1,000,000 bytes**, with every journal path carved out, because a size refusal
cannot apply to a file `R2` requires in every commit and `R3` requires to continue
its own bytes. **The sanctioned route for large data is a fetch script plus a
checksum manifest.** Commit the manifest. Do not commit the payload. **State your own route in your own refusal message.** The moment a refusal fires
is the moment its remedy is read. **A refusal that names no remedy will be
answered by bypassing the gate**, by the party least able at that moment to
judge whether that is safe.

**The branch topology.** A **working branch** receives every agent commit, in
sequence. A **trunk** receives the working branch only at milestone boundaries
(§1.8), and only through a merge that introduces no content of its own. Both are
force-push protected. **The campaign references of §3.9 are a third population**:
pushed, marked, and never merged into either, and therefore not a violation of "one
working branch", because the rule is about where history accumulates.

**The files-list rule.** Each entry declares the exact set of files its commit
touches, and the check is set equality. An entry cannot claim files it did not
touch, nor silently touch files it did not claim. A deletion counts as a touch. This
rule is machine-checked.

**Failure.** Narrative and diff drift apart while each stays internally consistent,
and reading either one alone shows nothing wrong. This single check binds the
explanation to the change, where the coupling rule only guarantees that *an*
explanation exists.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.6.

### 2.7 The amendment procedure

Any change to the constitution, to a charter, or to an enforcement script requires
**four** things. The first three belong to the constitution. The fourth belongs to
the source document, and binds that document's author only.

1. A **numbered decision record** recording the alternatives considered and the
   rationale.
2. An orchestrator journal entry accepting it.
3. If the change alters enforcement semantics, an updated case in the **enforcement
   self-test** proving the new behaviour.
4. If the change alters enforcement semantics — what a rule refuses, on which
   surface, under what posture — it obliges either a **re-edition** of the source
   document naming the clauses that moved, or a **logged waiver** stating that
   nothing there needs to change, and why. Both are acts with an author and a date.
   Neither may be silent.

**Status.** **No script tests any of the first three.** The coupling rule forces
*an* entry to exist on the amending commit. Nothing checks that the entry accepts
anything, that a decision record accompanies it, or that a test case was added.
Requirement 4 is owned by the specification lead, discharged in that seat's own
reasoning log, and review-enforced. **The record shows the procedure working twice,
and shows its debt: an instrument in force whose constitutional diffs are written
and not applied.**

**The enforcement self-test.** It is a runnable scenario suite. For each numbered
rule it constructs a commit that violates the rule, and asserts that the refusal
happens and names that rule. It makes an amendment falsifiable, because **a rule
whose test case cannot be written is a rule whose predicate nobody has pinned
down**. It lives beside the enforcement scripts, in the same scope, and it grows by
one case per semantic amendment, which is why item 3 is a precondition and not a
follow-up.

**Program-scope parameters.** State each parameter canonically in exactly one place,
and treat every repetition as convenience only. A change updates the canonical
statement and every restatement, and the amending decision record lists the touched
files.

**Reason for requirement 4.** Every falsehood the last two editions removed had one
life cycle: a sentence true when written, a machine that moved underneath it, and no act
obliging anyone to look at the sentence again. **Per-claim editing of a document this
size does not converge.** What converges is binding the document to the event that
invalidates it, and **the waiver limb matters as much as the re-edition limb**, because
a rule dischargeable only by editing a three-thousand-line document will be discharged
by not invoking it.

**Routed, and not applied.** The same binding belongs in the constitution's own
amendment clause, where it would bind every seat. That is an amendment, needing a
numbered decision record and touching a file outside the specification lead's scope.
The source records it as an amendment candidate. **A document that discovers a rule
its own constitution should carry proposes that rule. It does not enact the rule by
writing it down.**

**Failure.** The constitution changes because somebody needed it to, in a round
where nobody was looking at it as a constitution. Writing the alternatives down
is what lets a later reader tell a decision from a default, and requiring the
enforcement test case stops the class of amendment that changes the words and
leaves the machine doing the old thing.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §2.7.

## 3. The artifact grammar

A **packet** is a versioned file carrying work or a verdict between seats. It has
an identifier, a state, a named author and a named addressee. A seat commits it, so
a reader can diff it, cite it and bounce it.

**The taxonomy is closed by a rule, and that rule is a proposal.**

> **The proposed rule.** A new packet type may be minted only by the amendment
> procedure of §2.7: a numbered decision record naming the type, its required
> fields, its relay class and its state vocabulary, accepted by a seat that did not
> propose it. **No seat mints a type by using one.** A form that appears in the
> record without that route is not a new type. It is either an instance of an
> existing type, or an unrouted extension, and naming which is the reviewer's job.

**Status of the proposed rule.** **This rule binds nobody.** It is stated in no
binding artifact of this program. The constitution defines four packet types and
contains no minting clause, no relay-class duty and no state-vocabulary duty. No
decision record mints one, and no charter carries one. **The record's practice is
the inverse of the rule**: every extension form named below was minted by use. The
source carries the rule as a **proposal**, routes it as an amendment candidate, and
states that it binds nobody until that route completes.

**Six forms have a definition.** Four are types the constitution defines. Two are
the source document's own construction. **Read the count as scoped that way, and
not as *six forms are in use*.** The record runs more forms than six.

| Packet | Carries | Written by | Relay class | Where the type is defined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Work order** | A commissioned unit of work: basis, deliverables, definition of done, context handed over, what is out of scope | A lead, or the orchestrator | Summarizable | **Constitutional** |
| **Review verdict** | Accept, or a numbered defect list naming file, line and the clause violated, signed with the reviewer's entry identifier | The reviewing lead | Summarizable | **Constitutional.** Zero instances exist as a file (§3.2). |
| **Sign-off** | The verification line's pass or fail on one artifact, with everything §3.8 lists | The verification lead | **Verbatim** | **Constitutional** |
| **Defect packet** | One defect found after a seat accepted an artifact, routed to its owner | The verification lead | **Verbatim** | **Constitutional** |
| **Sealed prediction** | A frozen expectation, opened only after the evidence exists (§3.3) | Whichever seat will be scored | **Verbatim** | **The source document's construction.** The constitution gives the seal no packet row and no relay class. Its verbatim treatment comes from §4.3's protected-class list. |
| **Finding** | A severity-graded defect claim against an artifact, including one's own (§3.4) | Any seat | **Verbatim** when the auditor files it | **The source document's construction.** The constitution has no finding packet type. |

**Three things the table encodes.** Relay class is a property of the packet **type**,
and not of the round, so it decides in advance whether the routing seat may compress the
packet. A review verdict is a packet type that **has never existed as its own file** in
this record. And **two of the six rows are a document's model of its own program**,
which is legitimate for a description to carry and illegitimate to inherit as law.

**Forms outside the six, named.** Each is an **unrouted extension**, and none was
minted by the route above.

1. A **transit packet** that carried a harvest across an organizational boundary.
   **That form is now record rather than practice.** Somebody executed it exactly
   once, and the mechanism it carried was **retracted on 2026-08-17** (§3.10). The
   one instance keeps its identifier and stays in the packet directory as the record
   of the one delivery. No second one will exist, and its owed routing is
   **discharged by its retirement**.
2. An **adjudication companion** to a work order, holding a ruling too long for a
   return log.
3. A **pre-run reading note**, filed before a round begins, recording what its
   author read.
4. A **campaign packet**, a **defect manifest** and a **campaign brief** (§3.9). The
   manifest doubles as one of the auditor's own reports, which is what seats it.

**Status.** Items 2, 3 and 4 are **live forms with no instrument**, and their
routing is owed. **An unrouted extension can be retired as well as routed**, and a
taxonomy that only knows how to admit forms will carry dead ones forever.

**States outside the five.** The lifecycle of §3.2 is the work order's. The record
also runs **EXECUTED**, **CLOSED**, **RE-ISSUED**, **RE-VERDICT**, **UNSEALED**, and
**letter-suffixed splits** of one commission into parts. An adopter who builds the
five states and then meets the sixth will assume they misread the process. **A state
vocabulary is part of a packet type's definition, and a type whose states are
defined only by usage has no definition.**

**Identifier schemes.** Entry identifiers take the form `J-<seat>-NNNN`. Decision
records take `ADR-NNNN-<slug>`. Findings take `F-<origin>-<n>`. Gate signatures are
carried as entry references. The sole committer allocates each number at first
commit, and a draft in flight carries a placeholder rather than a guess.

**Status of the placeholder.** **This program has no literal placeholder token.**
The drafting seat writes the next free number per prefix, measured at a stated
commit, into the draft's own filename, and declares in its journal entry that the
identifier is provisional until the committer allocates. **That practice is real
and disciplined, and it is not greppable**, because a provisional identifier is
lexically indistinguishable from an allocated one. See Annex A.9(a).

**Dispatch-only rounds.** Everything moving between seats is a versioned file, with
one institutionalized exception. **Four work-order identifiers in this program's
record have no packet file at any commit**, while being live in reasoning-log
headers and in program state. The dispatch text alone carried the commission.

**The honest rule.** Not *everything is a file*, but: **every commission is a file,
or the round says in its own reasoning log that it was dispatch-only, and why.**
That converts an invisible exception into a countable one.

**Failure.** A task, verdict, finding or agreement existing only in a chat has no
author, no version, no diff and no addressee of record, so nobody can review
against it, bounce it or cite it. Conversation is where work is coordinated, and
files are where work exists.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3, for the packet skeleton, the identifier tokens and
the path layout.

### 3.1 The journal entry

The journal entry is the unit of the reasoning record.

1. A header line holds the entry identifier, the timestamp, the work-order
   identifier or `none`, and a one-line title.
2. **Trigger** states who invoked this round, and why.
3. **Inputs** states the exact files, specifications, decision records and prior
   entries read, with paths and, where it matters, commit identifiers.
4. **Reasoning** states the decision narrative: options considered, why the winner
   won, and what was rejected and why.
5. **Actions** states what was done.
6. **Evidence** states exact reproducible commands and their observed results.
7. **Outcome** states the status against the work order's definition of done, and
   where the work was handed.
8. **Open-questions** states escalations and unresolved items, or an explicit
   `none`.
9. **Files-in-this-commit** states the declared file set.

**Two rules on Evidence.** A claim there must reproduce at this commit, and the
auditor re-executes samples. Only two kinds of citation are admissible: a command
runnable from a checkout, and an externally verifiable reference such as a
continuous-integration run identifier and its conclusion. Label anything ephemeral
as ephemeral.

**Status.** **Of the eight sections, exactly one is machine-checked**: the declared
file set. **An entry with no *Reasoning* section at all commits clean.** The other
seven are carried by the entry template, by review, and by audit sampling. **That
sampling is owed**, and §1.1's table records that it has operated once. A reader of
a list of eight required sections would reasonably infer that a missing *Evidence*
section is refused. **It is not.**

**The parser rule.** An entry body may never contain a line beginning `## [J-` at
column zero for its own journal. Quote a prior header indented, or inside a
sentence. The parsers are deliberately simple and count such a line as a new entry.
**A script refuses a violation.**

**Entry identifiers are strictly monotonic per chain**, across volume boundaries and
not merely within a file. A script refuses a violation. The per-chain property is
the one that matters, because rotation is where a per-file guarantee silently
breaks.

**The spawn short-id.** Worker roles share one journal per role rather than per
spawn, so a worker entry carries the spawn short-id in its `task:` field and quotes
it verbatim in *Trigger*. The spawning seat mints that string into the prompt: the
packet identifier plus the spawn timestamp. **Status:** review-enforced. **No script
checks that the short-id in the entry is the one that was minted.**

**Failure of each section.** *Inputs* is the audit evidence for the blinding rules of
§1.4(c). *Reasoning* prevents entries that describe what without why, which the program
classes as its own finding category, named **vacuity**. *Evidence* prevents a confident
claim that reproduces nowhere. *Open-questions* prevents the loss of what an agent
noticed and could not act on.

**Status of the decay exhibit.** An audit re-ran a sample of citations on
2026-08-01 and found a small number false. **They were not fabricated. They were
decayed**: true when written, false at the current state, with nothing in the record
marking the difference. **Decay is not a property of careless records. It is a
property of records cited later than they are written**, which is all of them.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.1, for the entry facsimile.

### 3.2 Work orders, and the loop

1. A **work order** is a versioned packet stating its state, its from and to, the
   specification basis it rests on, the deliverables constrained to the assignee's
   write scope, the definition of done, the exact context handed over, and what is
   explicitly out of scope.
2. It carries a return log, and the participants append to that log.
3. Its lifecycle is a state machine written in the packet header: **DRAFT → ISSUED
   → RETURNED → ACCEPTED or BOUNCED**.
4. A bounced packet carries the numbered defect list and re-issues as a new
   revision.

**What "a new revision" means, literally.** The packet **keeps its identifier and
its file**. The state field records the whole chain rather than the current value.
The revision is a **letter suffix on the round**, and not on the packet's number,
and the verdicts carry that letter so they stay distinguishable. **Every superseded
verdict stays in the file, in place, with the reason it was superseded beside it.**
A bounce chain is the artifact, and not an embarrassment to tidy.

**One collision to know about.** The same letter suffix also marks a **split** of
one commission into parts, so a letter means *revision* on a state line and *part*
in a filename. The record contains both.

**Status of the review verdict.** It has the content of a packet: accept, or a
numbered defect list with file, line and the clause violated, signed with the
reviewer's entry identifier. **In this program's record it has never existed as its
own file.** The constitution defines the type, and not one instance exists in the
tree or in history. The practice is a verdict section appended to the work order's
own return log.

**Two further rules, both review-enforced.** The sole committer allocates packet
numbers at first commit, and drafts in flight use a placeholder. **The context
section is a control, and not a convenience**: blinding is enforced by what a packet
contains, so a packet that must omit something says so, in an explicit section
headed *what you may not read*.

**Failure, in three layers.** *Without the packet:* a task whose scope was never
fixed comes back as something adjacent, with no document to compare against.
*Without the state machine:* nobody can tell whether a returned item is
finished, in review or rejected, and both parties can believe the other holds
it.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.2.

### 3.3 Sealed predictions

**The cast, bound to §1.2's functions.** **The scored seat** is the verification
lead: it owns the suite the campaign measures, and it writes, freezes and scores the
seal. **The seeding seat** is the auditor: it composes the deliberate defects, and
it is the one seat the seal is kept from. **The operating seat** is the
orchestrator: it applies the defects and runs the suite, and authors none of the
evidence it produces.

**The rule that makes this real.** **A seal is a file in the commit that claims it,
or it is not a seal.** A commit may not introduce a claim that a result already
exists and is being withheld from the reader, unless that same commit stages the
artifact holding the withheld result, so the seal appears in that commit's own
declared file list. **A withheld result that is not a committed artifact is not a
seal. It is a claim.**

**Status.** This rule is **deliberately review-enforced**, and the constitution
declares that posture in the clause itself, on the ground that distinguishing a
claim from a quotation of a claim is not a lexical test.

**Three things the rule does not reach.**

1. A **forward commitment** is a promise, redeemed by the later commit that freezes
   the seal. **An unredeemed promise is not excused.** It is adjudicated as *no seal
   existed*, which means the claim it was to support may not be made.
2. A **retrospective reference** to a seal already in history is not a new claim,
   including quoting a seal in order to convict it.
3. **Sealing in the sense of finalizing a decision** withholds nothing, and sits
   outside the rule.

**What "withheld" means here.** The seal is committed in plaintext, in a repository
whose read access is unrestricted, so nothing is hidden from anyone. **The
withholding is temporal, and not cryptographic.** The seal does not prevent anyone
from *reading* the prediction. It prevents the author from *revising* it after the
evidence arrives. **If your substrate cannot protect history (Annex A.5), a
plaintext seal buys you nothing, and you need a different instrument.**

**What a seal file carries.** Its state. The campaign it belongs to. The base state
it was formed against, at that state's own identifier. The seat and entry identifier
that froze it. The **second copy**: the entry identifier of the append-only log
where the discriminating content is restated. **A seal does not carry the commit
that froze it, and cannot**, because the freezing seat never runs the version-control
tool and the commit does not exist until another seat creates it.

**Status of the state field, measured seal by seal.** The source re-derived this by
comparing every seal file's current bytes against its bytes at the commit that
introduced it. **Fourteen seals exist.** **Four — the earliest — flip their state
line** when the campaign opens, and those same four are exactly the seals whose files
differ from their introducing commit. **Ten carry a `FROZEN` state line nobody edited
at all**, and are byte-identical to the day they were frozen. **Somebody scored every
one of those ten**, each with a committed verdict quoting its sealed cells. **Of the
fourteen campaigns in this record that hold a committed seal, somebody scored all
fourteen, and ten of them were scored without one byte of the seal being touched.**
The later practice records the unsealing in the campaign packet's own verdict, and not
in the seal.

**The later form is strictly stronger, and it is the one to build.** A seal with a
designed mutable line is an author-editable field inside a block whose whole value is
that nobody edits it. With a mutable line the check is *the diff is empty but for one
line*, and somebody has to argue about it. Without one the check is **the diff against
the freezing commit is empty, full stop**. **A mechanism that needs the checker to
decide which difference is allowed has given the checker something to be charitable
about.**

**Status of the newest seal.** It pre-committed, in its own frozen text, to never
flipping its state line, and recorded its unsealing in the campaign packet's return
log. When the scoring round arrived holding one authorized flip, **the scoring seat
declined to use it**, on the stated ground that flipping would convert a flat check
into an argued one.

**Byte equality is established by two mechanisms, and neither is a field in the
file.** **A diff against the freezing commit**, which anyone can run, and which the
append-only and no-force-push guarantees make a fixed point. And **the second-copy
discipline**: the frozen content is committed twice, in the seal file and in
substance inside the freezing seat's reasoning-log entry, which is append-only by a
rule the seal file is not covered by. **If either copy is later edited to fit a
result, the other copy exposes the edit.**

**Nothing in the seal file is ever edited.** A wrong prediction is not amended. It
is adjudicated, and it dies on the record. **Status:** review-enforced. The
append-only guarantee covers journals, and not packets. In the current practice the
discipline is total: **nobody reopened the ten later seals for writing at all**, and
all ten, each of them now scored, record their unsealing in the campaign packet's
verdict.

**Four questions, answered in one place.**

1. **Author.** The seat that will be scored writes and freezes the seal.
2. **Freeze point.** Before any defect **diff** exists. Not "before any evidence
   existed": the weaker phrase permits a seal frozen after the defects are rendered
   and before they are run, which is precisely the state in which an author can fit
   a prediction to a patch the author has read.
3. **Blind direction.** Against the **seeder**. Neither the seal nor any part of it
   reaches the seeding seat until every diff is committed, and the routing seat
   carries the fact of the seal and none of its content. **It is not a blind against
   the seat that froze the seal.**
4. **Scorer.** The same seat that froze it. The record decides this: every seal was
   frozen by that seat, every scored campaign was scored by it, and **no instance
   was marked at the time as a deviation.** An earlier edition stated the opposite
   rule, and the source withdrew that rule as false. **A rule that has never once
   been kept is not a rule with a deviation. It is a description of a different
   organization.**

**What actually holds a self-scored campaign honest.** Four constraints, and a
stranger can check every one.

1. **The prediction is immutable, and its immutability is checkable.** The check is
   a diff against the freezing commit, expected **empty**, with no line exempted.
2. **It is committed twice**, and either copy convicts an edit to the other.
3. **The scorer's inputs are other seats' artifacts.** The defects are the
   independent seat's, authored blind. The runs are the operating seat's. The verdict
   quotes verbatim tool output at a run identifier, and not a summary.
4. **The discriminating content is the expected message text, and not the row set.**
   A seal predicting only *these units fail* is one almost any defect satisfies.

**The residue, named.** **Self-scoring is §5.7's sixth disguise** — the measurement
that grades the measurer. The source lists that disguise in its museum and denied it
in this section. **Self-scoring operates in the open, with the four constraints
above and nothing else.** What the record shows runs both ways. The first campaign's two findings were
both corrections to the scorer's own frozen predictions, and not to the suite.
One verdict convicted the stated reason inside its own seal while the prediction
stood. Later campaigns record their own sealed cells as falsified in their
headline.

**Rule.** Admissions against interest are evidence that self-scoring is being done
honestly. **They are not a separation, and a document may not offer them as one.**
The separation that is real here is the seeder's: the party composing the defects
neither predicts, nor scores, nor can repair.

**The repair that is available, stated as an option and not as law.** The blind's
purpose is discharged the moment every diff is committed, after which the independent
seat could score the campaign against the frozen seal with no loss of blinding. That
would convert the residue into a separation. It costs a round of an already
oversubscribed seat, and **it is another seat's duty to accept or refuse it.** The
source records it as an option and not as a recommendation, and enacts nothing.

**Failure.** Without a prediction frozen before the evidence, every outcome is
consistent with competence, and a suite that caught the defect is indistinguishable
from one whose output somebody read charitably afterwards. **The rule makes seals
countable, and not good**: a vacuous prediction satisfies the file requirement, and
adjudication catches it, because a prediction that discriminates nothing cannot be
scored.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.3, for the seal facsimile.

### 3.4 Findings

1. A **finding** is a written, severity-graded defect claim against an artifact.
2. Any seat may file one, including against its own work.
3. The scale has **four** grades: critical, major, minor, and a non-binding **note**.
4. The grading is part of the filing, and not of the response.
5. A critical finding from the auditor reaches the sponsor verbatim, as its own
   escalation class (§4.6).
6. A finding is not closed by being contradicted. It closes when its owner repairs
   it or rules on it with grounds, and the ruling is itself an artifact.
7. A finding sustained but cured elsewhere is recorded with its **route**: the owner
   and the trigger.

**Reason for the fourth grade.** The note is where a filer puts an observation it
wants on the record and does not want routed as a defect. Without it, those
observations either inflate into minors or vanish.

**Status.** This program's founding audit returned 1 critical, 7 major, 7 minor and
2 note, by the report's own corrected figures. **Rule 5 was exercised once, on
2026-08-01**, at the founding gate, where a critical finding blocked the gate until
a decision record dispositioned it and the auditor re-verified the disposition.

**Findings against one's own seat are ordinary.** The record contains a lead filing
a major finding against a value its own packet specified, a seat offering a rule
that raised the standard its own artifacts would be held to, and a seat correcting a
routing so that its own duties grew. **The process treats these as the normal case,
and not as heroism.**

**The direction test.** Check which way each of a finding's separable clauses moves
the filer's own exposure. Distrust a filing whose every limb reduces its filer's
obligations. A filing that **widens** its filer's exposure in one limb while
narrowing it in another has probably been reasoned about rather than motivated.
**The test is about limbs, and a finding is not limited to two of them.**

**Failure.** The natural equilibrium is that only cheap findings against other
parties get filed, because those cost the filer nothing. The result looks like a
healthy review culture and is one where nothing that matters is ever examined.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.4.

### 3.5 Countersignatures

1. A seat that did not write a normative change signs that change.
2. **The signature is narrow.** The signer states exactly what it checked and
   exactly what it did not check.
3. **The signature is asked of the constrained party** — the seat the clause
   constrains, which will live inside the guard.
4. The signer's question is **"is the guard exactly the rule, or is it wider than
   the rule?"**, and not "is this correct?".
5. **Refusal is a legitimate outcome.** A countersignature that cannot be refused is
   a formality.
6. A refusal or a narrowing is a contest, and a contest has a route: before
   acceptance the author redrafts the clause, and after acceptance the clause goes
   back through the amendment procedure.

**Status.** Rule 1 is review-enforced. The record practises it for every
constitutional amendment. **The record does not practise it for the source
document**, whose first edition asserted the rule and landed unsigned. **The
current edition owes signatures too, and it names them**: each seat whose
discipline the source describes confirms the description of its own discipline.
The source lists that owed traffic in its Annex B, which lives in
`docs/PROCESS-MEMOIR.md`.

**Where a countersignature physically lives.** It is written twice, in two places,
by two different acts, and the duplication is the mechanism. **The authority is a
sentence in the signer's own reasoning log**, inside a normal entry, saying in terms
that it signs the named thing at the named state, which §2.2 makes append-only and
therefore un-withdrawable. **The routed copy is a verdict section appended to the
commissioning packet's return log**, quoting that sentence and carrying the per-limb
verdicts. **Everything that cites a signature cites the entry identifier, and never
the packet.**

**Status.** Review-enforced. **No script reads a signature, an owed row, or the
correspondence between the two copies.**

**What the signature's shape forces.** A per-limb verdict table, so nobody can give
or refuse a signature wholesale. An explicit statement of what the signer did
**not** reach. And a list of findings filed with the signature, because a signature
process that produces no findings is measuring nothing.

**Delta-signatures.** Where a change moves after its signature was given, the
signature covers text that no longer exists. The remedy is a **delta-signature**,
which names the moved clauses, names the seats they re-owe, and records the traffic
as *owed* until somebody pays it. **The one-line rule: the accepting seat may
proceed with the traffic recorded as owed, and may not record owed traffic as
paid.** The accepting seat's own discipline carries that rule, and no instrument
reads an owed ledger. **A delta-signature is formed against a diff, and not against
a text**, and it says which hunks moved and which did not.

**The owed ledger has an address.** It is a numbered section inside the decision
record whose own clauses moved — not a standing register and not a separate file —
carrying one row per moved clause: the clause identifier, what moved, and whose
signature the movement re-owes. **Two properties make that the right address.** It
is **obliged reading**, because anyone accepting, citing or amending the instrument
opens the instrument. And it is **append-only in practice**, so a row moving from
OWED to a paid entry identifier leaves the movement visible.

**Residue.** **Nothing mechanical reads the ledger.** An instrument whose author
never writes the section has no ledger, and no signal that the ledger is missing,
which is why §3.6 makes the section a *required* part of the decision-record form.

**Failure.** An author who is also the only reader writes a guard wider or narrower
than the intended rule and never finds out, until the guard either blocks conforming
work or waves through the thing it was written to stop. The delta-signature exists
against a second failure: a signature quietly inherited by a later version of the
text, which converts a real check into a citation.

**Status of the exhibit.** On 2026-08-11 a constrained party signed and, in the same
entry, filed a major finding against the clause it was signing. It separately
offered a limb applying the amendment's own premise to a case the amendment had
exempted, at its own cost.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.5, for the two facsimiles.

### 3.6 Decision records

A **decision record** is a numbered document capturing one non-obvious choice. It
states its status, its deciders, what commissioned it, what it affects, the
context, the decision, the alternatives considered and why each was rejected, the
failure modes it creates or leaves open, and what it explicitly does not decide.

**Five rules give it force.**

1. **Every non-obvious choice gets one.** The test is not size. A record is owed
   when a change moves a **reading rule** — what a figure, a signature or a piece of
   evidence means. It is not owed for a figure, a scope, or a cure inside an
   already-ruled rule.
2. **Alternatives are recorded with their grounds.** The rejected options are what
   stops the same idea returning as fresh in six months.
3. **A record proposes, and a different seat accepts.** The **acceptance act** is a
   separate journal entry by the seat with authority, and it is what flips the
   record from proposed to in force.
4. **The instrument does not edit the file it governs.** Where a record amends the
   constitution, the record carries the new constitutional text as source text, and
   the seat that owns the constitution applies it in its own commit, citing the
   record.
5. **Status is dated.** A record states, per clause, what is in force at its own
   landing, and what act would change that.

**Which seat has authority is not left to inference.** For anything touching the
constitution, the charters or the enforcement scripts, it is the orchestrator,
because those are the files it alone can stage. For anything else it is the seat
named in the record's own deciders block, which must not be the proposing seat.

**Status of rule 4.** **The ownership half is mechanical**: no other seat can stage
the constitution, so an author physically cannot apply its own constitutional diff.
**The writing-it-in-the-record half is a convention.** The record shows it working,
and it also holds instruments whose diffs sit written and unapplied while the rule
they record is already in force.

**Status of rule 5.** Review-enforced. The live test of the form is a record that
says, in its own status block, *proposed, and the rule it records has been in force
since this date*. When the acts land, an act of its own flips the status line,
citing the accepting and applying entries, and **the original status table is
preserved rather than rewritten**.

**Failure.** A decision living in a conversation is re-litigated at every later
contact with it, by parties who each remember a slightly different version, and
the alternatives are gone. The separated acceptance act prevents a seat granting
itself a permission slip.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.6.

### 3.7 Gates

1. A **gate** is a committed checklist a program must fully sign to move to its next
   stage.
2. Every signature is a reference to a journal entry, so governance itself is
   diffable.
3. Each signature must reference an entry stating, in the signer's own log, that it
   signs that item.
4. There is exactly one exception: the sponsor, who has no log. A sponsor's act
   happens outside the repository. The gate row carries the act, dated and
   transcribed, and the authority lives in the **transcribing seat's** own entry
   recording that the act occurred.
5. The conventional set is one organization-ratification gate at the start (§1.7),
   then per phase a specification-freeze gate, an artifact-ready gate and a
   phase-acceptance gate.
6. The constitution states each precondition as a clause, and the checklist quotes
   those clauses verbatim rather than paraphrasing them.

**Status.** **No script parses a checklist.** The discipline is that a signature
which does not resolve to such a sentence — or, for the sponsor, to such a
transcription — is not a signature, and a reader checks that by following the
reference. Rules 3, 4 and 6 are review-enforced.

**Three properties of the checklist table are the mechanism.** The **Signature
column holds an entry reference** — never a name, never a bare date — and on a
sponsor row it holds the transcribed act and names the transcriber's entry. **A row
whose reference does not resolve to a sentence saying *I sign this item* is not
signed.** And **a gate with any row open is a gate that has not passed.**

**Three rules make a gate more than paperwork.**

1. **A gate file states no condition its cited source does not contain.** Write that
   sentence at the top of the checklist itself. This program put it in place on
   **2026-08-11**, with a flat declaration that the file adds no condition of its
   own.
2. **No box in a gate file is checked by its author.** The orchestrator transcribes
   every signature, the authority is the referenced entry, and the checklist edit is
   clerical.
3. **Open items are carried with owners and closing events**, and "to be addressed"
   is not a closing event.

**Status of rule 2, and its residue.** **A signer can stage the file carrying its
own signature.** What stops it is a sentence in that file and the reviewing seats.
Two repairs are available and are not equivalent. Narrow the scope table so the gate
directory is carved out of every signer's scope, which converts the convention into
a refusal. Or keep the scope and keep declaring the residue. **The first is an
amendment to another seat's file, and the source routes it rather than performing
it.**

**The exhibit for rule 1.** On 2026-08-11 a reusable gate block extended a
constitutional gate condition to a second kind of artifact while citing the
constitution for the extension, and the constitution never said it. **The packet
quoted its source accurately. The source was wrong.**

**Failure.** A signer that can edit the record of its own signature can edit the
scope of its own signature. **The second-order failure:** a control whose
enforcement mechanism nobody ever probed will be described as the strongest
mechanism available, rather than as the one in place.

**And the rule that keeps gates honest about themselves.** A gate item is not closed
by a document that proposes its reading, nor by a countersigned one, nor by an
accepted one. **It closes when the gate's own record is repaired and its row is
signed.**

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.7, for the checklist facsimile.

### 3.8 Sign-offs, and why honest failures are preserved

A **sign-off** is the verification line's verdict on one artifact. It states pass or
fail and carries the elements below. **The source states no count of them, and this
volume states none.**

1. The suite, named.
2. The exact commands to reproduce the run.
3. The requirement-to-test mapping, with the gaps declared.
4. The stress-test results.
5. The seeded-defect dispositions (§3.9).
6. **The external anchor's disposition per stimulus class.** Two conditions sit
   inside this one element, and neither is a separate element: what the anchor does
   **not** cover is named beside what it does, and **no artifact-level claim is
   assembled out of passing classes** (§1.4(e)).
7. The open defects.

**This list is a floor, and not a description of any packet.** It is the least a
sign-off may contain. A sign-off is a merge precondition, and a seat relays it
verbatim.

**Status.** **Exactly one sign-off exists in this program's record.** It carries
every element named above, the anchor element included. The source added that
element to the list **after** the measurement behind its stamp, on the verification
lead's own correction, and checked it against the packet in the same act. **Read
this section as a form executed once, well, and not as a form worn smooth by
repetition.**

**A sign-off may say FAIL, and a FAIL is preserved.** Nobody withdraws a failing
verdict when the defect is fixed. The repair is recorded beside the verdict. The
same applies to a prediction that was wrong and to a measurement later superseded:
**the record keeps what it measured.** **Status:** the one sign-off in this record
is a pass quoting its own two earlier failing verdicts inside itself, **which is the
only reason this rule can be said to have been tested at all.**

**A sign-off's reporting form is not allowed to force a fold.** Where the honest
answer has four columns, a rule demanding one ratio pressures its reporter into
collapsing distinctions.

**Status of the exhibit.** On 2026-08-11 a verification lead reported a campaign in five columns rather
than in the ratio the constitution asked for. Its ground was that collapsing a
never-rendered defect class into "killed" would overstate coverage, and that
collapsing it into "survived" would libel a suite never given anything to catch. **The
packet was right and the rule was wrong.** The constitutional clause then moved to
where the practice already was. **Four charters and the live gate checklist did not
move.** At the source's own edition, **four charters still oblige the ratio the
constitution now forbids, and the gate checklist a human signs still quotes the
pre-amendment clauses under a banner reading *verbatim*.**

**Two general forms.** When an honest reporter's output does not fit the reporting
rule, check the rule first. And **an amendment is not landed when its record is
accepted. It is landed when every artifact that restates it has been re-quoted** (§5.9).

**What a sign-off cannot account for is accounted for by somebody else.** A sign-off
is a verdict at one state, by the line that produced the measurements, and it cannot
report what it is about to miss. Every divergence found **after** it passes lands in
the auditor's **escape ledger** (§1.5), naming the sign-off that let it through, the
event that surfaced it, and the owning line's own root-cause entry. **The
verification line's cooperation is a duty. The record is not that line's property.**

**Status.** Review-enforced by design. **Sample size: zero. The ledger has never
existed** (§1.5 states that in full). What it means here is precise: **the pass rate
this section calls falsifiable has not yet been falsifiable in this record**, so the
sentences above describe the instrument's design and not its operation. State the
two facts next to each other, or a reader will take the second from the first: *this
is the sign-off's necessary companion*, and *the one sign-off in this record was
issued without one*.

**Failure.** A sign-off that can only say PASS is a rubber stamp, and everyone
downstream stops reading it. A system that erases superseded measurements loses the
ability to distinguish "we fixed it" from "we never had the problem".

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.8.

### 3.9 Seeded-defect campaigns

This is the mechanism that makes "the tests pass" falsifiable. **A green test suite
is consistent with a suite that checks nothing.**

**The mechanism, in four steps.** The **auditor** authors **defect manifests**:
deliberate defects, each in a stated class. The **orchestrator** applies them in an
isolated state and runs the suite. The scoring seat scores the results against
predictions **sealed before any defect diff existed** (§3.3). The sign-off reports
the disposition of every seeded defect.

**The campaign is placed per artifact, and not on a cadence.** It runs **after the
implementing line's review has accepted the artifact, and before the verification
line may issue its sign-off**, so the artifact-ready gate merely re-checks what the
sign-off already had to state. **A cadence would let an artifact reach sign-off
between campaigns. The placement makes a campaign a precondition of the merge.**

**How a reference is marked.** The marking is a **reserved name prefix**, one
segment, used for nothing else. It turns *"is this reference part of the
lineage?"* into a question a script can ask. **A model that depends on a population
being enumerable needs its enumeration rule written down.**

**The population, measured at the source edition's own commit.** The marked
population is **one hundred references**. **None of them is an ancestor of the
working branch's head.** They are grouped under **sixteen campaign prefixes and two
probes**. The sixteenth prefix is that edition's own newest campaign, whose fourteen
references carry thirteen seeded classes and one re-seed of the class that would not
compile.

**Read the unit carefully.** This is the one section that rules a reference
population cannot be a denominator. **One hundred is a count of references.** It is
not a count of campaigns, of which there are sixteen, and still less a count of
defect classes, which is the unit every rule below insists on.

**How the never-merged half was re-checked.** Each reference whose object the clone
holds was tested directly for ancestry of the head, and none is an ancestor. The
remainder are settled by a property of a full clone: **a commit that is an ancestor
of the head is necessarily present in a clone holding the head's whole history, so
an object the clone does not have cannot be one.** **An absence that follows from
the shape of the artifact is stronger evidence than a check somebody has to remember
to run.**

**The sequencing and the separations.**

1. **The subject under test is the test suite, and not the artifact.** The campaign
   brief says so explicitly, because a campaign read as a test of the artifact
   produces the wrong response to every result.
2. **The seeder never operates the repository.** It composes the defect table and
   cannot make a defect land differently from its manifest, because it does not push.
3. **The operator never authors the evidence.** The orchestrator executes a table it
   did not compose, so anybody can check its errors against the table.
4. **No agent in the graded line runs while a manifest is applied**, where the
   graded line is every seat that could repair the artifact under measurement.
5. **The seeder reads from an allowlist.** The campaign names the complete set of
   paths the seeder may read.
6. **Mutated artifacts never enter the lineage.** Manifests are applied on marked
   references nobody merges, or in a working state somebody reverts.

**Status of rules 1 to 6.** All six are **review-enforced**, and no script reads any
of them.

- Rules 2 and 3 became standing after a major finding was sustained against the
  commissioning seat's own dispatches, following an episode in which the auditor did
  cut, commit and push, on instruction, plus three earlier deviations. **The
  instrument that records the rule is still proposed.** Carry the rule. Do not carry
  the impression that it has been holding since the founding.
- Rule 5 holds **by declaration, and not by construction.** No read denial exists
  (Annex A.4), and the campaign packet carries the allowlist.
- Rule 6 states a **measured fact, and not a guarantee.** **Nothing prevents a marked
  reference from being merged.** No marked reference has ever been merged, and an
  independent seat re-checks that reference by reference. **If you adopt the
  pushed-reference model, that measurement is the control, and it has to be
  somebody's standing duty.**

**Failure.** An agent whose entire value is that it did not touch the artifact
touches the artifact, with an irreversible command, under an identity whose
write scope forbids the paths, and with no journal entry to attribute it. A
denylist cannot be defeated by a document its author forgot to enumerate, and
**an allowlist fails closed**.

**What is sealed is the discriminating part.** Seal which units must go red, which
must stay green, and the exact expected failure messages. **The set of units that
turns red rarely distinguishes one seeded class from another**, because two
unrelated defects routinely light the same units, so **the seal that discriminates
is the expected message text**, taken from whichever assertion speaks first.
**Status:** review-enforced.

#### The scoring rules

**Import these as rules. Do not re-derive them.** Each is a compressed ruling from a
round that mis-scored something, and the rounds cost far more than the rules.
**Every one of them is review-enforced.** No script reads a tally, a column, an
equivalence proof or the floor.

**A boundary on that instruction.** These are the properties the record must be able
to answer for. **They are not a reporting form.** The form belongs to the reporter,
and it is expected to move when a campaign shows it insufficient — in this record the
reporting schema moved twice in nine days. Nor does the instruction reach the
**figures**: the standing discipline there is the opposite one. **A figure carried
across rounds is re-derived by the method its carrier claims, or the carrier states
that it was quoted. Import the rules. Re-derive the numbers.**

1. **The question is present-tense.** The gate asks whether the suite *as it stands
   now* kills what was seeded, and not whether every past campaign scored perfectly.
   A campaign's score is a frozen measurement and is never retro-edited.
2. **Two columns, and the difference itemized.** *Sealed* is every defect class the
   manifest sealed, with nothing removed for any later reason. *Seeded* is the subset
   actually rendered as sealed and run. Every member of the difference is named with
   its ground.
3. **Kept long, and quoted rather than summarized.** *All three grounds sit in
   `sealed`, none sits in `seeded`, and each is named at the tally with its ground.*
   Without that sentence the two column definitions contradict each other on ground
   3, because a negative control **is** rendered and run.
4. **Ground 1 — never rendered.** The manifest sealed the class and nobody wrote a
   patch. **This is not a defect the suite failed to catch. It is a defect that does not
   exist.** Folding it into "caught" overstates coverage, and folding it into "missed"
   libels a suite never given anything to find.
5. **Ground 2 — unscoreable.** Somebody wrote a patch and ran it, and inspection
   afterwards showed it did not render the class the seal named. **Its run is a scope
   report, and it supports no claim about the suite in either direction.**
6. **Ground 3 — negative control.** Somebody seeded a class deliberately so a **named
   assertion stays green**, to show the campaign's instrument does not simply light up
   at everything. Its seal predicts the green and declares in advance that the class
   scores nothing. **Somebody renders and runs it like any other class, and it still
   sits in `sealed` and not in `seeded`.**
7. **The list of grounds is open, and the naming duty is not.** A class excluded on
   any ground is named with that ground, where the reviewer reads it and may refuse
   it.
8. **A ground that turns on what the seal disclosed holds only where the disclosure
   was frozen before the run**, and never on what the run returned. Grounds 2 and 3
   are both of that kind.
9. **The unit of the record is the defect class**, and not the branch or file that
   delivered it. A reference population grows by infrastructure accident.
10. **Every non-kill is named individually.** No non-kill is folded into a kill, and
    no ratio stands in for the dispositions.
11. **Kept long.** A defect that **survived its own campaign** — its seal predicted a
    kill and no unit killed it — and that somebody later caught, is dispositioned in
    exactly one evidence form: the **unmodified** committed diff, replayed against the
    suite as it stands now, at a run identifier, with the killing unit named.
    **Anything weaker lets a survivor be argued dead.**
12. **Kept long.** A campaign kill is a frozen measurement too, so its campaign record
    dispositions it **together with the named killing unit, present and green now.**
    That form catches a killing check deleted or disabled since the campaign. **It
    does not catch one weakened, nor a class whose rendering no longer applies to the
    artifact, nor a record whose unit names no longer exist in the suite.**
13. **Equivalent defects leave the denominator only on a proof.** A defect no
    conformant observation can distinguish from the correct artifact is *equivalent*,
    under three conditions together: a committed artifact proves the equivalence, the
    proof quantifies over the **specification's** legal input space and never over a
    suite, and the seeder records the exclusion in its own artifact. **An argument
    that a suite cannot reach the defect is a coverage gap, and not an equivalence.**
14. **Unreachable checks contribute nothing to a coverage claim**, and still
    discharge their requirement. A check **no possible defect of the artifact could
    reach** has already been counted once as a discharged requirement, and counting it
    again inside a coverage figure counts one observation twice. **A check the seeded
    set merely happened not to reach is a seeding gap, and not an unreachable
    check.** Publish the unreachable set **beside** the tally.
15. **A floor on the number of seeded classes**: at least three, spanning distinct
    defect classes, measured **before** any equivalence exclusion.

**Status of ground 3.** It is **case law, and not design intent.** A reviewing
council recorded that no exercised negative control exists in this program's record
and asked that ground 3 be marked as design intent. **The record refutes that.** One
campaign in the class-based era seeded a control whose seal, frozen before the run,
named one assertion, predicted it would stay green, and stated in terms that the
class scores no kill in its own right. The run observed that green at every member
and at both start conditions, and scored nothing. **Why four independent seats
missed it is the useful half**: the era's own carried figure had already removed
that class from its denominator, so a reader walking the tally never met it. **A
class excluded from a denominator is a class excluded from review.**

**Status of rule 12's limits.** The first limit is disclosed in the clause itself.
**Somebody found the other two afterwards, by applying the clause.** In this record,
**five of thirty-seven committed renderings no longer apply to the artifact they
were scored against** — a figure measured over the first artifact's campaign era,
before this record's eleventh campaign, and scoped that way so it keeps its truth as
the record grows. Separately, the unit names the older campaign records carry were
later retired wholesale, so a literal application of *the named killing unit,
present and green* fails at every class of four campaigns, for a reason that is not
a missing instrument. **A form whose limits you inherit as complete is more
dangerous than a form you know to be partial.**

#### The worked tally

**This volume does not reproduce the worked tally.** The source prints a filled
instance of the block above — one artifact, class-based era, ten campaigns — derived
from a committed machine-readable data file, with its two arithmetic identities
closing in print and its unreachable set published beside it rather than inside it.
**Read it at `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.9, and read the data file it is derived from.**

**Status of that block, on two limbs.** Since 2026-08-12 a governance check in this
repository runs on every push and **refuses** if the data file's two arithmetic
identities stop closing, or if any of its `sealed`, `seeded` and `killed` figures is
nowhere in the source's text. **No instrument holds the other limb**: the check asks
whether a figure appears **in the file**, and not whether it appears **in the block**.
So the closure is re-run for you, and the derivation of the printed block from the
data file is still a reviewer's act. **More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.9's posture note,
which states the same split.

**One departure the source states rather than leaves to be discovered.** The sign-off
packet in this record carries its era at a **different sealed figure**, computed under
a column definition the constitution has since replaced, and **the packet names the
movement itself**. **No measurement moved**: killed, survived and green-by-blindness
are unchanged, and what changed is which classes a definition admits to a column.
**Re-stating the packet's own cell at the amended definition is owed, by the
verification lead**, and it is not the source document's act.

**The general form.** When an amendment moves a column's definition, every figure
computed under the old definition is a figure in a unit that no longer exists.
Re-derive it with the old figure printed beside it. **Never overwrite the old figure,
and never leave the new definition unexercised.**

#### The exhibits

The source carries four anonymized exhibits here, and **this volume does not render
them**. Three of their rulings are stated as rules above: the naming duty at the tally
(rule 7), the evidence form for a survivor (rule 11), and the unit of the record (rule
9). The fourth is a rule about rules: **a singular noun inside a counting rule is a
specification of uniqueness, so if the record's instances are plural, somebody has
already applied the rule to something it does not fit.** **More:** `docs/PROCESS.md`
§3.9.

**The failure classes, collected.** *Without the campaign:* "the tests pass" is
unfalsifiable, and a suite silently rots into a syntax check. *Without the sealing:*
hindsight scores the campaign. *Without the separations:* the party whose work is
measured chooses the measurements. *Without the itemized dispositions:* a single
ratio hides exactly the cases that carry information. *Without the equivalence proof
standard:* "that one is equivalent" becomes the sentence a party writes when its
suite did not catch something, and the definition is unfalsifiable from the losing
side, because a blind suite and an equivalent defect emit identical evidence.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.9, for the marking facsimile and the full exhibits.

### 3.10 The lessons harvest

1. Every sign-off and every gate carries a **lessons harvest**. It is a precondition
   of the gate, and not a follow-up to it.
2. Each seat with a permanent journal mines **its own** log over the span since its
   last harvest.
3. Each seat states that span as an **entry-identifier interval**, so spans tile and
   a skipped harvest is a visible gap.
4. A lead also mines the worker spans it commissioned.

**A candidate rule is admissible only if it does three things.** It **cites the
incident** that taught it, at the state where a reader can see the thing going
wrong. It **states its observable in terms portable beyond the project.** It **says
what breaks without it** — a concrete outcome a reviewer could recognize in somebody
else's repository, and not a virtue.

**Portability has two grades.** **General**: the rule statement contains no proper
noun of any kind, and it must teach a stranger to both the domain and the project.
**Domain**: the statement may name domain nouns such as standards, algorithm
families and encodings, may name no project noun, and must name the **domain pack**
it belongs to. **Both grades are read with the provenance hidden.** Anything passing
neither grade is recorded as a **war story** — kept, binding nowhere, re-offerable
later — **or it is adopted locally by its own instrument**. **A nil yield is
declared, and never omitted.**

**A domain pack** is a named bucket of rules holding for one domain and not
universally. A domain-grade rule names its pack, so a later program pulls in only
the packs whose domains are its own. **State where your packs live before you run
your first harvest**, even if the answer is one file in this repository.

**The classifier.** The mining seat runs it on its own candidates, in a fixed order,
starting from the most general honest statement. A candidate reaches the domain
grade only **through** a general statement its author attempted and found hollow.
The collating seat re-runs the classifier as a check, with the provenance hidden.

**Collation is clerical.** The collating seat may bounce a defective statement back
to its author. **The collating seat may not improve one.**

**Status.** The bars, the grades, the span rule and the collation rule are
**review-enforced**, and the constitution declares that posture in its own clause.
The program's first harvest ran across five seats, and the gate block carries
unchecked boxes that hold the gate closed until somebody pays them.

**Where the harvest goes, since 2026-08-17.** The sponsor who commissioned this
organization **retracted the design under which a program's learning moved to
another repository**. Four things replace it.

1. **All destinations are local.** The collating seat lands each harvest in the
   program's own lessons file, in **one landing commit per harvest**, and never one
   per gate. Project-tier statements and war stories stay where they already are.
2. **That file doubles as the hand-off artifact, the travel copy.** Its header
   carries the contract: **learning leaves the repository only when a human hands the
   file, or its post-baseline tail, to another session or project, and the receiving
   end records that as seed provenance.** A **baseline** line marks where the
   program's own learning starts, and *empty* is a legitimate baseline that somebody
   must write down rather than leave implied.
3. **There is no transport.** No packet, no perimeter, no proposal and no consent
   question, because nothing transmits. A shared corpus, if one exists anywhere,
   grows by its own maintainer's hand-carried commits under ordinary review, and the
   originating program has no lane into it.
4. **The sponsor's candidate-by-candidate refusal power retires with the movement it
   gated.** The harvest's whole sponsor touchpoint is now the gate signature they
   were already giving. **A refusal power over a movement that does not happen is a
   power with no object.**

**The honest history of what was retracted.** Somebody designed, specified, built and
executed the replaced pipeline **exactly once, end to end**, and **that proposal never
landed**: its owner retracted it before its first landing, which was also its
designated first test. **A pipeline that has never landed is a design rather than an
asset**, and the cost of removing this one was one round precisely because nothing was
running on it. **The discipline survived the transport's retraction whole**: every
bar, span rule, grade, classifier and gate precondition above is byte-unchanged, and
one arrow moved.

**Failure classes.** *Without the cadence:* lessons live in one session's
context and die with it. *Without the provenance requirement:* a lesson decays
into a preference nobody can attach to an incident.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §3.10.

## 4. The operating disciplines

Machinery enforces the rules of §2. Habit and review enforce the practices of this
section, and they are the practices an adopter who has not paid for them is most
likely to drop.

### 4.1 The abort-first precheck

1. Before opening a single file for editing, an agent checks the state of the
   working tree.
2. It also checks the current commit against what its instructions said to expect.
3. It records both outputs in its journal.
4. If the tree is dirty in a way the dispatch did not declare, the agent **stops**
   and returns a refusal with the forensic detail.
5. If the commit is not the expected one, and is not a descendant of it, the agent
   **stops** and returns a refusal with the forensic detail.

**Status.** Review-enforced. The dispatch text carries it, and the *Trigger* and
*Actions* sections of the resulting entry record it. **No instrument enforces it.**
The audit that produced the source's stamps performed it, and so did the round that
produced the eighth edition, and both recorded the two commands' output.

**Failure.** An agent that starts editing a tree it has not looked at either commits
somebody else's uncommitted work under its own name, or builds on a state that has
since moved.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.1.

### 4.2 Declared siblings, in both directions

1. **Outward.** A dispatch names every other agent that may be writing concurrently,
   and names the paths they may touch. An undeclared dirty path is an abort.
2. **Inward.** If the current commit moves while an agent is working, the agent
   verifies rather than assumes: that the new commit is a descendant of the expected
   one, which paths it touched, and whether any intersect its own.
3. The agent re-runs any measurement that could have changed.
4. The agent records all of it in its return.

**Status.** Review-enforced. **No instrument enforces either direction.**

**Failure.** Without the outward declaration, a legitimate concurrent landing is
indistinguishable from contamination. Without the inward declaration, a
mid-round landing is invisible, and the agent's measurements were taken against
a state that no longer exists.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.2.

### 4.3 Verbatim relay, with the relayer's additions marked

1. Traffic between seats is classified, and a routing seat may summarize routine
   traffic.
2. A routing seat relays a **protected class** unedited. The protected classes are
   sign-off packets, defect packets, **sealed predictions**, and every auditor
   finding.
3. **Before unsealing, a routing seat does not relay a seal at all.** A seal nobody
   has opened does not move, and the routing seat's duty is to carry the fact of the
   seal and none of its content.
4. Where a relaying seat adds anything of its own, it **marks the addition as the
   relayer's**, so a reader can attack it rather than absorb it.
5. Where a relay's fidelity matters to an argument, the **receiving seat checks the
   relay against the source artifact**, and does not reason from the relay.

**Status of the sampling instrument.** The relay-fidelity spot-check on the
protected classes is the auditor's charter duty. **It is owed. It has never
happened.** Early in the program the auditor recorded that fidelity was untestable,
because no protected-class packet had yet been relayed. Protected-class packets have
existed since then, in quantity, and no spot-check followed.

**What that does not say.** It names **one instrument**, and not the whole subject.
**Two fidelity checks have in fact fired in this record, both by receiving seats**
reading a relay against its source artifact. That is rule 5, and it is a different
control with a different failure mode: it fires only where a receiver happens to
look, and it cannot sample the relays nobody contested. **The owed instrument stays
owed. The record is not empty.** An adopter who hears only the first half will build
the sampling round and drop the receiving-seat habit, which is the one that has
actually caught things here.

**Reason for rule 3.** Relay a seal early and the campaign becomes a confirmation.
Summarize a seal late and the score becomes negotiable.

**Failure.** A relayer softens a finding by compression, and compression always
removes the sharp part first, so the receiving seat then reasons correctly from a
subtly wrong premise. Marking additions prevents the second-order version, where the
relayer's gloss acquires the original filer's authority one hop later.

**Status of the three exhibits, dated 2026-08-11.** One relay dropped a single word
from a finding about record fidelity, and nothing depended on the word. The same relay
reported a killing unit as **present** under the name the suite uses today, for classes
whose campaign record names that unit in a **retired namespace**: the mapping was
correct, somebody performed it silently, and it is invisible in the artifact. A later
relay quoted the committed act accurately and **carried three of its findings not at
all**. **A receiving seat caught both non-benign cases by reading the committed source.
The relayer caught neither, and a sampling instrument that does not exist would have
caught neither.**

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.3.

### 4.4 Push at every landing

1. Committed is not safe.
2. Work is durable only once somebody pushes it.
3. The discipline is to push at every landing, and not at the end of a session.

**Status.** Review-enforced. It is a habit of the committing seat, with no
instrument. **It is the one discipline in this section the audit behind the source's
stamps did not measure**, because a reader cannot check it from a repository copy
without comparing against the remote.

**Failure.** A container, a session or a machine dies with an hour of unpushed work
inside it, and the replacement cannot tell what was lost, so it either redoes work
that already landed or omits work that did not.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.4.

### 4.5 Incident recovery

When a session dies mid-round, the recovery has a fixed shape.

1. **Preserve the partial as evidence**, outside the working tree.
2. **Discard the partial from the tree.** A partial edit is not a draft. It is an
   artifact whose author cannot be asked what it was going to say.
3. **Derive fresh.** Tell the replacement explicitly that no prior partial exists,
   and that everything is derived from the committed record.
4. **Record the incident** in the round's journal entry, including the fact that the
   round is a respawn.

**Status.** Review-enforced, with no instrument. A round executed it in full
immediately before the audit that produced the source's stamps.

**Failure.** A half-applied change looks like a complete one, because nobody
wrote the parts that would have contradicted it, so the resuming agent's review
finds nothing wrong. **Discarding and re-deriving costs a round. Resuming costs a
defect nobody can date.**

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.5.

### 4.6 Escalation classes

Escalation is not a mood. A fixed, short list of classes may reach the sponsor, and
the organization decides everything else internally and records it.

| Class | What it is |
|---|---|
| **E1** | Phase-gate approval |
| **E2** | Scope changes — adding or dropping a requirement, a phase, or a role |
| **E3** | Toolchain and licensing decisions |
| **E4** | Auditor critical findings, relayed verbatim and never summarized away |
| **E5** | Two-lead deadlock surviving one round of written argument |
| **E6** | Budget or schedule anomalies past a threshold |

**Status.** Review-enforced. **No instrument routes or counts escalations.** The
table is **partly exercised**: E1, E3 and E4 have each been used, and **E2, E5 and
E6 have never been used** in this program's life. **A class that has never fired is
not thereby wrong**, and an adopter should know which parts of this table are tested
and which are design.

**The E6 threshold.** This program's constitution carries exactly one figure for it,
and carries it as an example rather than as a bound: **a phase tracking past twice
its estimate**. That is the whole of what exists. There is no budget figure and no
second trigger. **No round has ever fired this class**, so nobody has applied the
figure to anything. **The number is illustrative, and the parameter is
adopter-chosen** (Annex A.9(b)).

**Rule.** What transfers is not the multiplier. It is the requirement to write the
threshold down **before the first anomaly**, because a threshold chosen while looking
at an overrun is chosen by the party the escalation would embarrass. **A class whose
threshold is decided at its first firing has no threshold. It has a negotiation.**

**Three further rules.** Every escalation arrives **decision-ready**, with options, a
recommendation and cost, and one that does not is bounced back. A lead that wants
work done outside its own scope **files a request, and does not do the work**. And
where both parties reject an adjudicating seat's ruling and one round of written
argument fails, that seat **declares deadlock** rather than ruling again by fiat.

**Failure classes.** *Without fixed classes:* the sponsor is either flooded and
stops reading, or never told about the one item that is theirs. *Without
decision-ready form:* the sponsor is asked to do the analysis the organization
exists to do.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.6.

### 4.7 The sponsor's reserved decisions

The sponsor's surface is deliberately small. As of the source's eighth edition it
holds four standing items, and the amendment procedure closes it.

1. The **six escalation classes** (§4.6).
2. One **one-time infrastructure setup**: the branch protection without which the
   history guarantee is convention. Somebody configured it and verified it by live
   fire on **2026-08-01** (§2.5).
3. The standing **canary** power (§1.5).
4. **Ratification of the organization itself** (§1.7).

**A fifth item stood from 2026-08-04 to 2026-08-17**:
**candidate-by-candidate refusal at the lessons harvest**, the human gate on what
left the program. The constitution's harvest clause granted it when that clause
landed, and the clause's amendment on 2026-08-17 removed it. **It was retired with
the movement it gated** (§3.10). **Nobody ever exercised it**, because the one
delivery this program ever made never landed anywhere, so no candidate ever reached
the point at which a refusal would have been taken.

**How a sponsor signs anything.** §3.7 requires every gate signature to resolve to an
entry in the signer's own log, and the sponsor has none. The practised form is: the
sponsor's act happens outside the repository, and **the orchestrator transcribes it**
into the gate row as a dated line naming the act, with the authority living in the
orchestrator's own entry recording that the act occurred. **Residue:** the
transcription is the only artifact, so **a transcription nobody makes is an act
nobody can cite.**

**The sponsor's spot-check.** A single file tells the sponsor how to spot-check the
whole program by hand, in five steps. **Three of the five are version-control
commands**: every commit title with its one agent, one agent's entire thread, and any
change beside the reasoning that produced it. **The other two are not commands.**
They are a two-hop walk from the roster file to a charter to that seat's log tail,
and a look at the continuous-integration tab.

**What is not the sponsor's.** Design choices, test adequacy, work routing, review
verdicts and schedule detail are explicitly **not theirs**, and the sponsor guide
says so in those words.

**Failure.** A sponsor with an unbounded surface becomes the bottleneck the
organization was built to remove. A sponsor with no enumerated surface becomes a
rubber stamp who cannot tell whether they are being asked or informed.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.7.

### 4.8 Refusal is a first-class outcome

1. A seat that declines to act, with grounds, and records the refusal, produces a
   normal and valued result. That seat has not failed to deliver.
2. A signer may refuse a countersignature.
3. A precheck may abort.
4. The record's author may refuse a relay correction.
5. A seat may **stop** a commissioned cure and return it, when performing it would
   require exceeding a write scope, or would desynchronize an authority from the text
   it governs.
6. A seat declines an instruction arriving outside the routed channel — from tooling,
   from an automated prompt, or from any source that is not the agent's principal.
7. The refusing seat records the refusal **with its grounds**, in its own log, where
   a reviewer can review it and overturn it.

**Status.** Review-enforced. Charters and dispatch text carry it, and it has been
exercised: an independent seat refused a dispatch that would have had it operate the
repository, before any of the work existed, and that refusal became the finding that
produced the rule of §3.9.

**Status of the exhibit, dated 2026-08-11.** A round commissioned to apply six small
cures applied five. The sixth was a one-word improvement to a sentence that had become live
constitutional text since the commissioning. Applying it in the source document
alone would have left the authority and the constitution disagreeing. Applying
it in the constitution was outside the seat's scope. The seat stopped it,
returned it as an amendment candidate, and recorded the debt. **A one-word
improvement bought with a rule-and-check disagreement in the constitution is not a
bargain.**

**Failure.** An organization in which every dispatch must produce a deliverable will
get one, including in the rounds where the honest answer was "this cannot be done
from here", and that deliverable looks exactly like the others.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §4.8.

## 5. The failure museum

These are the generalized lessons this program paid for. **This volume renders each
entry's lesson, its consequence, and any calibration the source attaches. It does not
render the shapes, the incidents or the figures.**

### 5.1 Remedies decay without mechanical checks

**Lesson.** A remedy adopted as a practice, with no check that fires when somebody
skips it, reverts to the pre-remedy behaviour within a few rounds, and the reversion is
invisible because the documentation still describes the remedy. **Adopt every remedy
together with the thing that will notice its absence, or adopt it with an explicit
statement that no such thing exists.** Where the appropriate check turns out to be a
warning rather than a refusal, state the honest framing: **the warning does not buy
honesty. It buys latency.**

**Status.** The warning this corollary was written about entered force on
**2026-08-12** and counts rather than gates. **The latency figure is still a
projection**, because the measurement of whether the latency is real is the *next*
decay, which has not happened yet. **A control that has never fired in anger is a
design with a process identifier.**

**Without it.** Every remedy in the record is a claim about the past rather than a
property of the present, and a reader auditing the rules cannot tell which are live.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.1.

### 5.2 A column's name is not its definition

**Lesson.** The label on a column and the rule that decides what enters it are two
different things, and they drift. **A word that does normative work in a verdict line,
while appearing in no instrument, is a normative property nobody wrote down.** When the
same defect appears three times, stop patching instances: the rule declares **the list
of grounds open and the naming duty closed**.

**Without it.** A reader of a number cannot recover what the number counted, and
somebody cites the number as if the reader could.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.2.

### 5.3 A relay can demonstrate the hazard it reports

**Lesson.** A message about fidelity is itself subject to fidelity loss. **Handle a
report about a control with the control it reports on**, and quote the source artifact
rather than the relay whenever the quotation goes into a binding instrument.
**Meta-level traffic is not exempt from the hazards it discusses.**

**Without it.** The reports that would repair your controls arrive already damaged by
the defect they describe, and the damage is invisible because everyone reads them as
reports rather than as artifacts.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.3.

### 5.4 Timestamps are testimony, and entry identifiers are sequence

**Lesson.** A timestamp written by an author is an assertion about a clock, and
authors' clocks run wrong, so **carry order by something mechanically enforced, such as
a monotonic counter, and not by a stamp**. A record should say which of its fields is
testimony and which is structure. Take stamps honestly at authoring, even when that
makes one read *earlier* than the commit that carries it. **And a frozen record is not
repaired by rewriting it**: when an audit found eight dated rows disagreeing with the
commit dates they cited, **no row was edited**, and what was added was a note stating
which field to read when order matters.

**Without it.** A reader reconstructs sequence from timestamps, and reconstructs it
wrong, in a record whose whole value is that sequence is recoverable.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.4.

### 5.5 A guard that forecloses conduct is enforced differently from one that routes traffic

**Lesson.** A rule that **forecloses conduct** can be enforced at a boundary as a
refusal, and should be. A rule that **routes traffic** cannot be enforced by refusal at
all, because its failure mode is **silence**: an act that never happens, which no
boundary check can observe. A routing rule needs a **named owner**, a **named
triggering event**, and a **visible debt** some later reader is obliged to read. **The
enforcement is that the unpaid debt is legible, and not that the omission is blocked.**
The constitution states the resulting rule as a posture declaration: **a clause says
whether it is machine-enforced or review-enforced in its own text.**

**Status of the practice.** **Three clauses** in this program's constitution carry a
**labelled** posture declaration. **Several more** declare it in the clause's own
prose, without the tag. **The rest** state it in surrounding prose, or not at all,
including the whole of the commit protocol. **The number to carry is three**, and the
boundary is *labelled*.

**Without it.** You will build an enforcement layer that is simultaneously oppressive
and porous, refusing things that should be argued and advising about things that should
be impossible.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.5.

### 5.6 Repairs that verify each other belong in one commit

**Lesson.** Where two changes are mutually load-bearing — where each becomes derivable,
checkable or even statable only once the other exists — splitting them across commits
produces an intermediate state in which the record asserts something nobody can verify
from the tree at that point. **Land them together.** And take evidence at the
artifact's own state, because **evidence taken at a neighbouring state, plus an
argument that nothing moved, is a claim that decays unchecked**.

**Without it.** History contains commits that were correct in the author's head and
unverifiable in the repository, and an auditor re-executing evidence at the recorded
state finds failures that are artifacts of the split rather than of the work.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.6.

### 5.7 The author grading its own homework is the root class

**Lesson.** Almost every other failure in this museum is a specialization of one:
somewhere, a party ended up assessing its own work, and the assessment was structurally
incapable of returning "no". **The six disguises are the useful part.**

1. The graded party as its own reviewer.
2. The document that reads its own source into compliance.
3. The author of an exclusion being the party the exclusion benefits.
4. The permission slip, which is an instrument authorizing its own author to make the
   change it proposes.
5. The self-inherited signature.
6. The measurement that grades the measurer.

**The general test to apply to any proposed control.** Ask who is made worse off when
the control fires, and whether that party controls whether it fires. **If the answer is
the same party, the control does not exist yet, whatever the document says.**

**Status.** Four of the six are anchored to incidents in this program's record. **Two
are marked**, because an audit of the source could anchor the codification and not the
episode. **Disguise 6 is operating in this program, in the open**, under the four
constraints of §3.3.

**Without it.** Every other guarantee in the source is decorative, because each
terminates in some party's assessment of something.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.7.

### 5.8 Two smaller lessons worth carrying

**Lesson 1.** A qualification drawn from an author's own practice describes the author,
so **mark such a generalization as the author's own formulation, in the text**.

**Lesson 2.** An agent crosses an absolute prohibition in a task's opening moves, by
habit, before it has read the text in full. **Prohibitions therefore belong at the top
of the document that carries them, in the imperative**, and the enforcement belongs at
the boundary.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.8.

### 5.9 The amendment pipeline has no last mile

**Lesson.** An amendment is not landed when its decision record is accepted. **It is
landed when every artifact that restates the amended rule has been re-quoted.** Nothing
in the amendment procedure checks that, so the gap is invisible from inside every
document that cites the record. **A debt ledger with no closing cadence is a debt
ledger that grows monotonically**, and the tell is a row that names its trigger, records
that the trigger fired, and is still open.

**The cure, in three parts of increasing cost.** *One:* an amendment record's
owed-restatement list names each target **file**, and nobody accepts the record until
each is either re-quoted or carries a dated refusal. *Two:* the ledger's rows carry a
**cadence**, and not just an owner and a trigger. *Three:* the restatement check becomes
a script that greps each amended clause's distinctive text across the artifacts that
restate it.

**Status.** Review-enforced, and thinly: what carries it is a reviewer noticing.
**Somebody exercised part one once, on 2026-08-17**, and the first exercise returned the
thing the cure was designed to catch — the author's list came back **two rows longer**
than the list it was handed. **Parts two and three remain unbuilt**, and the ledger's
rows still carry closing events rather than cadences. One more rule the same round
settled: **a debt is discharged either by building the instrument or by refusing it
with grounds** (§2.4).

**Without it.** The organization runs two rules — the record's and the artifacts' — and
the rule a working seat obeys is whichever artifact is nearest to hand, which is
reliably the stale one.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §5.9.

## 6. Adopting this

### 6.0 The export unit — the source document plus the shell

**The source document is half of an export unit, and it is the half that explains.**
The other half is an executable **shell**: the machinery, extracted into its own
repository, which an adopter clones and starts from. It is the repository
`generic-agentic-fpga-org`, at `github.com/renatom11/generic-agentic-fpga-org`. The
name is a program noun, deliberately, because an identifier a reader cannot resolve is
worth nothing.

**The shell is FPGA-generic, and not domain-free.** Three independent readers fetched
it and returned the same thing: its README opens *"A reusable AI-agent organization for
FPGA programs"*, its policy module hard-codes this program's nine seat names, and its
implementation charter opens by claiming ownership of hardware description language.
**The shell is generic across projects in one domain. It is not generic across
domains, and no step of §6.2 de-domains it.**

**Status.** Review-enforced, and weakly. **The specification lead has not inspected
the shell.** The statement rests on three committed council reports that did.

**What carries domain load.** **None**, so clone and run: the commit gate, the history
re-check, the chain verifier, the self-test harness, and the policy module's *shape*.
**Full**, so rewrite: the policy module's *contents*, which are this program's seat
names and path prefixes. **Heavy**, so keep the form and rewrite the bodies: the
charter set and the launcher prompts. **Light or medium**: the workflow, the
constitution template, the packet forms and the sponsor guide.

**The stated repair.** §3.10 defines a **domain pack**, and applied to the shell's own
files that split **is** that classification. The stated intent is to refactor the shell
into a core plus an FPGA pack. **Read that as an intent and not as a fact. It is not
done. It is the shell's repository and not this one's.**

**The pin, stated to the limit of what this repository can attest.**

1. **Facsimiles taken at:** this repository, at the source edition's own commit. The
   facsimile blocks in the source are transcribed from **this program's** artifacts,

2. **Shell observed at:** `main` = **`cb8a9f3`**, on **2026-08-17**.
3. **Read the grade of that observation.** It is **first-party**: this program's own

#### The precedence rule

There are **two adoption orders**, and an adopter holding both halves meets the fork at
minute one.

1. **With the shell in hand, the shell's own `BOOTSTRAP.md` governs the sequence.** It
   is written from inside the artifact being installed, and where the two orders
   disagree about *what to do when*, it has the machinery in front of it.
2. **§6.2 governs as the shell-less fallback, and as the reasons underneath either
   order.** It is **the only order that anybody has ever executed end to end — twice,
   and both times in worlds that could not reach the shell** — and it is where every
   step's *why* is written.
3. **Follow the shell's sequence, and read §6.2's grounds for each step.** Where the
   shell's order does something §6.2 warns against, check §6.2's warning rather than
   obeying it blindly.

**Status.** Review-enforced. Nothing checks which order an adopter followed.

**The one concrete disagreement is stale, and none is currently known.** Through the
seventh edition the source said the shell configures platform protections early while
§6.2 places them after continuous integration has run green once. The third adoption
run went to apply that adjudication and **found nothing to apply**, because at the
pinned shell the two orders already agreed. **The disagreement was stale at the pin the
document was pinned to, and the document could not tell.** **What survives is the
constraint, and not the adjudication**: the platform's required-check binding cannot
name a check the platform has never seen (Annex A.5), so **any** order must put the
first green run before the protections. **Check your order against the constraint, and
not against this history.**

**The rule's honest limit.** **The precedence rule adjudicates sequence, and is silent
about coverage.** It answers *who wins when both orders speak*, and says nothing about
*who speaks when only one does*, which is the ordinary case. Three findings follow from
one execution. **The shell's operative order is not one file**: it spans three, and the
rule that decided that run's entire path lived in the one the kit table names nowhere.
**Four instruments exist only in §6.2** — the preflight, the Step 0 trace, the residue
list and the definition of done — and every one still ran, under a meta-rule the
adopter had to invent: *the shell's text governs wherever it speaks, and the items with
no counterpart still execute, landing as reports rather than as law.* And **the better
cross-check instrument is the six invariants of §6.2**, three of which **dissolve** on
the with-shell path rather than needing satisfaction.

**Status of the executed runs.** **The with-shell path has been executed twice, and
this program commissioned both executions.** §6.2 carries the runs and their figures.
**Reachability is discharged. Independence is not**, because both runs were
commissioned by the party whose claim they support, and **a measurement of a document
by the document's own organization raises the floor and closes nothing.**

#### How learning travels

**The shell is an independently maintained repository with its own maintainer and its
own law.** It is not frozen, kept or controlled from this program. On **2026-08-17**,
sponsor-directed, it **retired the transfer law it used to carry**. What replaces it:
**two roles and no third** — a **canonical shell**, which is the installer and runs no
program, and a **founded project**, which is a copy running one program. **Founding is
one hop**: clone the shell, push it to a new empty repository, land the founding commit
recording the copy's own identity **on the default branch**, and get continuous
integration green, which opens the founding gate. **The commit chain must arrive
unsquashed.** **Each program's learning lands in its own lessons file**, which doubles
as the travel copy. **Transport is a human handing over a file** — one direction, one
hop, no machinery, with the receiving end recording seed provenance. **The shell's
corpus grows only by its maintainer's hand-carried commits**, and **the originating
program has no lane**. **The defect channel is unchanged**, because **defects were
never lessons**.

**Disposition of the one delivery ever made.** It reached the shell on **2026-08-11**
as an open proposal, carrying **353 collated candidates**, and **it never landed**.
Those 353 then landed **locally**, as the first entry in this program's own travel
copy, seeded verbatim from the frozen delivery packet: **352 general and one
domain-graded**. The delivery packet stays frozen in place. **A retired mechanism's
artifacts are annotated, and never deleted, or the thing it did becomes unauditable.**
**The law an adopter must now look for at the shell is the retraction itself.**

#### The two instruments that bind the halves

1. **The doc–shell drift check** would test that the source's §2.6 rule table agrees
   with the shell's actual rule set, and would fail loudly when either moves. **This
   instrument does not exist.**
2. **The governance check landed on 2026-08-12**, runs in this repository's continuous
   integration on every push, and **refuses** rather than warns.

**What the governance check holds.** Zero superseded-text sentinels in the law volume.
The two-volume contract: wherever the law volume names the companion, the companion
exists, and both carry an edition anchor in their head. The worked tally's closure,
twice: the machine-readable tally closes on its own two arithmetic identities, and the
law volume's printed figures are found in its text. And render safety: fence markers
pair off in both volumes.

**What it deliberately does not hold.** It does **not** run the line-accounting

### 6.1 What transfers, and what has to be re-earned

**Transfers directly**, subject throughout to the substrate parameters of Annex
A. The seat topology and the two monopolies. The write-scope table. The commit
rules, and the coupling of work to reasoning. Append-only journals with chained
volumes. The packet forms and the work-order state machine. The countersignature
discipline. The decision-record and amendment route. Gates as signed, diffable
checklists. Sealed predictions. Seeded-defect campaigns. **The external
anchoring of any model that grades** (§1.4(e)). The escalation classes. The
harvest cadence.

**Two qualifications on that list.** The sole-spawner monopoly is a substrate fact in
Annex A.1, and it becomes a policy needing new mechanism if your substrate differs.
**Three of the six escalation classes have never fired here** (§4.6).

**Has to be re-earned.** The judgment about which rules in a given program need to be
machine-enforced, which are review-enforced, and which should not exist. That
judgment came from incidents. **An adopter who imports the whole rule set on faith
gets the ceremony without the calibration, and the first schedule crunch removes the
wrong half.**

**The one thing that must not be imported as a claim.** Any statement in the source
that a control is mechanically enforced is a measurement of somebody else's
machinery.

**Status of executing that instruction.** This program executed it against itself for
the first time in the round that produced the second edition. Of 127 claims carrying
a posture, **15 were false and 8 were owed**. **Not one of the false ones was a
judgement call**: a file the reader could open refuted every one.

**Two lessons for an adopter.** **A document's false claims cluster where it describes
artifacts, and not where it describes scripts**, because scripts refuse and artifacts
merely testify. And the cost of finding all fifteen was one seat and one round, against
a record that made every claim checkable.

#### The fork contract

Every stamp, margin and annex in the source is a measurement of **this** program, and
each becomes false-by-inheritance on the fork's first commit. **The fork operation is
defined per mark, and it is one of exactly two acts.**

**Every mark disposes in one of exactly two acts, and one mark disposes in a third.**

1. **`[MC]` and `[RE]` stamps: re-anchor or strip.** Trace the claim to a refusal in
   *your* machinery (§6.2 Step 0). If you trace it, re-stamp against your own posture
   list's row. If not, re-stamp `[RE]` or `[PLANNED]`, whichever is true of you.
   **Never carry a `C-nn` row number**: it indexes a file you do not have.
2. **`[P1]` stamps: strip.** A performed, dated event of another organization is not a
   fact about yours.
3. **`[CORRECTED]` stamps and the claims they point at: strip, or keep as history and
   say so**, only under a heading saying *this is the exporting program's revision
   history*. **A fork inherits the mechanism, and not the archaeology.**
4. **`[UNANCHORED]` stamps: strip with the exhibit**, unless you can anchor it in
   *your* record.
5. **`[SF]` stamps: re-measure. This is the one mark you must not strip and must not
   keep.** A substrate fact is a claim about a runtime, and yours is the only one that
   decides it. Take the Annex A row the mark names, measure that property in your own
   environment, then either keep the `[SF]` with your own annex row behind it, or drop
   the posture and **mint the compensating control the annex says you now owe**.
   **Stripping it silently is the worst of the three.**
6. **Pointers into the annexes: strip, or re-point at your own ledger.** Do not keep
   them pointing at this program's annexes.
7. **Facsimile blocks: replace with your own, or delete.**
8. **Annex B: delete whole and start your own.** Copy its *shape*: dated conditions
   with owners, owed confirmations, routed-not-performed items, instruments named and
   not in force.
9. **Annex C, the name map: keep it until Step 0's trace is finished, then delete
   whole and write your own — in that order.** Step 0 traces the source's claims into
   the **shell's** machinery, whose tokens are this program's, so the inherited map is
   the lookup table Step 0's greps need.
10. **The golden tally data file: delete whole and write your own, at your first
    campaign.** Copy its shape, and **start it empty rather than forked**: an inherited
    tally is the one artifact that would let your gate read another organization's
    coverage.
11. **The edition anchor and the origin-repository line beside it: replace both.**
12. **The companion volume: do not fork it.** Take the *split rule*, and start your
    companion empty, at your first correction.

**The rule under the table.** **A fork inherits the rules and the reasons, and
inherits no measurement.** Stripping is always safe, and re-anchoring is always work.
A fork that does neither ships a document whose evidence apparatus points at a
repository its readers cannot open.

### 6.2 The order that works

This is a load-ordered checklist, each step keyed to the kit artifact that performs
it. **The order is not a preference.** Each step's failure is unrecoverable once the
following steps have run on top of it.

**Three parties executed this order, and none of them wrote it.** Each was handed
the source document, in an empty repository, and told to run the procedure literally
and to log a halt wherever the text under-determines, contradicts itself, or fails
against the platform. All three halt logs are committed artifacts of this program.

| Run | Executed against | Halts | STOPPED |
|---|---|---|---|
| **one** | the second edition's order | 18 | **3** |
| **two** | the fourth edition's restructured order, acts 1a to 1d | 17 | **0** |
| **three** | the seventh edition's §6.0 and §6.2, **with the shell reachable** | 16 | **2** |

**Read the stop column, and not the halt column.** Run one's stops were
world-reconstruction failures, and it reported the machinery as **roughly one hundred
per cent invention**. Run two had none of those — **every grammar, identifier,
threshold and rule number transferred as a facsimile** — and still wrote some eight
hundred and fifty lines of enforcement logic, because its world could not reach the
shell. Run three cloned the shell and **invented zero lines of enforcement**, at
roughly **ninety-five per cent imported by weight of machinery**, with the remaining
five per cent almost entirely judgement rather than code.

**Three cautions on those figures.** Neither of the first two runs closed the
replication claim, and **run two proceeded by improvisation seventeen times**. **A
falling stop count over a flat halt count is a document that has stopped being
unexecutable and has not yet become determinate.** And **both of run three's stops are by law**, and not by under-determination.
One is a retro-audit row an actor of one *must* leave open. The other is a stale
navigation aid a cadence rule *forbids* repairing in place. **A stop by law and
a stop by under-determination are different objects.**

#### The invariants, which are not the order

**Read these first if you are running somebody else's sequence.** The steps below are
*an* order. **Six of their orderings are not preferences**: their violation is
unrecoverable once later work has run on top of it, and each was learned by watching
the reverse fail.

1. **Declare the founding range before anything reads it.** The commits that install
   the gate cannot pass the gate. Declare that range as committed data, loudly,
   before the re-check first runs over history — not as a flag, and not afterwards as
   an explanation.
2. **Land the machinery before the adversarial review.** Review the charters into a
   gated repository. Reversed, the review's own commits are the ungated history the
   review exists to prevent.
3. **Run continuous integration green at least once before configuring the platform
   protections.** The required-check binding cannot name a check the platform has
   never seen (Annex A.5), so the reverse order sends a sponsor to an empty picker
   with no explanation.
4. **Make the protections bounce a real attempted violation before anything rests on
   them.** An unverified protective setting is a belief, and everything in §2 rests
   on this one.
5. **Author the constitution from your own machinery, before any step quotes it.**
   Author it by reading what your scripts **refuse**, and not by transcribing the
   source document.
6. **Sign the founding gate before the first work order issues.** Not *mostly*
   signed. An organization that starts work with open gate rows has converted its
   gate into a record of intentions.

**Status.** Review-enforced. Each is a precondition somebody checks, and no
instrument reads any of them. **Three of the six dissolve on the with-shell path**
rather than needing satisfaction, because the bootstrap paradox does not arise when
the gate predates the adopter's first commit.

#### The order in ten lines

**A human under adoption pressure will make this list by hand otherwise.** This is the
spine, and it is not a substitute for reading the steps once.

```
−1  Preflight: sponsor recruited · platform protections inspected · CI can run ·
    shell fetched AND pinned to a stated commit.
 0  Trace the source document's [MC] claims into the shell's machinery with the
    inherited name map; record the trace; then delete the map. Open the residue
    file. Re-stamp what you cannot trace.
 1  a roster → b machinery + the committing seat → c every other seat → d the
    constitution, written from what your scripts refuse → e the program-state
    file and the journal index. Acts 1a–1b are the declared founding range.
 2  Adversarial review of 1c's charters and 1d's constitution, into the now-gated
    repository. Every finding disposed of in a committed artifact.
 3  Platform protections, empty bypass list, then a live-fire bounce cited by id.
 4  Self-test green, bound to your own amendment procedure.
 5  The founding gate: retro-audit of the founding range by a seat that did not
    write it, plus the sponsor's ratification. No work order until every row is
    signed.
 6  Start the work, and start the harvest cadence with it.
```

**What the order costs.** Budget two things as steps, and not as accidents. **Most of
a day** for the executable layer, if you have no shell. And **one live-fire failure of
your own machinery**: run two's first push through its own just-committed hook failed
outright. **Repairing enforcement machinery mid-adoption is not a hiccup. It is an
amendment**, and its route is the procedure you author at act 1d and bind at Step 4.
**An order that treats its own first failure as an accident invites the adopter to
bypass the gate they have just installed.**

#### Step −1 — the preflight

Three of this order's steps consume things that are not in the repository, and one of
them is a person. **Confirm all four items before Step 0.**

1. **A human sponsor, recruited and willing.** Step 5 cannot close without them,
   because ratification is the one act §1.7 says an organization cannot perform for
   itself. **Recruit the human first.** Hand them the sponsor guide and §4.7: their
   whole surface is four items, and one is a five-minute platform setting.
2. **A hosting platform whose protections you have actually looked at.** You need
   force-push refusal and deletion refusal on named branches, with an **empty bypass
   list** (Annex A.5). If there is no reachable platform at all, that is a different
   substrate, and Annex A.6b says what drops to convention. Decide that now, and not
   at Step 3.
3. **A continuous-integration surface that can run.** Step 3 depends on it having run
   **once**. If it cannot run, Step 3's required-check binding is unavailable and
   §2.5's adjudicator becomes the committing seat's self-report.
4. **Network reach to the shell, tested — and then a stated commit, and not a
   branch.** Do not assume it. Fetch it. Reachability decides which of the two
   adoption orders governs, and whether most of a day of executable-layer work is on
   your critical path. **Both of the shell-less runs failed this check, and neither
   knew that was a finding.** Then check out the commit §6.0's pin names, or record
   your own observed head with its date. Whichever you take, the founding gate records
   the digest.

**Status.** Review-enforced. It is a checklist item, and no instrument reads it.
**An order whose first step is inside the repository has already assumed everything
outside it.** An unpinned dependency is not a dependency you have. It is a dependency
you will have again tomorrow, differently.

#### Step 0 — verify the reference implementation's claims

Take the source's mechanically-enforced claims and check them in the shell.
**Perform this step in four ordered acts, and the order is the whole repair.**

1. **Keep the inherited name map, and trace with it.** For each `[MC]` stamp find the
   refusal. For each numbered rule find the self-test case that constructs a
   violation of it.
2. **Record the trace as an artifact of your own.** A table beside your copy is the
   acceptable form, and Step 0's definition-of-done row quantifies over it.
3. **Then delete the inherited map, whole.**
4. **Write your own map as Step 1 lands each seat**, which is the only point at which
   you have names to map onto. Writing it is how you discover which of your own names
   disagree with each other.

**Two further acts in this step.** Anything you cannot trace is `[RE]` in your hands,
whatever the source stamps it. **Open your residue list file in this act**, with its
routing columns empty, so the definition-of-done row asking for a file is satisfiable
before the seats exist to fill it.

**Reason for the sequencing.** The trace runs against the **shell's** machinery, whose
tokens are the exporting program's, so the lookup table the greps need is the inherited
name map. An earlier edition ordered its deletion as the first act of Step 0, which
destroyed the map the step then required.

**Reason for the step.** The program that wrote the source had never performed
it, and performing it returned fifteen false claims. **You are inheriting a
corrected document. You are not inheriting the correction of your own copy.**

**Kit consumed:** the self-test, the commit script, the policy module, the posture
list, the inherited name map.

#### Step 1 — roster, machinery, seats, constitution, rehydration aids

**Every commit from act 1a onward is one you intend to keep.** Acts 1a and 1b are the
**founding range**: they land before the gate can bind them. Declare the range. Do
not throw the commits away.

**1a — Derive the roster, and write it down first.** Decide the seats by function
(§1.2). Land the **roster file** in the six-column shape §1.6 prints: Seat, Tier,
Reports to, Charter, Journal, Status. Decide each seat's **write scope** in this act
too, and write it into that seat's charter section 1 as the charters are drafted. The
roster carries no scope column, and **the policy module's scope table is the
charters' scope fields made executable**. **A row here is a plan, and not a seat**:
its Status stays *planned* until 1b or 1c makes it real.

**1b — Land the machinery, and with it the one seat that cannot be onboarded under
it.** Land coupling, append-only with the chain, path isolation, files-list equality,
monotonic identifiers, trailer protection, the large-file gate and the full-history
re-check. The policy module carries **exactly one scope row** at this act, the
committing seat's, because that seat's first commit cannot be gated by a gate it is
installing. In the same act that seat's four things land together: charter, scope
row, launcher text, seeded empty log. **Its roster row flips *planned* to *active* in
that same commit.**

**1c — Onboard every remaining seat, one act per seat, under the gate.** For each,
land four things together: the charter draft, its scope row added to the policy
module, the launcher text, and its seeded empty log. Flip the roster row in the same
commit. Every one of these commits passes the layer 1b installed. **The charters are
drafts here, and Step 2 is what attacks them.**

**1d — Author the constitution, from the machinery rather than from intention.**
Write the rules you just installed as a document a program can amend: each clause
carrying its own posture declaration (§5.5), the scope table restated as its
canonical statement (§2.7), and the numbered rule set printed. **Write it by reading
what your scripts actually refuse.** A constitution written from a description is a
second copy that drifts from the machine on its first amendment.

**1e — Create the two rehydration aids.** Land both in one act: the **program-state
file** in the shape §1.6 prints, and the **journal index**, one row per log with its
last entry identifier and a one-line state. Two rules come with them. The committing
seat updates the program-state file **in the same commit as any state change it
describes**, which is the only cadence that does not decay. The journal index is
**derived**, and it decays exactly as fast as its refresh trigger is rare, so either
derive it mechanically at read time or **write into the file itself the date it was
last true**. **Neither file is optional and neither is cosmetic**: the recovery
procedure of §1.6 opens with the first one.

**Two things this step says out loud, both learned by watching it fail.** **The
machinery comes before the adversarial review**: when an adopter executed the reverse
literally, the review landed in four commits made before the layer existed, and the
full-history re-check then convicted all four. And **the commit that introduces the gate
cannot pass the gate**, because no journal exists yet for it to couple to.

**The founding declaration, in full.** Adoption run two built this missing piece, and
the form below is its invention. **A committed founding declaration** is one file, in
the repository, carrying the range's commit count as data. Four properties bind it.

1. The re-check **reads it from committed state**, and never from a flag, an
   environment variable or an argument the pusher supplies.
2. Every run of the re-check **announces it in a banner**, saying in words that this
   is a declaration and not a quiet exemption.
3. The founding gate's own row **quotes it verbatim**.
4. The Step 5 retro-audit **examines it by name**, and states how many commits it
   found outside the gate and whether that number matches.

**Kit consumed:** the commit script, the history re-check, the policy module, the
workflow, the chain verifier, the charter template, the launcher prompts, the
constitution template.

#### Step 2 — launder the roles through adversarial review

Attack **the charter drafts of act 1c and the constitution of act 1d**, from at least
three lenses: role coherence, enforceability, and readability by the non-specialist
who will sponsor it. Dispose of every finding in a committed artifact. **Run the
review into the repository**, whose machinery is on as of act 1b, so its evidence
survives and is gated.

**What a solo adopter does here.** A solo adopter has one actor, so three independent
reviewers is structurally unachievable, and the review is §1.4(a)'s forbidden
self-review by construction. The right shape is: write all three lens reviews,
**declare the non-independence inside the review artifact itself**, file it as a
critical finding against your own founding, and carry it into the retro-audit. **The
findings are real even when the independence is not. A declared fiction is a debt. An
undeclared one is a lie.**

**Kit consumed:** the charter template, the anonymized auditor charter.

#### Step 3 — configure the out-of-repository dependency and verify it by live fire

**Do this after your continuous integration has run green at least once.** Configure
branch protection on every protected branch. Use an **empty bypass list**, so the
account your own automation pushes with cannot override it. Then attempt a violation
that actually bounces, and cite the bounce by identifier in the gate record. **An
unverified protective setting is a belief.**

**Status.** This program did this, on both branches, on its first day, and had the
setting re-proved in anger later when the platform refused a forced push on a working
reference mid-incident.

**Kit consumed:** the sponsor guide.

#### Step 4 — run the self-test green, and bind it to the amendment procedure

Every amendment that changes enforcement semantics adds a case (§2.7). **A rule whose
test case cannot be written has a predicate nobody has pinned down.** The amendment
procedure this binds to is a clause of **your own constitution, authored at act 1d**.
The kit's template is what you wrote your constitution from, and not what you bind to.

**Three things to know before you start.** This step is unexecutable until your rules
have **numbers**. **One rule will resist the self-test's own form.** The branch limb of `R9` has no
script whose refusal could name it, so no violating construction exists. **Do not
invent an instrument to satisfy the count.** Record that case as discharged by Step 3's
live-fire bounce instead, and say so in the suite. And expect
one construction to be intercepted by a different rule before the rule under test can
fire, which is the suite discovering, correctly, that the rule's real invariant is
narrower than its name.

**Kit consumed:** the self-test. The constitution template was consumed at act 1d.

#### Step 5 — instantiate the gate that closes the founding

1. Retro-audit the whole founding range, by a seat that did not exist during it, with
   its own weakness declared in its text.
2. Carry the sponsor's ratification row.
3. Quote the checklist's conditions **verbatim from the constitution act 1d
   produced** (§3.7).
4. **Do not issue the first real work order until every row is signed.**

**What a solo adopter can and cannot do here.** Two acts **cannot be performed from
inside**: ratification, and the retro-audit's independence, which needs a seat that
did not witness the range. A solo cold boot has neither.

**The honest result, and it is a result rather than a failure.** Found the
organization **self-signed**, leave the ratification row and the independence row
**open** in the gate record, and declare the limitation in the gate file itself. The
gate stays open, and the first real work order does not issue until an external party
closes those rows.

**Two prohibitions.** **Do not self-sign as the sponsor**: that is §5.7's permission
slip wearing a gate's clothes, and §1.7 forecloses it in terms. **Do not stage a
fictional independent seat**: perform the audit in persona if you must, and have it
declare in its own text that its author witnessed everything it audits. **The
findings are then checkable by a stranger even though the independence is fiction,
and the fiction is written down.**

**Kit consumed:** the packet forms, the gate-checklist facsimile at §3.7.

#### Step 6 — start the work

Start the work, and start the harvest cadence with it rather than after it. Decide
the harvest's destination on day one (§3.10), even if that destination is one file in
your own repository.

#### The definition of done for an adoption

State it so that "we adopted it" is a falsifiable claim rather than a feeling.

- [ ] The self-test **runs green from a clean clone**, and carries **at least one case
      per numbered rule**, verified by reading the suite against your rule table, plus
      one case per semantic amendment since. **This is not an equation between case
      count and rule count**: rows are not rules, and cases are not rows. **A
      falsifiability instrument that cannot be evaluated is worse than none, because
      somebody checks it off anyway.**
- [ ] Every `[MC]` claim in **your** copy has been traced to a refusal in **your**
      machinery, and every claim you could not trace has been re-stamped `[RE]` or
      `[PLANNED]` in your copy. Step 0, recorded. **A trace table beside the document
      is a form deviation and an acceptable one. Say which you did.** Expect the trace
      to find defects in **your own** machinery as well as untraceable claims in the
      source.
- [ ] The platform dependency has bounced a real attempted violation, and your
      founding gate record cites the bounce.
- [ ] A seat that did not write the founding range has retro-audited it, the audit
      returned findings, and any critical finding blocked the gate until somebody
      dispositioned and re-verified it. **Alternatively:** the independence is declared
      unachievable in your environment, the audit is performed with its own weakness in
      its text, and this row stays **open**. **An open row is a true row.**
- [ ] Your residue list exists as a file, naming every control you have that is
      review-enforced, with the seat it is routed to, **and a cadence for each**.
- [ ] **You have walked the recovery sequence, from the files rather than from
      memory**: program-state file, constitution, roster, then the log tail of a seat
      with open work. Confirm each exists and points where it says.

**Reason for the last row.** The recovery drill — kill the session and rehydrate on
purpose — is the one founding obligation this program scheduled and **has never
run**. **An untested recovery procedure is a hypothesis, and not a property.**

**The residue list's shape.** One row per control: an identifier, the control in one
line, why no instrument exists, the seat it is routed to, the cadence, and the event
that would make it machine-checked. **Open the file at Step 0 with the routing
columns empty, and fill them at Step 2.** **The cadence column is the one that
matters, and the one an adopter will leave blank.**

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §6.2, for the halt-log paths and the per-halt citations.

### 6.3 The failure of exporting rules alone

**Rule sets extracted from working programs do not transplant.** The rules survive the
move. The reasons do not. What arrives at the new program is a list of obligations of
no evident purpose, which reads as ceremony — and **the parts that look most like
ceremony are, reliably, the parts purchased at the highest price**: the append-only
constraint, the files-list equality check, the sealed prediction, and the
countersignature from the party who would rather not sign.

**So the failure classes are not commentary on the rules. They are the export.** A rule
whose failure class you can state is a rule you can adapt, defend and enforce. **A rule
whose failure class you cannot state is one you will drop, and you will drop it in the
round where it would have mattered.**

**The counterweight, from this program's own history.** The seats that had the reasons
firsthand needed **twenty-three** numbered decision records, a self-test grown to
**fifty-one** cases, and repeated green-locally-and-red-in-the-re-check incidents to get
from those reasons to working mechanisms. **Reasons did not regenerate mechanisms even
for the people who owned them.** Ship both halves, or the half you ship will be the half
that reads as ceremony.

**More:** `docs/PROCESS.md` §6.3.

## Annex A: substrate parameters

Every control in the source rests on facts about the runtime it was built for.
**Measure each of these from your own environment.** Where yours differs, the posture
of the control changes, and this annex says how. **A control whose substrate
assumption is invisible is a control an adopter inherits as a guarantee and operates
as a hope.**

### A.1 — A spawned agent cannot spawn

**Anchor:** a property of the agent runtime this design was built for, and not of any
rule here. **What rests on it:** the sole-spawner monopoly of §1.1 is free, because
nothing needs to enforce it. **If your substrate differs:** the monopoly becomes a
**policy**, its posture drops from substrate fact to review-enforced, and **you then
need a different mechanism for the property it was buying**, which is attribution of
who commissioned what. Nothing else in §1.1 changes, because that section is about
attribution and not about the tree shape.

### A.2 — One working tree, one index

**Anchor:** the version-control tool, and not this design. **What rests on it:** the
commit handoff of §2.1, and both precheck disciplines of §4.1 and §4.2, which exist
because two seats cannot safely be mid-edit in one tree. **If your substrate
differs** — per-seat clones or worktrees — the handoff simplifies and **the precheck
disciplines become more important, and not less**, because a stale base is no longer
visible as a dirty tree.

### A.3 — The reader's single-file limit, and the thresholds anchored to it

**Anchor:** the maximum bytes the agents' file-reading tool returns in one call. In
this environment that is 256 KiB. **Derived:** a journal warns at that figure, which
is 262,144 bytes, and refuses at twice it, which is 524,288 bytes. **The anchor is
the rule *one volume should be one readable unit*, and the numbers are
consequences.** **If your substrate differs:** recompute both thresholds from your own
limit, and restate the anchor in the same act.

**If the anchor's quantity does not exist in your substrate's terms** — a reader whose
limit is stated in lines, or in nothing at all — the instruction above is
unexecutable as written. **The lawful improvisation, which is now the instruction:**
adopt the received figures, declare them unmeasured in your own residue list, and
carry the measurement as an open item in your founding gate. **An adopted figure that
says it is adopted is a parameter. One that says nothing is a number somebody will
defend later as if it had been measured.**

**The live residue, in three parts.** In this program the hard threshold's **refusal**
is on the commit surface. **Since 2026-08-12 the size limb also runs on the
pushed-history surface, as a designed warning and not as a refusal** (§2.2, §2.5, and
§2.6's second `R10` row state the split by limb). The recorded ground for the
asymmetry: history is immutable, so a permanent red is disproportionate to harm
bounded by one file's readability. Both thresholds are read from the
**environment** and either surface can override them per invocation, so the anchored
numbers are defaults rather than constants. And **the source's own law volume is past
this anchor**: its boundary block states its byte figure against the 262,144 above.
**The discipline was applied to journals, which rotate, and to nothing else.** A
parameter a document enforces on its artifacts and not on itself is a parameter with
an exemption nobody voted for.

### A.4 — Read access cannot be denied per path

**Anchor:** the agent runtime. No per-path read denial exists. **What rests on it:**
the blinding separation of §1.4(c) and the seeder allowlist of §3.9 are
**review-enforced**, carried by prompt content, packet content and an audit of
recorded reading, and the source says so rather than claiming coverage. **If your
substrate can deny reads:** those two postures move to machine-checked, the
compensating audit becomes optional rather than load-bearing, and **say so in the
clause**, because a reader of an inherited document will otherwise keep operating
your stronger control as if it were the weaker one.

### A.5 — Branch protection semantics

**Anchor:** the hosting platform. **What it buys, exactly:** refusal of force pushes
and of branch deletion, on named branches, with an **empty bypass list**, so the
privileged account the program itself pushes with cannot override it. **What it does
not buy:** it does not stop a bad commit. It stops a rewrite of history.

**One asymmetry worth copying.** A required status check makes *direct* pushes
impossible on a branch, because a new commit cannot already have a passing check. So
the check requirement binds the trunk, and force-push blocking plus deletion blocking
plus a publicly-failing check guard the working branch.

**If your substrate differs:** §2.5's no-rewrite guarantee drops to **convention**,
and the append-only property of §2.2 becomes unenforced. **Verify it by live fire
(§6.2 Step 3), and not by reading the settings page.**

**Two mechanics of the platform this was built for.** **Use the platform's newer
rulesets rather than its classic branch-protection form**, because on this platform
an empty bypass list makes a ruleset admin-proof by default. And **the required-check
binding is not configurable until the check has run at least once**, because the
picker offers only checks the platform has already seen. That is a sequencing
constraint on the adoption order. **If your platform differs, the constraint may not
exist. Assume it does until you have watched the picker.**

### A.6b — There is no reachable hosting platform at all

**Anchor:** your environment, and not this design. **What rests on it:** everything in
A.5, plus the continuous-integration surface of §2.5 and the whole of §6.2 Step 3.
**If your substrate differs this way:** both fall to **convention**, and say so
rather than substituting.

**A local stand-in** — a bare remote with a hook refusing non-fast-forward pushes and
deletions — reproduces the *bounce*, and is worth building for the live-fire
evidence. **Declare the two material differences:** the empty-bypass-list concept has
no analogue, and any actor with filesystem access can remove the hook. **A guarantee
whose enforcement anyone in the room can delete is convention wearing a refusal's
clothes.** Likewise **a re-check run by hand after each push is not continuous
integration**: it converts the adjudicator into the committing seat's self-report.

### A.6 — The only environment that can run the suite runs on pushed references

**Anchor:** this program's toolchain decision, recorded in its own decision record. A
local run is impossible. **What rests on it:** the campaign model of §3.9 — marked
references, pushed and never merged — rather than the transient apply-and-revert
model, which the constitution originally described. **If your substrate differs** and
you can run the suite locally: **take the transient model. It is strictly better**,
because the never-merged property stops being a measured fact somebody has to keep
measuring.

### A.7 — The large-file threshold

**Anchor:** what your version-control host and your reviewers can carry. In this
program it is **1,000,000 bytes** per staged file, with every journal path carved out.
**Derived:** nothing. Unlike A.3's thresholds this one is a policy choice rather than
a consequence, which is exactly why somebody has to **state** it rather than describe
it. **What rests on it:** `R11` of §2.6, on both surfaces. **If your substrate
differs:** pick your number, and state it in the same act.

**The remedy, which is the half an adopter meets first.** The lawful route for data
exceeding the threshold is **a fetch script plus a checksum manifest**. Commit both.
Do not commit the payload. **State the remedy in the refusal message and in the
document, in the same words**: the message is read at the moment of refusal by a
party under pressure, and the document is read beforehand by the party choosing what
to commit. **A parameter a document calls stated and does not state is worse than an
unstated one, because the reader stops looking.**

### A.8 — Capability tiers of the agents themselves

**Anchor:** the model family available to you, and its price. In this program every
long-lived seat that reviews, adjudicates, grades or audits runs on the stronger
reasoning tier, and every short-lived, packet-scoped worker on the cheaper one.

**Where the assignment binds.** **The launcher definitions bind it**, because that is
the artifact a spawn actually reads. **Four other places restate it**: the roster
file's Tier column, the constitution's own seats paragraph, each charter's identity
section, and §1.6's roster facsimile in the source document.

**What rests on it:** the invariant of §1.2 — **the reviewing tier is at least the
producing tier** — which is what stops a review from being a formality that cannot see
the defect it was convened for. **If your substrate differs:** **the invariant is the
part that transfers, and not the tier names.**

**Two transferable halves from the repair of this parameter.** The repair is to **name
which site binds and which sites restate**, because a parameter with five statement
sites and no designated canonical one will diverge. And **the sites that restate a
binding parameter are exactly the sites that go stale**, so generate them from the
binding one if your runtime lets you.

### A.9 — Two parameters this program does not have

**Anchor:** neither is a substrate fact. Both are policy choices this program's own
text called *stated* or *literal* while stating nothing. They sit here in the annex's
idiom, because writing a number into a running line to close a finding would be the
source inventing a practice and then describing it.

**A.9(a) — The draft-identifier placeholder token.** *What rests on it:* §3's
greppability property, that a search can find an unallocated identifier before the
sole committer allocates the real one. *What this program has:* **no token.** Its
practice is the next free number per prefix, measured at a stated commit and declared
provisional in the drafting seat's own entry — disciplined, and lexically
indistinguishable from an allocated identifier, **so the greppability the property
claims does not exist here.** *Choose yours*, and the requirement is the transferable
part: a **fixed literal**, **impossible as an allocated identifier**, carried **in
the filename** and not only in prose.

**A.9(b) — The E6 escalation threshold.** *What rests on it:* the sixth escalation
class of §4.6, the only class with a quantity in it. *What this program has:* one
figure, in its constitution, introduced by *for example* — a phase tracking past twice
its estimate — and **zero firings**, so **the figure has never been tested against an
actual overrun.** *Choose yours*, from your own cost structure, and write it down
**before your first anomaly**: a threshold set while looking at the overrun is set by
the party the escalation would embarrass.

## The fidelity sweep

**What this volume claims.** Every rule in `docs/PROCESS.md` §§1–6 and Annex A is
findable here, either as a rendered rule or as an explicit **More** pointer at its own
section.

**How to check the claim.** Every numbered subsection of the source has a heading here
with the same number. Read the two side by side, section by section. A heading present
here with only a **More** line is a deliberate pointer, and not an omission.

**What this volume deliberately does not render.** These are pointers, and the source
is the only place they exist.

1. The anonymized patterns and the exhibits, except where a section renders the ruling
   an exhibit produced.
2. The margins and the revision history, which live in `docs/PROCESS-MEMOIR.md`.
3. The facsimile blocks, including the worked tally of §3.9 and the golden data file
   it is derived from.
4. The subsection index, the seam counts and the boundary block of *Read this first*,
   which are measurements of the source's own coverage.
5. Annex B and Annex C, which live in `docs/PROCESS-MEMOIR.md` and are made entirely
   of program nouns.

**What a reader must not conclude from this volume.** This volume states no posture
stamp and cites no posture-list row. **A Status line here is a rendition of the
source's calibration, and not an independent measurement.** Nobody re-measured any
claim in the act of writing this volume.

**One exception, at this revision, and it is stated so the sentence above stays
true.** Repairing the cold probe's findings required one measurement over this
program's own record: the seal census of §3.3, re-derived seal by seal by comparing
each seal file's current bytes against its bytes at the commit that introduced it,
and each campaign's scored state against its committed verdict. **That measurement
moved the source**, and this volume carries the moved figures. Every other Status line
here remains a rendition of the source's calibration and nothing more.
